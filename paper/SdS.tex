\begin{abstract}
Conformal compactification of asymptotically flat evolution equations can
introduce formally singular terms at future null infinity.  Misner proposed
regulating those terms with a small positive cosmological constant, replacing
null infinity by a cosmological horizon where the conformal factor remains
nonzero.  We give a quantitative black-hole proof of principle using scalar
waves on Schwarzschild--de Sitter spacetime.  A useful regulator is not
specified by the metric alone: its foliation, compactification, initial-data
map, and waveform clock must approach a common Schwarzschild problem as the
cosmological length $L=\sqrt{3/\Lambda}$ grows.  We identify a bridge
foliation with this global limit and test the resulting family against an
independent Schwarzschild evolution.  Direct finite-$L$ waveform errors
decrease but remain observable dependent.  Two nested extrapolants formed
from $L/M=80,160,320,640$ recover the independent Schwarzschild waveform at
a conservative one-percent level for the cumulative, prompt-dominated
fixed-mode test and for a localized multimode response on its common
archived interval.  The latter provides a nontrivial angular and caustic
stress test.  We make no claim of uniform one-percent late-time accuracy.
Caustic-echo timing supplies supporting Schwarzschild--de Sitter physics and
is consistent with a local $\mathcal O((M/L)^2)$ correction and a larger
outer $\mathcal O(M/L)$ response, but its deterministic sensitivities do not
support precision fit coefficients.  The calculation establishes a scalar
regulator protocol and target accuracy for a future application to the full
Einstein equations; it is not itself an Einstein evolution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Radiation from isolated systems has a  clean definition at future null
infinity, $\scri^+$.  Hyperboloidal evolution brings this clean definition
onto the computational domain through spacelike slices that approach
outgoing null hypersurfaces.  After spatial compactification places infinity
at a finite coordinate location, waves leave the grid through a
characteristic, pure outflow boundary.  Friedrich's conformal field equations regularize
the asymptotic problem \cite{friedrich1983cauchy}.  Applying scri fixing
directly to the Einstein equations exposes formally singular conformal
source terms whose limits must preserve the constraints in the chosen gauge
\cite{zenginoglu_hyperboloidal_2008,Zenginoglu:2008pw,
Moncrief:2008ie}.  Stable calculations now include Einstein evolution in
axisymmetry and spherical symmetry
\cite{Rinne:2009qx,Vano-Vinuales:2014koa,Vano-Vinuales:2017qij}.
Three-dimensional test fields and asymptotic model systems extend those
tests \cite{Zenginoglu:2010zm,Peterson:2023bha}.

Misner introduced artificial cosmology
\cite{misner_hyperboloidal_2006,misner_over_2006,misner_excising_2006}.
A small positive cosmological constant serves there as a regulator.
It replaces future null infinity with a distant cosmological horizon, so the
grid terminates in the pure-outflow region beyond it.  Black-hole excision
uses the same causal mechanism inside an event horizon.  Misner and
collaborators tested parts of this idea with Maxwell fields on de Sitter
backgrounds.  Scalar waves on Schwarzschild--de Sitter (SdS) provide a
black-hole model with a strong-field scattering region between the future
black-hole horizon $\hb$ and the future cosmological horizon $\hc$.

For $\Lambda>0$, the future conformal boundary is spacelike.  Its asymptotic
symmetries and radiative degrees of freedom differ from those at
$\scri^+$, as do the associated fluxes.  Ashtekar et al. developed this
structure for linear fields and derived a
positive-$\Lambda$ quadrupole formula
\cite{Ashtekar:2014zfa,Ashtekar:2015lla,Ashtekar:2015lxa}.  Here $\Lambda$
acts as a numerical regulator for scalar evolution.  We determine the
region over which that evolution approximates a specified asymptotically
flat solution, with the conformal boundary confining the comparison to a
finite spacetime region.

Regularity at $\hb$ and $\hc$ fixes the leading behavior of a bridge near
each horizon.  Its continuation into the expanding region beyond $\hc$
selects the asymptotic end.  Horizon crossings leave enough freedom for a
one-parameter family to approach a Cauchy-like Schwarzschild end.  The
regulator family includes its foliation and compactification along with the
data map and waveform clock.  Scalar observables remain invariant under
coordinate changes.  Our analysis tracks the limiting coordinates and the
conditioning they induce in the numerical equations.

Writing $\Lambda=3/L^2$, the SdS metric approaches Schwarzschild on every
fixed near-zone interval as $L/M\to\infty$.  Each finite value of $L$ keeps
the SdS global causal structure.  Schwarzschild fields develop inverse-power
tails
\cite{price1972nonspherical,zengin2008hyperboloidal,
Angelopoulos:2016wcv}.  SdS fields eventually display cosmological decay
\cite{Brady:1996za,brady_radiative_1999,Molina:2003dc}.  A finite-$L$
calculation approximates the asymptotically flat waveform over a finite
interval.  Cosmological decay sets its later behavior.

Using the bridge constructions and conformal geometry developed in
Ref.~\cite{zenginoglu_bridge_2025}, we form a one-parameter regulator family
on a fixed compact domain.  Analytic large-$L$ limits follow the slices to
their asymptotic ends.  Retarded-time offsets set a common clock, and the
compactified coefficients expose the conditioning of each choice.  We then
evolve a regular first-order scalar system in the minimal gauge and compare
each finite-$L$ waveform with an independently evolved Schwarzschild
reference without fitting a relative time translation.

The paper has two goals.  The primary goal is to test Misner's proposal as a
controlled regulator: we determine both the direct finite-$L$ error and the
accuracy of an $L\to\infty$ extrapolation.  A fixed pure-$\ell=2$ datum tests
the waveform limit cleanly, while a localized source excites many angular
modes and stresses the construction through caustics.  The secondary goal is
to study physical finite-$\Lambda$ effects, including caustic echoes and the
eventual departure from Schwarzschild late-time behavior.  Those effects
support the regulator analysis but do not constitute a second headline.

Section~\ref{sec:bridges} gives the global foliation criterion and identifies
the regulator gauge.  Section~\ref{sec:scalar} defines the scalar model,
matched data, clock, numerical methods, and comparison measures.
Section~\ref{sec:results} presents direct and extrapolated Schwarzschild
recovery followed by the localized-source test and supporting SdS physics.
The appendices collect convergence, sensitivity, and reproducibility details.

\section{Bridge foliations and the regulator limit}
\label{sec:bridges}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Schwarzschild--de Sitter static patch}

The SdS metric in static coordinates is
\begin{align}
 ds^2&=-f(r)dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2d\omega^2,
 \label{eq:sds_metric}\\
 f(r)&=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{r^2}{L^2},
 \qquad d\omega^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2.
 \label{eq:f}
\end{align}
We use geometrized units $G_{\rm N}=c=1$.  The simulations set $M=1$ and
restore factors of $M$ in reported coordinates and dimensionless
diagnostics.
For $0<M<L/(3\sqrt{3})$, $f$ has a black-hole root $r_b$, a
cosmological root $r_c$, and a negative root $r_u=-(r_b+r_c)$.  They obey
\begin{align}
 L^2&=r_b^2+r_br_c+r_c^2,
 &2ML^2&=r_br_c(r_b+r_c),
 \label{eq:root_relations}\\
 f(r)&=\frac{(r-r_b)(r_c-r)(r-r_u)}{L^2r}.
 \label{eq:f_factorized}
\end{align}
For $i=b,c$, the positive surface gravities follow from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kappa}.  The partial-fraction formulas use the positive
algebraic coefficient $\kappa_u:=|f'(r_u)|/2$ at the negative root:
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_i=\frac12\left|f'(r_i)\right|
 =\frac12\left|\frac{1}{r_i}-\frac{3r_i}{L^2}\right|,
 \qquad i\in\{b,c,u\}.
 \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
With an arbitrary reference radius $r_0\in(r_b,r_c)$, the normalized
tortoise coordinate is
\begin{align}
r_{*,L}(r)={}&\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}
 \ln\frac{r-r_b}{r_0-r_b}
-\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}
 \ln\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_0}
\notag\\
&+\frac{1}{2\kappa_u}
 \ln\frac{r-r_u}{r_0-r_u}.
\label{eq:tortoise}
\end{align}
The tortoise coordinate tends to $-\infty$ at $r_b$ and to $+\infty$ at
$r_c$.  Its flat limit has
\begin{equation}
 r_b\to2M,\qquad r_c=L-M+\mathcal{O}(M^2/L),
 \label{eq:horizon_flat_limit}
\end{equation}
and the static patch expands to the Schwarzschild exterior.

\subsection{The global criterion beyond the horizons}

We introduce a stationary height function,
\begin{equation}
 \tau=t+h(r),\qquad B(r):=f(r)h'(r),
 \label{eq:height}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the boost relative to $r_*$.  For a radial coordinate
$\rho=g(r)$ and $G=d\rho/dr$, the metric becomes
\begin{equation}
 ds^2=-f\,d\tau^2+\frac{2B}{G}\,d\tau d\rho
 +\frac{1-B^2}{fG^2}\,d\rho^2+r^2d\omega^2.
 \label{eq:transformed_metric}
\end{equation}
A future-directed spacelike bridge within the static patch satisfies
\begin{equation}
 |B|<1,\qquad B(r_b)=+1,\qquad B(r_c)=-1,
\label{eq:boost_conditions}
\end{equation}
where $(1-B^2)/f$ must tend to a finite positive nonzero value at each
horizon for a radial map with finite nonzero endpoint values of $G$.
Equivalently, $h\sim+r_*$ at $\hb$ and $h\sim-r_*$ at $\hc$.  The leaves
are null-transverse and the transformed coefficients have regular horizon
limits \cite{zenginoglu_bridge_2025}.  Beyond $\hc$, where $f<0$, spacelike
continuation on the future branch requires $B<-1$.  The value $B(r_c)=-1$
supplies the local crossing data.  The behavior of $B$ beyond that point
determines how the leaves approach the spacelike future boundary.  This
expanding region supports forward and scattering problems
\cite{Schlue:2012pq,Bernhardt:2024hfs}.

Two heights normalized by $h(r_0)=0$ carry the same endpoint data:
\begin{align}
h_{\rm min}(r)={}&
\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}\ln\left|\frac{r-r_b}{r_0-r_b}\right|
+\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}\ln\left|\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_0}\right|,
\label{eq:minimum_height}\\
h_{\rm mg}(r)={}&
\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}
\ln\left|\frac{(r-r_b)r_0}{(r_0-r_b)r}\right|
+\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}
\ln\left|\frac{(r_c-r)r_0}{(r_c-r_0)r}\right|
\notag\\
&-\frac{1}{2\kappa_u}
\ln\left|\frac{(r-r_u)r_0}{(r_0-r_u)r}\right|.
\label{eq:minimal_height}
\end{align}
Local horizon regularity supplies the two logarithms in the minimum height.
The minimal gauge adds the third-root term and radial factors
\cite{PanossoMacedo:2023qzp,zenginoglu_bridge_2025}.  Equation
\eqref{eq:boost_conditions} holds for both families.  Figure
\ref{fig:bridge_foliations} follows their leaves beyond $\hc$.  As
$L\to\infty$, the minimum-height family closes toward a Cauchy-like
asymptotic end.  Minimal-gauge leaves open toward Schwarzschild $\scri^+$,
as seen in the two conformal diagrams.

For a bridge normalized by $h(r_0)=0$ and $r_{*,L}(r_0)=0$, the boost gives
the clock diagnostic
\begin{equation}
 q_B(L;r_0):=\lim_{r\to r_c}(h+r_{*,L})
 =\int_{r_0}^{r_c}\frac{1+B}{f}\,dr.
 \label{eq:general_q}
\end{equation}
The integrand is regular at $r_c$.  A divergent $q_B$ sends the origin of
the source clock to infinity as the outer null surface recedes.  A finite
$q_B$ supplies the required clock limit.

\subsection{Six bridges and a fixed compactification}

In addition to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:minimum_height} and
\eqref{eq:minimal_height}, we consider four boosts specified directly:
\begin{align}
B_{\rm lin}&=-1+2\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_b},
\label{eq:linear_boost}\\
B_{\rm fl}&=-1+2\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_b}\frac{r_b^2}{r^2},
\label{eq:flat_linear_boost}\\
B_{\rm Mav}&=-\frac{1-3M/r}{\sqrt{1-9M^2\Lambda}}
 \sqrt{1+\frac{6M}{r}},
\label{eq:mav_boost}\\
B_{\rm sr}&=-\gamma r+\frac{\beta}{r^2},
\label{eq:slow_roll_boost}\\
\gamma&=\frac{r_c^2+r_b^2}{r_c^3-r_b^3},\qquad
\beta=\frac{r_c^2r_b^2(r_c+r_b)}{r_c^3-r_b^3}.
\label{eq:slow_roll_parameters}
\end{align}
The Mavrogiannis boost is motivated by wave estimates on SdS and turns at the
photon sphere $r=3M$
\cite{mavrogiannis2024quasilinear,mavrogiannis2023morawetz}.

At fixed areal radius, with $x=M/r$, the six boosts have the limits
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{lcc}
\text{bridge}&B_0(r)&B_0(\infty)\\
\hline
\text{minimum height}&3x-\tfrac12&-\tfrac12\\
\text{minimal gauge}&-1+8x^2&-1\\
\text{linear}&+1&+1\\
\text{modified linear}&-1+8x^2&-1\\
\text{Mavrogiannis}&-(1-3x)\sqrt{1+6x}&-1\\
\text{slow roll}&4x^2&0
\end{array}
\label{eq:boost_limits}
\end{equation}
An outgoing Schwarzschild hyperboloidal slice necessarily has $B_0\to-1$ at
$r\to\infty$.  With the compactification \eqref{eq:compactification}, a
regular null-infinity limit further requires
$1+B_0=\mathcal{O}(M^2/r^2)$ with a finite positive leading coefficient.
The minimum-height, linear, and slow-roll limits fall outside this
asymptotic-nullness condition.  Minimal, modified-linear, and Mavrogiannis
slices satisfy it.  A full regulator limit also requires uniform
compactified coefficients through the expanding region.  For the
modified-linear boost, an outer layer breaks uniformity near $\rho=1$.

We place both horizons at fixed coordinate locations using
\begin{align}
 \rho&=\frac{1-r_b/r}{1-r_b/r_c},
 &r(\rho)&=\frac{r_b}{1-(1-r_b/r_c)\rho},
 \label{eq:compactification}\\
 G(r)&=\frac{d\rho}{dr}
 =\frac{r_b}{(1-r_b/r_c)r^2}.
 \label{eq:G}
\end{align}
The black-hole and cosmological horizons are at $\rho=0$ and $1$,
respectively.  At fixed $r$,
\begin{equation}
 \rho\to\rho_0:=1-\frac{2M}{r},
 \label{eq:rho_limit}
\end{equation}
which is also the compactification used by the independent Schwarzschild
solver.

\section{Scalar evolution and comparison protocol}
\label{sec:scalar}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Regular first-order system}

For a minimally coupled massless scalar field, write
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(t,r,\theta,\varphi)=\frac{1}{r}
 \sum_{\ell m}u_{\ell m}(t,r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi).
 \label{eq:scalar_decomposition}
\end{equation}
Each mode satisfies
\begin{align}
 \left(-\partial_t^2+\partial_{r_*}^2-V_\ell\right)u&=0,
 \label{eq:wave_equation}\\
 V_\ell&=f\left[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}
 +\frac{f'(r)}{r}\right].
 \label{eq:scalar_potential}
\end{align}
We suppress the mode labels below and introduce
\begin{equation}
 p:=\frac{d\rho}{dr_*}=fG,\qquad
 A:=\frac{p}{1-B^2},\qquad P:=\frac{V_\ell}{p},
 \label{eq:coefficients}
\end{equation}
The first-order fields are
\begin{equation}
 \psi:=\partial_\rho u,\qquad
 \pi:=\frac{1-B^2}{p}\partial_\tau u-B\psi.
 \label{eq:first_order_variables}
\end{equation}
The evolution system is
\begin{align}
 \partial_\tau u&=A(B\psi+\pi),
 \label{eq:evolve_u}\\
 \partial_\tau\psi&=\partial_\rho\!\left[A(B\psi+\pi)\right],
 \label{eq:evolve_psi}\\
 \partial_\tau\pi&=\partial_\rho\!\left[A(\psi+B\pi)\right]-Pu.
 \label{eq:evolve_pi}
\end{align}
We monitor the reduction constraint
\begin{equation}
 C:=\psi-\partial_\rho u.
 \label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}

The radial characteristic speeds are
\begin{align}
 c_-&=-A(1+B)=-\frac{p}{1-B},
\notag\\
 c_+&= A(1-B)= \frac{p}{1+B}.
\label{eq:characteristic_speeds}
\end{align}
The endpoint speeds are $(c_+,c_-)=(0,c_-<0)$ at $\rho=0$ and
$(c_+,c_-)=(c_+>0,0)$ at $\rho=1$.  The characteristic flow determines all
endpoint values.  For a
simple crossing with $B'(r_h)\ne0$, analytic cancellation gives
\begin{equation}
 A_h=\frac{f'(r_h)G(r_h)}{-2s_hB'(r_h)},\qquad
 s_b=+1,\quad s_c=-1.
 \label{eq:A_endpoint}
\end{equation}
We cancel every height-function pole algebraically and assign
Eq.~\eqref{eq:A_endpoint} directly at each endpoint.

The evolution domain ends on $\hc$.  The analytic continuation of the height
function beyond that horizon is nevertheless essential: it selects the
asymptotic end approached by the family as $L/M\to\infty$.  The finite-$L$
evolutions test the regular crossing coefficients and extract the outer
waveform on the cosmological horizon.

For the minimal gauge, the limiting Schwarzschild coefficients are
\begin{align}
 B_0&=-1+2(1-\rho_0)^2,
 &A_0&=\frac{1}{8M(2-\rho_0)},
 \label{eq:schwarz_coefficients}\\
 P_0&=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)+(1-\rho_0)}{2M}.
 \label{eq:schwarz_potential}
\end{align}
The Schwarzschild code implements these expressions independently of the
finite-$L$ code.

\subsection{Numerical method}

We solve Eqs.~\eqref{eq:evolve_u}--\eqref{eq:evolve_pi} with Dedalus~3
\cite{burns_dedalus_2020}.  The radial direction uses a Chebyshev-$T$
collocation basis on $0\leq\rho\leq1$, with the second-order RK222 method
advancing time in double precision.  Fields at finite-radius
observers are evaluated directly by spectral interpolation.

\subsection{Geometrically matched data and clocks}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Fixed-window data.}
For the finite-time flat limit, the reduced field is the same smooth compact
profile in areal radius on every background:
\begin{equation}
x=\frac{r-4M}{1.5M},
\label{eq:displacement_x}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
u(0,r)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\exp\left(1-\frac{1}{1-x^2}\right),& |x|<1,\\
0,&|x|\geq1.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:displacement_data}
\end{equation}
The support is $2.5M<r<5.5M$.  We initialize
$\psi=D_\rho u$, where $D_\rho$ is the Chebyshev differentiation operator in
the finite spectral representation, and
\begin{equation}
 \pi=-B\psi,
\label{eq:killing_stationary_data}
\end{equation}
so that $\partial_\tau u=\partial_tu=0$ initially.  The data are momentarily
stationary with respect to the static Killing field and retain a nonzero
normal derivative on the bridge.  Their support in areal radius stays fixed
across $L$.

\paragraph{Tail data.}
For the late-time study,
\begin{equation}
 u=\psi=0,\qquad \partial_\tau u=G_{\rm v}(r),\qquad
 \pi=\frac{G_{\rm v}(r)}{A},
\label{eq:velocity_data}
\end{equation}
where $x_{\rm v}=(r-6M)/(3M)$ and the peak-normalized $C^\infty$ profile is
\begin{equation}
 G_{\rm v}(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\exp\left(1-\frac{1}{1-x_{\rm v}^2}\right),
   & |x_{\rm v}|<1,\\
 0,& |x_{\rm v}|\geq1.
 \end{cases}
\label{eq:velocity_profile}
\end{equation}
Its support is $3M<r<9M$.  Dividing by the background-dependent $A$ fixes
the prescribed Killing-time derivative profile across backgrounds.  The
momentum array changes with $A$.  Since the $\tau=0$ bridge hypersurfaces
vary with $L$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:displacement_data}--\eqref{eq:velocity_profile}
define a geometric map among their data.

\paragraph{Retarded time.}
We set $r_0=4M$ and normalize
\begin{equation}
 h_L(r_0)=0,\qquad r_{*,L}(r_0)=0.
\end{equation}
For the minimal gauge, the cosmological-horizon logarithms in
$h_L+r_{*,L}$ cancel exactly, giving
\begin{align}
q_L={}&\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}
\ln\frac{(r_c-r_b)^2r_0}{r_c(r_0-r_b)^2}
\notag\\
&+\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa_u}-\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}\right)
\ln\frac{r_c}{r_0}.
\label{eq:q_L}
\end{align}
The independent Schwarzschild value is
\begin{equation}
 q_0=4M\ln\frac{r_0}{r_0-2M}=4M\ln2.
 \label{eq:q_zero}
\end{equation}
Waveforms are compared at the geometric retarded time
\begin{equation}
 U=\tau-q_L.
\label{eq:retarded_time}
\end{equation}
At $\hc$ this is the outgoing retarded-time coordinate.  The same
normalization reaches Schwarzschild $\scri^+$ in the limit.  At finite
radius, $U$ is a stationary clock normalized at the outer boundary.  Its
relation to the local outgoing coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 U-(t-r_{*,L})=h_L(r)+r_{*,L}(r)-q_L.
\label{eq:finite_radius_clock}
\end{equation}
Since the constant drops out of a decay rate, reported transition times keep
the geometric origin in Eq.~\eqref{eq:retarded_time}.

\subsection{Localized source and regulator observables}
\label{sec:localized_protocol}

The fixed-mode calculation isolates the radial and temporal regulator limit.
A second family uses a normalized source localized in radius, time, and
angle.  Its spherical-harmonic decomposition excites a multimode response,
including propagation through caustics.  The physical source prescription is
held fixed across the SdS and Schwarzschild backgrounds; source width is
therefore a property of the chosen response, not a numerical error bar.

For a common time interval $\mathcal J$, the direct fixed-mode waveform error

\begin{equation}
 E_2(L;\mathcal J)=

the sphere-integrated Parseval norm
\begin{equation}
 \|W\|_{\mathcal J,S^2}^2=
 \int_{\mathcal J}dU\sum_{\ell m}|u_{\ell m}(U)|^2.
 \label{eq:parseval_norm}
\end{equation}
Assuming the finite-$L$ observable admits the tested expansion
\begin{equation}
 W_L=W_0+c_1\frac{M}{L}+c_2\left(\frac{M}{L}\right)^2
 +\mathcal O\!\left((M/L)^3\right),
 \label{eq:regulator_expansion}
\end{equation}
the three-level combination
\begin{equation}
 W_\infty^{(L)}=
 \frac{W_L-6W_{2L}+8W_{4L}}{3}
 \label{eq:nested_extrapolant}
\end{equation}
cancels the displayed finite-$L$ terms.  We test the extrapolation rather
than assume it by comparing the nested triples based at $L/M=80$ and $160$
with one another and with $W_0$.

 \ne

endpoint has $Mc_{+,\rm fl}(1)\to1/2$.  Taking the Schwarzschild limit in the

finite clock in Eq.~\eqref{eq:q_zero}.  Its coefficients stay bounded across
the compact domain.  An independent closed-form Schwarzschild
implementation supplies the reference.

\subsection{Direct finite-\texorpdfstring{$L$}{L} recovery}
\label{sec:direct_recovery}

The direct comparison answers the practical regulator question: how large
must $L/M$ be before a finite cosmological calculation approximates the
chosen Schwarzschild observable?  The answer depends on the observable and
accuracy threshold.  For the cumulative, prompt-dominated pure-$\ell=2$
sequence, agreement including the case-specific numerical margin first
reaches $5\%$ at $L/M=320$ and $2\%$ at $L/M=640$; direct $1\%$ agreement is
not reached through $L/M=640$.  These thresholds do not apply to the
localized-source response.

\subsection{Nested recovery of the Schwarzschild waveform}

The primary regulator test is the agreement among

state the result conservatively as recovery of the tested Schwarzschild

\subsection{Supporting Schwarzschild--de Sitter physics}
\label{sec:sds_physics}

Finite $L$ is not merely a numerical nuisance.  The localized response
contains caustic echoes whose arrival times, amplitudes, and phase structure
probe the global SdS geometry.  A first-echo-spacing observable $D_1$ is
consistent with a local correction of order $(M/L)^2$ at fixed areal radius
and a parametrically larger outer response approaching order $M/L$.  The
combined deterministic timing sensitivities do not meet the requested
precision targets, however, so these curves are consistency evidence rather
than precision measurements of expansion coefficients.  Local $L/M=640$
timings and all phase pairs that fail the null-ray arrival check remain
diagnostic and are excluded from quantitative interpretation.

The older late-time decay calculation provides complementary evidence for
the finite interval over which SdS follows Schwarzschild before cosmological
decay dominates.  Its detailed estimator study should not obscure the
regulator result and may be condensed or moved to an appendix.

\subsection{Scope of the demonstration}
\label{sec:scope}

The calculation tests the complete regulator protocol for a linear scalar
field on fixed black-hole backgrounds.  It does not evolve the full Einstein
equations and does not demonstrate regularization of their conformal source
terms.  Its contribution is a quantitative target and validation strategy:
choose a globally compatible regulator family, keep the physical comparison
contract fixed, measure direct finite-$L$ errors, and verify nested
extrapolation against an independent asymptotically flat reference.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

An artificial-cosmology regulator is a coherent limiting construction, not
only a choice of positive cosmological constant.  The metric, foliation,
compactification, initial-data map, and waveform clock must all select the
same asymptotically flat problem.  Local regularity at the cosmological
horizon does not ensure this limit: the continuation of the slices beyond
that horizon determines which Schwarzschild end is recovered.  The minimal
gauge used here has the required global end, a finite limiting clock, and
well-conditioned compactified evolution coefficients.

For the tested scalar observables, direct SdS waveforms approach their
Schwarzschild counterparts as $L/M$ grows, but a single finite-$L$ run is not
uniformly an effectively Schwarzschild calculation.  The two nested
extrapolants provide the more useful regulator protocol.  Their agreement
with one another and with an independent Schwarzschild calculation supports
controlled recovery at a conservative $1\%$ level for the cumulative
prompt-dominated fixed-mode test and for the localized multimode response on
its common archived interval.  No corresponding claim is made for the
unresolved disjoint late-time windows.

The localized response also exposes genuine finite-$\Lambda$ physics.
Caustic timing is consistent with a near-zone correction of order
$(M/L)^2$ and a slower outer response approaching order $M/L$, although
deterministic extraction sensitivities preclude precision coefficient
claims.  Together these calculations establish a reproducible scalar
black-hole proof of principle for Misner's proposal.  Applying the protocol
to the full Einstein equations remains the central future test, where the
formally singular conformal source terms and the coupled gauge and constraint
systems must be treated directly.
