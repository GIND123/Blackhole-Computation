\begin{abstract}
Misner proposed using a small positive cosmological constant to regulate the
formally singular terms introduced by conformal compactification at future
null infinity.  The proposal raises a practical question: how can a spacetime
with a cosmological horizon approximate radiation from an isolated system?
Scalar waves on Schwarzschild--de Sitter backgrounds provide a setting in
which to examine the geometric choices behind this approximation,
particularly the foliation and the identification of the asymptotically flat
limit.  Confining the cosmological modification to an exterior region opens a
further possibility: preserving the black-hole near zone while regulating the
asymptotic domain.  We investigate the promise and limitations of artificial
cosmology from this perspective.
\end{abstract}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Radiation from isolated systems has a clean definition at future null
infinity, $\scri^+$.  Hyperboloidal evolution brings this boundary onto the
computational domain with spacelike slices that become asymptotically null;
compactification then places infinity at a finite outflow boundary.  The
remaining challenge is a regular, well-conditioned evolution of the nonlinear
Einstein equations.  Misner proposed regulating their formally singular
conformal terms with a small positive cosmological constant
\cite{misner_hyperboloidal_2006,misner_over_2006,misner_excising_2006}.
At finite cosmological length $L$, a cosmological horizon replaces the
$\Omega=0$ endpoint by one with $\Omega_L>0$, at the price of a physical
bias.  A useful finite-$L$ family must approach one asymptotically flat
problem in its metric, foliation, compactification, data, and waveform clock;
regular horizon crossing alone is insufficient.

Several complementary programs reach or include the asymptotic boundary.
Characteristic evolution and Cauchy--characteristic matching now extend to
nonlinear binary-black-hole mergers and tails
\cite{Winicour:2012characteristic,MaEtAl:2025CCM}.  Friedrich's conformal
field equations enlarge the regular variable set \cite{friedrich1983cauchy};
early global calculations exposed constraint, gauge, and moving-boundary
resolution difficulties
\cite{Frauendiener:1998II,Hubner:1999scheme,Hubner:2001timelike,
FrauendienerHein:2002,Husa:2002problems}.  Generalized conformal field
equations based on a Weyl connection and conformal geodesics have enabled
nonlinear black-hole scattering, although their current initial-boundary
framework does not fix $\scri^+$ spatially
\cite{Friedrich:1995general,Friedrich:2003geodesics,
BeyerEtAl:2017GCFE,FrauendienerStevens:2021massloss,
FrauendienerStevensThwala:2025,CamdenEtAl:2025,
FrauendienerStevens:2023compactification}.

Scri fixing instead anchors $\scri^+$ and, on fixed black-hole backgrounds,
removes artificial outer data and finite-radius waveform extrapolation
\cite{zenginoglu_hyperboloidal_2008,Zenginoglu:2008pw}.  Time-domain
Schwarzschild and Kerr calculations demonstrated accurate long evolutions,
including power-law tails
\cite{zengin2008hyperboloidal,ZenginogluNunezHusa:2009,
ZenginogluTiglio:2009,RaczToth:2011,Bernuzzi:2011aj,
ZenginogluKhanna:2011,Harms:2014dqa}; frequency-domain work later exploited
the same regularity for spectral and self-force calculations
\cite{AnsorgMacedo:2016,Macedo:2020,MacedoEtAl:2022}.  This record motivates
our use of a finite-$L$ family that approaches a scri-fixed Schwarzschild
gauge.

Model-field evolutions test such infrastructure directly
\cite{ZenginogluKidder:2010,Peterson:2023bha,Rinne:2025nonlinear,
ZenginogluBernuzziNutzi:2026}.  Gravitational approaches include
prescribed-conformal-factor formulations
\cite{zenginoglu_hyperboloidal_2008,Zenginoglu:2008pw}, nonlinear conformal
BSSN/Z4 systems in spherical symmetry
\cite{Vano-Vinuales:2014koa,Vano-Vinuales:2017qij,
VanoVinuales:2024reference,Alvares:2025emkg}, and constrained CMC schemes that
have reached axisymmetry
\cite{Moncrief:2008ie,Rinne:2009qx,RinneMoncrief:2013,
BuchmanPfeifferBardeen:2009,BardeenSarbachBuchman:2011}.  Dual-foliation
generalized harmonic evolution has advanced from model problems to spherical
Einstein evolution
\cite{Hilditch:2015aba,HilditchEtAl:2018,DuarteEtAl:2023,
Peterson:2024spherical,Peterson:2025strong}, while N\"utzi's
symmetric-hyperbolic system fixes null infinity for nonlinear small data
\cite{Nutzi:2023,Nutzi:2025,Nutzi:2026}.  A robust free-evolution code that
fixes $\scri^+$ for generic three-dimensional binary-black-hole spacetimes
remains open.

Scalar waves on Schwarzschild--de Sitter (SdS) provide a black-hole test
between $\hb$ and $\hc$.  For $\Lambda>0$, the future
conformal boundary is spacelike, with different asymptotic symmetries,
radiative degrees of freedom, and fluxes from those at $\scri^+$
\cite{Ashtekar:2014zfa,Ashtekar:2015lla,Ashtekar:2015lxa}.  We restrict the
fixed-background question: how closely a finite cosmological calculation
approximates a specified asymptotically flat solution.  We compare minimal and
conformal scalar coupling, $\xi=0$ and $1/6$, separating common regulator
behavior from dependence on the asymptotic field equation
\cite{brady_radiative_1999}.

Writing $\Lambda=3/L^2$, SdS approaches Schwarzschild at fixed radius while
retaining its global causal structure at every finite $L$.  Schwarzschild
fields have inverse-power tails
\cite{price1972nonspherical,zengin2008hyperboloidal,
Angelopoulos:2016wcv}; radiative SdS modes eventually display cosmological
decay at a coupling-dependent rate
\cite{Brady:1996za,brady_radiative_1999,Molina:2003dc}.  In the large-horizon
limit, Brady et al. find the decay rate
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\gamma_{\ell,\xi}}{\kappa_c}
 =\ell+\frac32-\frac12\sqrt{9-48\xi}
 +\mathcal O(M/L),
 \label{eq:intro_brady_exponent}
\end{equation}
so the leading $\ell=1$ values are
$\gamma_{1,0}/\kappa_c=1$ and
$\gamma_{1,1/6}/\kappa_c=2$.
A finite-$L$ waveform can therefore agree with Schwarzschild only over a
finite interval.

The geometric setting is not new: we use the bridge construction of
Ref.~\cite{zenginoglu_bridge_2025} and the SdS limiting geometry of
Ref.~\cite{ZhouPanossoMacedo:2025}.  Our contribution is a quantitative
time-domain test of artificial cosmology.  We diagnose the waveform clock and
compactified coefficients, compare geometrically matched SdS and
Schwarzschild evolutions, measure controlled finite-time recovery, and
construct a conserved exterior-supported cosmological source.  An
intermediate Price-law interval provides a regulator benchmark; the
qualitative asymptotic distinction between Schwarzschild and SdS tails is
already established \cite{Brady:1996za,brady_radiative_1999,Molina:2003dc}.
The exterior construction improves the tested ringdown waveform at fixed
$L$, but its transition also generates long-time structure.  These are
fixed-background scalar results, not a test of the conformal Einstein
equations.

Section~\ref{sec:bridges} reviews the selected bridge and its flat limit,
Sec.~\ref{sec:scalar} defines the scalar comparison, and
Secs.~\ref{sec:results} and~\ref{sec:exterior-supported} give the uniform and
exterior-supported results.  The appendices collect numerical and
implementation audits.

\section{Controlled flat limit and benchmark foliation}
\label{sec:bridges}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Schwarzschild--de Sitter static patch}

The SdS metric in static coordinates is
\begin{align}
 ds^2&=-f(r)dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2d\omega^2,
 \label{eq:sds_metric}\\
 f(r)&=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{r^2}{L^2},
 \qquad d\omega^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2.
 \label{eq:f}
\end{align}
We use geometrized units $G_{\rm N}=c=1$.  The simulations set $M=1$;
coordinates are quoted in units of $M$, and comparison measures are
dimensionless.
For $0<M<L/(3\sqrt{3})$, $f$ has a black-hole root $r_b$, a
cosmological root $r_c$, and a negative root $r_u=-(r_b+r_c)$.  They obey
\begin{align}
 L^2&=r_b^2+r_br_c+r_c^2,
 &2ML^2&=r_br_c(r_b+r_c),
 \label{eq:root_relations}\\
 f(r)&=\frac{(r-r_b)(r_c-r)(r-r_u)}{L^2r}.
 \label{eq:f_factorized}
\end{align}
For $i=b,c$, the positive surface gravities follow from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kappa}.  The partial-fraction formulas use the positive
algebraic coefficient $\kappa_u:=|f'(r_u)|/2$ at the negative root:
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_i=\frac12\left|f'(r_i)\right|
 =\frac12\left|\frac{1}{r_i}-\frac{3r_i}{L^2}\right|,
 \qquad i\in\{b,c,u\}.
 \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
With an arbitrary reference radius $r_0\in(r_b,r_c)$, the normalized
tortoise coordinate is
\begin{align}
r_{*,L}(r)={}&\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}
 \ln\frac{r-r_b}{r_0-r_b}
-\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}
 \ln\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_0}
\notag\\
&+\frac{1}{2\kappa_u}
 \ln\frac{r-r_u}{r_0-r_u}.
\label{eq:tortoise}
\end{align}
The tortoise coordinate tends to $-\infty$ at $r_b$ and to $+\infty$ at
$r_c$.  In the large-$L$ limit,
\begin{equation}
 r_b\to2M,\qquad r_c=L-M+\mathcal{O}(M^2/L),
 \label{eq:horizon_flat_limit}
\end{equation}
so the static patch expands to the Schwarzschild exterior.  A precise
geometric identification of this limit is given in
Ref.~\cite{ZhouPanossoMacedo:2025}.

\subsection{Global bridge criterion}

To determine whether a horizon-regular foliation also has a controlled
Schwarzschild limit, introduce a stationary height function,
\begin{equation}
 \tau=t+h(r),\qquad B(r):=f(r)h'(r),
 \label{eq:height}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the boost relative to the tortoise coordinate $r_*$.  For a radial coordinate
$\rho=g(r)$ and $G=d\rho/dr$, the metric becomes
\begin{equation}
 ds^2=-f\,d\tau^2+\frac{2B}{G}\,d\tau d\rho
 +\frac{1-B^2}{fG^2}\,d\rho^2+r^2d\omega^2.
 \label{eq:transformed_metric}
\end{equation}
A future-directed spacelike bridge within the static patch satisfies
\begin{equation}
 |B|<1,\qquad B(r_b)=+1,\qquad B(r_c)=-1,
\label{eq:boost_conditions}
\end{equation}
where $(1-B^2)/f$ must tend to a finite positive nonzero value at each
horizon for a radial map with finite nonzero endpoint values of $G$.
Equivalently, $h\sim+r_*$ at $\hb$ and $h\sim-r_*$ at $\hc$.  These conditions
make the leaves null-transverse and give the transformed coefficients regular
horizon limits \cite{zenginoglu_bridge_2025}.  They are local conditions,
however, and do not determine the foliation beyond $\hc$.  On the future
branch of the expanding region, where $f<0$, spacelike continuation requires
$B<-1$.  How $B$ continues from its horizon value $B(r_c)=-1$ determines how
the leaves approach the spacelike future boundary.  This region also supports
forward and scattering problems \cite{Schlue:2012pq,Bernhardt:2024hfs}.

Two height functions demonstrate this remaining freedom.  Both are normalized
by $h(r_0)=0$ and have the same horizon data:
\begin{align}
h_{\rm min}(r)={}&
\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}\ln\left|\frac{r-r_b}{r_0-r_b}\right|
+\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}\ln\left|\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_0}\right|,
\label{eq:minimum_height}\\
h_{\rm mg}(r)={}&
\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}
\ln\left|\frac{(r-r_b)r_0}{(r_0-r_b)r}\right|
+\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}
\ln\left|\frac{(r_c-r)r_0}{(r_c-r_0)r}\right|
\notag\\
&-\frac{1}{2\kappa_u}
\ln\left|\frac{(r-r_u)r_0}{(r_0-r_u)r}\right|.
\label{eq:minimal_height}
\end{align}
Local horizon regularity supplies only the two logarithms in $h_{\rm min}$.
The construction arose in hyperboloidal QNM expansions
\cite{AnsorgMacedo:2016,MacedoJaramilloAnsorg:2018}, and the term ``minimal
gauge'' was introduced in Ref.~\cite{Macedo:2020}.  Its derivation for static
spherical metrics uses the in--out and out--in strategies of
Ref.~\cite{PanossoMacedo:2023qzp}.  For SdS, the out--in construction adds the
regular third-root contribution and yields a controlled Schwarzschild limit
\cite{ZhouPanossoMacedo:2025}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:minimal_height} gives that construction in our sign and
normalization conventions.

Both families satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:boost_conditions}, but their global limits
differ.  Figure~\ref{fig:bridge_foliations} follows the leaves beyond $\hc$.
The curves are constant-time leaves, not a sequence in $L$.  Their geometric
continuation distinguishes the two families: the analytic large-$L$ limit of
the minimum-height family closes toward a Cauchy-like asymptotic end, whereas
the minimal-gauge family opens toward Schwarzschild $\scri^+$.  Regular
horizon crossing alone therefore does not select the desired flat limit.

Matched waveforms also require a common retarded-time origin.  For a bridge
normalized by $h(r_0)=0$ and $r_{*,L}(r_0)=0$, the offset between $\tau$ and
the outgoing null coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 q_B(L;r_0):=\lim_{r\to r_c}(h+r_{*,L})
 =\int_{r_0}^{r_c}\frac{1+B}{f}\,dr.
 \label{eq:general_q}
\end{equation}
The integrand is regular at $r_c$.  If $q_B$ diverges, the finite-$L$ waveform
clock has no finite Schwarzschild limit.  A finite $q_B$ supplies the required
common time origin.

For the minimal-gauge Schwarzschild limit and the normalization used below,
\begin{equation}
 q_0=4M\ln\frac{r_0}{r_0-2M},
 \label{eq:q_zero}
\end{equation}
which gives $q_0=4M\ln2$ at $r_0=4M$.

\subsection{Compactification, clock, and coefficient behavior}

In addition to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:minimum_height} and
\eqref{eq:minimal_height}, we consider four boosts specified directly:
\begin{align}
B_{\rm lin}&=-1+2\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_b},
\label{eq:linear_boost}\\
B_{\rm fl}&=-1+2\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_b}\frac{r_b^2}{r^2},
\label{eq:flat_linear_boost}\\
B_{\rm Mav}&=-\frac{1-3M/r}{\sqrt{1-9M^2\Lambda}}
 \sqrt{1+\frac{6M}{r}},
\label{eq:mav_boost}\\
B_{\rm sr}&=-\gamma r+\frac{\beta}{r^2},
\label{eq:slow_roll_boost}\\
\gamma&=\frac{r_c^2+r_b^2}{r_c^3-r_b^3},\qquad
\beta=\frac{r_c^2r_b^2(r_c+r_b)}{r_c^3-r_b^3}.
\label{eq:slow_roll_parameters}
\end{align}
The Mavrogiannis boost is motivated by wave estimates on SdS and turns at the
photon sphere $r=3M$
\cite{mavrogiannis2024quasilinear,mavrogiannis2023morawetz}.

At fixed areal radius, with $x=M/r$, the six boosts have the limits
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{lcc}
\text{bridge}&B_0(r)&B_0(\infty)\\
\hline
\text{minimum height}&3x-\tfrac12&-\tfrac12\\
\text{minimal gauge}&-1+8x^2&-1\\
\text{linear}&+1&+1\\
\text{modified linear}&-1+8x^2&-1\\
\text{Mavrogiannis}&-(1-3x)\sqrt{1+6x}&-1\\
\text{slow roll}&4x^2&0
\end{array}
\label{eq:boost_limits}
\end{equation}
An outgoing Schwarzschild hyperboloidal slice must satisfy $B_0\to-1$ as
$r\to\infty$.  With compactification~\eqref{eq:compactification}, regularity
at null infinity further requires $1+B_0=\mathcal{O}(M^2/r^2)$ with a finite
positive leading coefficient.  These conditions exclude the minimum-height,
linear, and slow-roll families.  The minimal, modified-linear, and
Mavrogiannis families satisfy the pointwise criterion.  Pointwise convergence
does not by itself control a compactified evolution.  For this comparison, we
adopt uniform convergence and nondegeneracy of the principal and
characteristic coefficients on the compact interval as a design criterion.
This is not a proof that every observable requires the same conditions.  We
examine the lower-order potential separately; in particular, its minimally
coupled endpoint limit remains nonuniform in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:coupling_endpoint_limits}.  An outer layer in the
modified-linear family already violates the principal-coefficient criterion
near $\rho=1$.

We place both horizons at fixed coordinate locations using
\begin{align}
 \rho&=\frac{1-r_b/r}{1-r_b/r_c},
 &r(\rho)&=\frac{r_b}{1-(1-r_b/r_c)\rho},
 \label{eq:compactification}\\
 G(r)&=\frac{d\rho}{dr}
 =\frac{r_b}{(1-r_b/r_c)r^2}.
 \label{eq:G}
\end{align}
The black-hole and cosmological horizons are at $\rho=0$ and $1$,
respectively.  At fixed $r$,
\begin{equation}
 \rho\to\rho_0:=1-\frac{2M}{r},
 \label{eq:rho_limit}
\end{equation}
which is also the compactification used by the separate Schwarzschild
implementation.

The corresponding dimensionless spacetime conformal factor may be chosen as
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_L:=\frac{r_b}{r}
 =1-\left(1-\frac{r_b}{r_c}\right)\rho .
 \label{eq:conformal_factor}
\end{equation}
At every finite $L$ the outer endpoint is the cosmological horizon and
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_L(1)=\frac{r_b}{r_c}>0.
 \label{eq:finite_L_conformal_factor}
\end{equation}
In the Schwarzschild limit,
$\Omega_L\to\Omega_0=1-\rho$ and $\Omega_0(1)=0$, so the same endpoint becomes
$\scri^+$.  Misner's regulator relies on the contrast between this vanishing
limit and the finite-$L$ value in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite_L_conformal_factor}.  To expose the relevant scale,
suppose that $g$ satisfies $G_{ab}[g]+\Lambda g_{ab}=0$ and define
$\bar g=\Omega_L^2g$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 G_{ab}[\bar g]=\mathcal T_{ab}[\Omega_L]
 -\frac{\Lambda}{\Omega_L^2}\bar g_{ab},
 \label{eq:conformal_einstein_scaling}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T_{ab}$ collects derivatives of $\Omega_L$
\cite{Zenginoglu:2008pw}.  At the cosmological horizon,
$\Lambda/\Omega_L^2\to3/(4M^2)$ as $L/M\to\infty$.  A nonzero $\Omega_L$,
however, neither proves regularity nor guarantees good conditioning.  The
scalar equations contain no tensorial conformal Einstein source terms, gauge
couplings, or gravitational constraints and therefore cannot test those
properties.  Minimal scalar coupling nevertheless retains a lower-order
endpoint term with the same $\Lambda/\Omega_L^2$ scaling, whereas conformal
coupling cancels it.  The two cases provide, respectively, an algebraic stress
test and a conformally regular radiative analogue.  They test whether matched
scalar observables recover their Schwarzschild counterparts in a controlled
large-$L$ limit, not whether the Einstein system does so.

For the coefficient diagnostics, define
\begin{align}
 p&:=\frac{d\rho}{dr_*}=fG,
 &A&:=\frac{p}{1-B^2},
 \label{eq:coefficients}\\
 c_-&=-A(1+B)=-\frac{p}{1-B},
 &c_+&=A(1-B)=\frac{p}{1+B}.
 \label{eq:characteristic_speeds}
\end{align}
Figure~\ref{fig:foliation_limit} tests the six bridge families under the fixed
compactification~\eqref{eq:compactification}.  The minimum-height, linear,
and slow-roll clock offsets diverge as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 q_{\rm min}&=\tfrac12L\ln2+\mathcal O\!\left[M\ln(L/M)\right],\\
 q_{\rm lin}&=2L\ln2+\mathcal O\!\left[M\ln(L/M)\right],\\
 q_{\rm sr}&=L\ln2+\mathcal O\!\left[M\ln(L/M)\right].
\end{split}
\label{eq:offset_asymptotics}
\end{equation}
Across $20\leq L/M\leq160$, the minimal-gauge offset moves from
$2.4619M$ to $2.7243M$ toward $q_0=2.7726M$.  The modified-linear family
approaches the same value, whereas the finite Mavrogiannis offset approaches a
different Schwarzschild hyperboloidal clock.

 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figs/foliation_flat_limit.pdf}
 \caption{Large-$L$ bridge diagnostics.  (a) Fixed-$r$ boost limits;
 (b) outer-boundary clock offsets; (c) maximum characteristic speeds; and
 (d) minimum $A$ on the compact interval.  The minimal gauge has the required

 \label{fig:foliation_limit}

Under the adopted matched-protocol criteria, the coefficient panels exclude
the minimum-height, linear, and slow-roll families, while the modified-linear
family fails uniform principal-part convergence at the outer endpoint.  The
Mavrogiannis family remains bounded but approaches a different Schwarzschild
hyperboloidal clock.
For the linear boost at $L/M=160$,
$M\max|c_\pm|=19.86$ and $M\min A=3.88\times10^{-5}$, with the maximum speed
growing linearly in $L/M$.  The minimum of $MA$ falls as $(M/L)^2$ for the
minimum-height and slow-roll families, and linear slices share this decay.
The minimal and Mavrogiannis gauges instead keep coefficients of order
$M^{-1}$; at $L/M=160$ their
$(M\max|c_\pm|,M\min A)$ pairs are $(0.2498,0.06324)$ and
$(0.1499,0.07495)$.  The modified-linear family has the right pointwise boost
but a nonuniform outer layer,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{L\to\infty}M A_{\rm fl}(1)=\frac14
 \ne
 \lim_{\rho\to1^-}\lim_{L\to\infty}M A_{\rm fl}(\rho)=\frac18,
\label{eq:modified_linear_layer}
\end{equation}
with $Mc_{+,\rm fl}(1)\to1/2$ rather than the interior Schwarzschild value
$1/4$.  Thus some horizon-regular families have the wrong asymptotic end,
some have divergent clocks, and others develop degenerate or nonuniform
compactified coefficients.  The minimal gauge provides the required end,
the chosen Schwarzschild clock, and bounded coefficients, and we use it for
the matched numerical sequence
\cite{PanossoMacedo:2023qzp,ZhouPanossoMacedo:2025}.

\section{Scalar-wave benchmark and comparison protocol}
\label{sec:scalar}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Curvature coupling and regular first-order system}

We study the massless curvature-coupled equation
\begin{equation}
 \left(\Box_g-\xi R[g]\right)\Phi=0,
 \qquad \xi\in\left\{0,\frac16\right\},
 \label{eq:curvature_coupled_wave}
\end{equation}
where $g$ denotes the physical SdS or Schwarzschild metric.  Minimal coupling
has $\xi=0$; conformal coupling in four spacetime dimensions has $\xi=1/6$.
Write
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(t,r,\theta,\varphi)=\frac{1}{r}
 \sum_{\ell m}u_{\ell m}(t,r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi).
 \label{eq:scalar_decomposition}
\end{equation}
Each mode satisfies
\begin{align}
 \left(-\partial_t^2+\partial_{r_*}^2-V_{\ell,\xi}\right)u&=0,
 \label{eq:wave_equation}\\
 V_{\ell,\xi}&=f\left[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}
 +\frac{f'(r)}{r}+\xi R[g]\right].
 \label{eq:scalar_potential}
\end{align}
For uniform SdS, $R[g]=12/L^2$ and therefore
\begin{equation}
 V_{\ell,\xi}=f\left[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}
 +\frac{2M}{r^3}+\frac{2(6\xi-1)}{L^2}\right].
 \label{eq:uniform_coupled_potential}
\end{equation}
The explicit $-2/L^2$ term occurs at $\xi=0$ and cancels at $\xi=1/6$.
In Schwarzschild, $R[g]=0$, so both couplings give the same equation and the
same reference waveform.

The conformal transformation isolates the role of curvature coupling.  Let
$\bar g=\Omega_L^2 g$ and $\phi=\Omega_L^{-1}\Phi$.  For the sourced equation,
conformal covariance gives \cite{ZenginogluKidder:2010}
\begin{equation}
 \left(\Box_{\bar g}-\frac{R[\bar g]}6\right)\phi
 +\left(\frac16-\xi\right)\frac{R[g]}{\Omega_L^2}\phi
 =\Omega_L^{-3}S.
 \label{eq:conformal_source_transform}
\end{equation}
The second term survives at $\xi=0$ but vanishes at $\xi=1/6$.  Because
$\Omega_L=r_b/r$, the evolved modal field is proportional to its unphysical
counterpart, $\phi_{\ell m}=(u_{\ell m}/r_b)Y_{\ell m}$.  The distinction
therefore lies not in whether the field is rescaled, but in the physical
curvature coupling represented by that field.  The same identity marks the
limits of the analogy with the motivating Einstein system: neither coupling
captures all of its tensorial, gauge, and constraint structure.  The uniform,
localized-source, cross-code, caustic, and ringdown-window results below use
$\xi=0$.  The uniform large-$L$ Price-law ladder also uses $\xi=0$; the
matched $L/M=640$ uniform and exterior tail comparison uses both $\xi=0$ and
$1/6$.  Finally, ``minimal'' in ``minimal gauge'' describes the foliation and
is unrelated to minimal scalar coupling.

We suppress the mode labels below.  With $p$ and $A$ defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:coefficients}, set
\begin{equation}
 P_{\ell,\xi}:=\frac{V_{\ell,\xi}}{p}.
 \label{eq:potential_coefficient}
\end{equation}
The first-order fields are
\begin{equation}
 \psi:=\partial_\rho u,\qquad
 \pi:=\frac{1-B^2}{p}\partial_\tau u-B\psi.
 \label{eq:first_order_variables}
\end{equation}
The evolution system is
\begin{align}
 \partial_\tau u&=A(B\psi+\pi),
 \label{eq:evolve_u}\\
 \partial_\tau\psi&=\partial_\rho\!\left[A(B\psi+\pi)\right],
 \label{eq:evolve_psi}\\
 \partial_\tau\pi&=\partial_\rho\!\left[A(\psi+B\pi)\right]-P_{\ell,\xi}u.
 \label{eq:evolve_pi}
\end{align}
We monitor the reduction constraint
\begin{equation}
 C:=\psi-\partial_\rho u.
 \label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}

The long exterior-tail calculation uses a conservative characteristic
reduction and is checked independently against an algebraically equivalent
endpoint-factored formulation.  Appendix~\ref{app:latetime} gives the
reduction and comparison.

The radial characteristic speeds are given in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:characteristic_speeds}.
The endpoint speeds are $(c_+,c_-)=(0,c_-<0)$ at $\rho=0$ and
$(c_+,c_-)=(c_+>0,0)$ at $\rho=1$.  The characteristic flow determines all
endpoint values.  For a
simple crossing with $B'(r_h)\ne0$, analytic cancellation gives
\begin{equation}
 A_h=\frac{f'(r_h)G(r_h)}{-2s_hB'(r_h)},\qquad
 s_b=+1,\quad s_c=-1.
 \label{eq:A_endpoint}
\end{equation}
We cancel every height-function pole algebraically and assign
Eq.~\eqref{eq:A_endpoint} directly at each endpoint.

The evolution domain ends on $\hc$; no data from beyond it enter the
calculation.  We use the analytic continuation of the height function only as
a geometric diagnostic of the foliation family and of the asymptotic end it
approaches as $L/M\to\infty$.  The finite-$L$ evolutions test the regular
horizon coefficients and extract the outer waveform on $\hc$.

For the minimal gauge, the limiting Schwarzschild coefficients are
\begin{align}
 B_0&=-1+2(1-\rho_0)^2,
 &A_0&=\frac{1}{8M(2-\rho_0)},
 \label{eq:schwarz_coefficients}\\
 P_{\rm Schw}&=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)+(1-\rho_0)}{2M}.
 \label{eq:schwarz_potential}
\end{align}
The Schwarzschild code implements these closed-form expressions separately
from the finite-$L$ geometry.
For uniform SdS at fixed interior $\rho$, both finite-$L$ potentials approach
this result.  At the moving outer endpoint, however, the limits are
\begin{align}
 \lim_{L\to\infty}P_{\ell,0}(1)
 &=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)-2}{2M},\notag\\
 \lim_{L\to\infty}P_{\ell,1/6}(1)
 &=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2M}=P_{\rm Schw}(1).
 \label{eq:coupling_endpoint_limits}
\end{align}
For $\ell=1$, the minimal-coupling endpoint limit vanishes while the
conformal-coupling limit equals the Schwarzschild value $1/M$.  This
nonuniformity is a lower-order effect and does not alter the characteristics,
but it can change the waveform tail at the outer boundary.

\subsection{Matched data, sources, clocks, and observables}
\label{sec:protocol}

The physical data and source are matched geometrically, not by assigning the
same compact-coordinate profile.
Define the peak-normalized bump
\begin{equation}
 b(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 \exp[1-1/(1-x^2)],&|x|<1,\\
 0,&|x|\geq1.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:bump}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Fixed-window data.}
The finite-time comparison uses the same areal-radius profile on every
background,
\begin{equation}
 u(0,r)=b\!\left(\frac{r-4M}{1.5M}\right),\qquad
 \psi=D_\rho u,\qquad \pi=-B\psi,
\label{eq:displacement_data}
\end{equation}
with support $2.5M<r<5.5M$.  Here $D_\rho$ is the spectral differentiation
operator; $\partial_\tau u=\partial_tu=0$ initially, so the data are
Killing-stationary while retaining a nonzero bridge-normal derivative.

\paragraph{Tail data.}
For the late-time study,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 u&=\psi=0, &G_{\rm v}(r)&=b\!\left(\frac{r-6M}{3M}\right),\\
 \partial_\tau u&=G_{\rm v}, &\pi&=G_{\rm v}/A,
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:velocity_profile}
\end{equation}
with support $3M<r<9M$.  The factor $A^{-1}$ fixes the Killing-time derivative
across the $L$-dependent bridge hypersurfaces.  Equations
\eqref{eq:displacement_data} and \eqref{eq:velocity_profile} thus define the
geometric data map.

\paragraph{Retarded time.}
We set $r_0=4M$ and normalize
\begin{equation}
 h_L(r_0)=0,\qquad r_{*,L}(r_0)=0.
\end{equation}
For the minimal gauge, the cosmological-horizon logarithms in
$h_L+r_{*,L}$ cancel exactly, giving
\begin{align}
q_L={}&\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}
\ln\frac{(r_c-r_b)^2r_0}{r_c(r_0-r_b)^2}
\notag\\
&+\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa_u}-\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}\right)
\ln\frac{r_c}{r_0}.
\label{eq:q_L}
\end{align}
Waveforms are compared at the geometric retarded time
\begin{equation}
 U=\tau-q_L.
\label{eq:retarded_time}
\end{equation}
No relative time translation, amplitude rescaling, time dilation, or transfer
correction is fitted.
At $\hc$, $U$ is the outgoing retarded-time coordinate.  The same
normalization reaches Schwarzschild $\scri^+$ in the limit.  At finite
radius, $U$ is a stationary clock normalized at the outer boundary.  Its
relation to the local outgoing coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 U-(t-r_{*,L})=h_L(r)+r_{*,L}(r)-q_L.
\label{eq:finite_radius_clock}
\end{equation}
Because an additive clock offset does not affect a decay rate, we quote
transition times using the geometric origin in Eq.~\eqref{eq:retarded_time}.

\paragraph{Localized source.}
\label{sec:localized_protocol}

We also use a normalized source localized in radius, time, and angle.  Its
independent spherical-harmonic modes coherently reconstruct a multimode and
caustic response
\cite{DolanOttewill:2011,ZenginogluGalley:2012,CasalsNolan:2023}; no
nonspherical or nonlinear evolution is involved.  With zero initial data,
\begin{equation}
 \left(\Box_g-\xi R[g]\right)\Phi=S,
 \qquad \Phi|_{\tau=0}=\partial_\tau\Phi|_{\tau=0}=0.
 \label{eq:sourced_wave_equation}
\end{equation}
The same physical source $S$ is used on every background; its conformal
representation is $\Omega_L^{-3}S$ by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:conformal_source_transform}.  Let $\gamma$ be the angle from
$(\theta_s,\varphi_s)=(\pi/2,0)$ and set
\begin{equation}
 S=\mathcal A\,T(t)\,R(r)\,\mathcal P_\kappa(\gamma),
 \label{eq:source_product}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
 T(t)&=\frac{b\left[(t-t_s)/\sigma_t\right]}{\sigma_t\beta_0},
 &
 R(r)&=\frac{b\left[(r-r_s)/\sigma_r\right]}{\beta_2},
 \notag\\
 \mathcal P_\kappa(\gamma)&=\frac{\kappa\,e^{\kappa(\cos\gamma-1)}}
 {2\pi\left(1-e^{-2\kappa}\right)}.
 \label{eq:source_angular}
\end{align}
Here $\beta_0=\int_{-1}^{1}b(x)dx$ and
$\beta_2=\int b[(r-r_s)/\sigma_r]r^2dr$, so each factor has unit integral and
$\int\sqrt{-g}S\,d^4x=\mathcal A$.  We use $r_s=6M$,
$\sigma_r=0.75M$, $t_s=30M$, $\sigma_t=2M$, and $\kappa=64$, corresponding
to radial support $5.25M<r<6.75M$, temporal support $28M<t<32M$, and angular
scale $\kappa^{-1/2}=0.125$ radians.  Source width is therefore a matched
physical parameter, not a numerical error bar.  The production sequence uses
$\xi=0$.

The real-spherical-harmonic coefficients are
\begin{equation}
 a_{\ell m}=\frac{i_\ell(\kappa)}{i_0(\kappa)}
 Y_{\ell m}(\theta_s,\varphi_s),
 \label{eq:source_modal_amplitudes}
\end{equation}
where $i_\ell$ is a modified spherical Bessel function.  Modes below
$10^{-13}$ of the maximum are dropped.  The $m$ dependence factors out, so
one response per retained $\ell$ solves
\begin{equation}
 \left(-\partial_t^2+\partial_{r_*}^2-V_{\ell,\xi}\right)v_\ell
 =f r\,\mathcal A T(t)R(r).
 \label{eq:sourced_radial_equation}
\end{equation}
In the first-order system the source is evaluated explicitly at the Killing
time $t=\tau-h_L(r)$, ensuring the same emitter on every background.  With
$u_{\ell m}=a_{\ell m}v_\ell$, the reconstructed waveform is
\begin{equation}
 u(U,\theta,\varphi)
 =\sum_{\ell m}a_{\ell m}v_\ell(U)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi),
 \label{eq:angular_reconstruction}
\end{equation}
at $r=8M$, $r=12M$, and the outer boundary, always using
Eq.~\eqref{eq:retarded_time}.

At the outer endpoint, the $\xi=0$ fixed-mode observable is the
reduced field $W_L(U)\equiv W_{L,0}(U):=u_2(U,\rho=1)$ on $\hc$; its
Schwarzschild counterpart $W_{\rm Schw}(U):=u_2(U,\rho_0=1)$ is evaluated at
$\scri^+$.  Thus $W$ is the
relevant spherical-harmonic coefficient of the radiation-scaled field
$r\Phi$, not the full field $\Phi$.  For a common time interval
$\mathcal J$, the direct fixed-mode waveform error is
\begin{equation}
 E_2(L;\mathcal J)=
 \frac{\|W_L-W_{\rm Schw}\|_{L^2(\mathcal J)}}
      {\|W_{\rm Schw}\|_{L^2(\mathcal J)}},
 \label{eq:regulator_E2}
\end{equation}
where $W_{\rm Schw}$ is computed with the separate closed-form Schwarzschild
coefficient implementation.  For the localized source, $W$ denotes the
outer-boundary modal
collection $\{u_{\ell m}\}$ and the primary comparison uses the
sphere-integrated Parseval norm
\begin{equation}
 \|W\|_{\mathcal J,S^2}^2=
 \int_{\mathcal J}dU\sum_{\ell m}|u_{\ell m}(U)|^2.
 \label{eq:parseval_norm}
\end{equation}
The corresponding relative error is
\begin{equation}
 E_{2,S^2}(L;\mathcal J)=
 \frac{\|W_L-W_{\rm Schw}\|_{\mathcal J,S^2}}
      {\|W_{\rm Schw}\|_{\mathcal J,S^2}}.
 \label{eq:localized_regulator_E2}
\end{equation}
For the minimally coupled production sequence, we use the fixed-window
asymptotic ansatz
\begin{equation}
 W_{L,0}=W_{\rm Schw}+c_{1,0}\frac{M}{L}
 +c_{2,0}\left(\frac{M}{L}\right)^2
 +\mathcal O\!\left((M/L)^3\right).
 \label{eq:regulator_expansion}
\end{equation}
This is an empirical asymptotic ansatz for each fixed comparison interval,
not an analytically derived expansion uniform in retarded time.  Its adequacy
on the stated intervals is tested by the two nested triples and by comparison
with the independently evolved Schwarzschild waveform.  We make no
corresponding claim for the unresolved late-time regime.

The three-level combination
\begin{equation}
 W_\infty^{(L)}=
 \frac{W_L-6W_{2L}+8W_{4L}}{3},
 \label{eq:nested_extrapolant}
\end{equation}
cancels the two displayed finite-$L$ corrections.  We test this extrapolation,
rather than assume it, by comparing the nested triples based at $L/M=80$ and
$160$ with one another and with $W_{\rm Schw}$.  A conformally coupled
sequence must establish its own finite-$L$ expansion rather than inherit
these coefficients.

The fixed-mode comparison uses cumulative intervals that begin at $U=0$ and
end at $U/M=40$, $80$, and $160$, together with the disjoint intervals
$40\leq U/M\leq80$ and $80\leq U/M\leq160$.  The localized-source comparison
uses the common simulated interval $0\lesssim U/M\leq57.2$, from the first
nonnegative output time through the last time reached by every evolution.
Before evaluating a norm, we linearly interpolate all samples onto a common
$U$ grid.

\subsection{Numerical implementations and error control}

Two double-precision implementations use independent spatial discretizations
and time integrators.  The fixed-mode family uses Dedalus~3
\cite{burns_dedalus_2020}, a Chebyshev-$T$ basis, and RK222.  The localized
source uses a uniform radial grid, eighth-order finite differences, and
classical RK4 with staged source evaluation.  Its radial derivative uses a
centered nine-point stencil in the interior and polynomially matched
one-sided nine-point closures at the first and last four nodes.  The closures
use only physical grid values; there are no ghost points, artificial
dissipation, or filters.  Both characteristic endpoints are evolved without
prescribed boundary data.  The implementations share the analytic geometry,
comparison protocol, and modal reconstruction, but not the spatial or
temporal discretization.  Appendix~\ref{app:convergence} gives a direct
sourced-waveform comparison.

Every production family has a three-level refinement ladder.  Uniform and
localized-source comparisons use matched Schwarzschild controls; when the
exterior ringdown sequence shares a frozen Schwarzschild reference, its
independently measured floor enters the ordering audit.  We distinguish
discretization error from estimator, window, and cadence sensitivities and
from physical source-width dependence.  Appendix~\ref{app:convergence} gives
the complete ladders and numerical audit; Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}
records the exterior implementation and provenance.

\section{Schwarzschild recovery with uniform artificial cosmology}
\label{sec:results}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

With the minimal-gauge family, the finite-$L$ waveforms converge directly
toward the separately evolved Schwarzschild reference, and the two nested
extrapolants support agreement at the conservative $1\%$ level for the tested
prompt-dominated observables.  Unless stated otherwise, the results in this
section use minimal coupling, $\xi=0$.

\subsection{Direct and extrapolated fixed-mode recovery}
\label{sec:direct_recovery}
\label{sec:extrapolated_recovery}

For the minimally coupled, prompt-dominated pure-$\ell=2$ sequence, direct
agreement---including the case-specific numerical margin---first reaches
$5\%$ at $L/M=320$ and $2\%$ at $L/M=640$.  It does not reach $1\%$ through
$L/M=640$.  These results answer the practical scalar-wave question of how
large $L/M$ must be for a finite cosmological calculation to approximate the
chosen Schwarzschild observable.  The answer depends on the observable and
accuracy threshold; in particular, these thresholds do not apply to the
localized-source response.

Across $L/M=20$ through $640$, the boundary waveforms retain the prompt-pulse
and ringdown morphology of the Schwarzschild reference
(Fig.~\ref{fig:flat_sequence}).  In the large-$L$ regime, the dominant

expansion in Eq.~\eqref{eq:regulator_expansion}.

\subsection{Supporting Schwarzschild--de Sitter physics}
\label{sec:sds_physics}

Caustic timing supplies an independent probe of the global SdS geometry.  In
the minimally coupled localized response, we measure the shift $D_1$ in the
spacing between the direct pulse and the first echo.  Its scaling is
compatible with a local correction of order $(M/L)^2$ at fixed areal radius
and a larger outer response approaching order $M/L$.  The combined
deterministic timing sensitivities exceed the specified tolerance in all four
target-bearing cases.  The fits therefore test these powers but do not
determine precise coefficients.

Appendices~\ref{app:convergence} and~\ref{app:latetime} give the complete
timing estimator, selection, fit, and sensitivity audit.

The cosmological scale also limits how long a finite-$L$, minimally coupled
waveform can track Schwarzschild.  At $r=8M$, the $\ell=1$ waveform departs
from Schwarzschild decay at $U=115.9M$ for $L/M=80$ and at $133.5M$ for
$L/M=160$.  The near constancy of these times in units of $M$ identifies a
black-hole scale.  Cosmological decay begins at $\kappa_cU=2.83$ and $2.94$,
whose near constancy instead identifies the scale $\kappa_c^{-1}$.  These
scales separate as $L$ increases.

The $L/M=80$ and $160$ waveforms depart before the Schwarzschild Price-law
regime is established: at the same radius, the independent reference does not
settle within $5\%$ of its $\ell=1$ power law until $U=220.3M$.  This
short-length sequence therefore shows a Schwarzschild-like transient,
departure, and cosmological decay, rather than a power-law plateau followed by
a cosmological tail.  Appendix~\ref{app:latetime} gives the rate-estimator

the large-$L$ recovery benchmark, while
Sec.~\ref{sec:matched-tail-result} compares uniform and exterior-supported
tails at $L/M=640$ for both curvature couplings.

\section{Exterior-supported artificial cosmology}
\label{sec:exterior-supported}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The uniform construction alters the metric throughout the static patch,
including the strong-field region.  To reduce this finite-$L$ bias while
retaining a cosmological horizon, we choose the same exterior value
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda=\frac{3}{L^2},
 \label{eq:exterior_lambda}
\end{equation}
but make its conserved source vanish across a domain containing the event
horizon, initial-data support, scalar emitter, and photon sphere.  The metric,
boost, curvature, and scalar potential are then exactly Schwarzschild there;
differences arise only from the outer layer, cosmological boundary, and clock.
Comparison with uniform SdS at fixed $L$ tests whether removing the near-zone
deformation reduces the total waveform bias.  The two families also differ in
their outer geometry and foliation, including the $R/6$ layer term for
conformal coupling.  The bridge and clock still obey the global limit of
Sec.~\ref{sec:bridges}.  Exact near-zone shielding is special to the static
spherical ansatz and need not survive the constraints of a nonlinear $3+1$
problem.

\subsection{Conserved cosmological tensor and exact metric}

A coordinate-dependent substitution $\Lambda\mapsto\lambda(r)$ in the
vacuum equations is inconsistent:
\begin{equation}
 G_{ab}+\lambda g_{ab}=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 0=\nabla^aG_{ab}=-\nabla_b\lambda,
 \label{eq:variable_lambda_bianchi}
\end{equation}
so $\lambda$ must be constant.  We instead use a conserved tensor,
\begin{equation}
 G^a{}_b+\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b=0,
 \qquad \nabla_a\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b=0,
 \label{eq:cosmological_tensor_equations}
\end{equation}
which becomes $\Lambda\delta^a{}_b$ wherever the cosmological term is
constant.  This uses Dymnikova's radially boost-invariant anisotropic-vacuum
framework \cite{Dymnikova:2000algebraic,Dymnikova:2002mass}, with the usual
regular-center profile reversed: the tensor vanishes for $r\leq R_0$ and
reaches the constant-$\Lambda$ form for $r\geq R_1$.

Let $\eta(x)=e^{-1/x}$ for $x>0$ and zero otherwise, and
$S(x)=\eta(x)/[\eta(x)+\eta(1-x)]$.  For $2M<R_0<R_1<r_c$, choose
\begin{equation}
 \chi_L(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&r\leq R_0,\\[1mm]
 S\!\left(\zeta_L(r)\right),&R_0<r<R_1,\\[1mm]
 1,&r\geq R_1,
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:exterior_switch}
\end{equation}
where $\zeta_L$ is a smooth monotone map with
$\zeta_L(R_0)=0$ and $\zeta_L(R_1)=1$.
The production prescription keeps both the transition and exact-SdS cap
resolved at large $L$ (Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}).  Set
$\lambda(r)=\Lambda\chi_L(r)$.

The metric that preserves the exterior Kottler mass parameter $M$ is
\begin{equation}
 ds^2=-f_\chi(r)dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f_\chi(r)}+r^2d\omega^2,
 \label{eq:exterior_metric}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 f_\chi(r)&=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{r^2}{3}\lambda(r)\\
 &=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{r^2}{L^2}\chi_L(r).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exterior_f}
\end{equation}
For $M>0$, this metric is exactly Schwarzschild below $R_0$ and SdS above
$R_1$.  The layer is nonvacuum in ordinary Einstein gravity but exactly
solves Eq.~\eqref{eq:cosmological_tensor_equations} with an anisotropic source.

Write
\begin{equation}
 f_\chi=1-\frac{2m_\chi}{r},
 \qquad
 m_\chi(r)=M+\frac{r^3\lambda(r)}{6}.
 \label{eq:exterior_mass_function}
\end{equation}
Define the two source eigenvalues
\begin{align}
 \Lambda_{\parallel}(r)&=\lambda+\frac{r}{3}\lambda',
 \label{eq:lambda_parallel}\\
 \Lambda_{\perp}(r)&=\lambda+r\lambda'
 +\frac{r^2}{6}\lambda''.
 \label{eq:lambda_perp}
\end{align}
The exact cosmological tensor is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
 \Lambda_{\parallel},\Lambda_{\parallel},
 \Lambda_{\perp},\Lambda_{\perp}\right).
 \label{eq:exterior_cosmological_tensor}
\end{equation}
The only nontrivial component of the conservation law reduces to
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_a\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_r
 =\Lambda_{\parallel}'
 +\frac{2}{r}\left(\Lambda_{\parallel}-\Lambda_{\perp}\right)=0,
 \label{eq:exterior_conservation_check}
\end{equation}
which follows directly from the eigenvalues; the other components vanish by
symmetry.  Thus $\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b=0$ below $R_0$ and
$\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b=\Lambda\delta^a{}_b$ above $R_1$, while conservation
requires the derivative terms in the layer.

On the matter side,
$T^a{}_b=-(8\pi)^{-1}\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b
=\operatorname{diag}(-\varepsilon,p_r,p_\perp,p_\perp)$, with
$\varepsilon=\Lambda_{\parallel}/(8\pi)$,
$p_r=-\varepsilon$, and $p_\perp=-\Lambda_{\perp}/(8\pi)$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon+p_r=0,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon+p_\perp=-\frac{r}{16\pi}\Lambda_{\parallel}'.
 \label{eq:exterior_nec}
\end{equation}
The radial null energy condition is saturated.  Since
$\int_{R_0}^{R_1}\Lambda_{\parallel}'dr=\Lambda>0$, the tangential condition
is violated somewhere in the layer.  This artificial medium is therefore not
ordinary matter, an important limitation for stability and any covariant
nonlinear completion.

We do not claim here that $\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b$ follows from a covariant
matter action or possesses a consistent independent dynamics.

The curvature scalar is
\begin{equation}
 R_\chi=4\lambda+\frac{8}{3}r\lambda'
 +\frac{1}{3}r^2\lambda'',
 \label{eq:exterior_ricci_scalar}
\end{equation}
and is zero in the interior and $4\Lambda$ in the exterior.  The layer is
smooth; Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} gives further diagnostics.  The
Misner--Sharp mass accumulated across the layer is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Delta m_{\rm tr}}{M}
 :=\frac{m_\chi(R_1)-m_\chi(R_0)}{M}
 =\frac12\frac{(R_1/M)^3}{(L/M)^2}.
 \label{eq:transition_mass}
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} gives its large-$L$ scaling.
The tested sequence is still preasymptotic: at $L/M=640$ this quantity is
$104.3$, which is not perturbatively small.  Its large value is compatible
with small local curvature because the source is diffuse and distant, but it
need not be weak in nonlinear Einstein evolution.  We retain it as a global
diagnostic.

A narrow transition can produce large derivatives and scattering even when
$\Lambda$ is small; the production profile therefore uses a broad,
spectrally resolved layer.

Because $0\leq\chi_L\leq1$,
$f_{\rm SdS}\leq f_\chi\leq f_{\rm Schw}$.  For $R_0>6M$ and $R_1<r_c$,
this excludes a transition-region horizon: $f_\chi>0$ on $(2M,r_c)$.  The
black-hole horizon and surface gravity are exactly $2M$ and $(4M)^{-1}$, and
$r_c$ obeys $1-2M/r_c-r_c^2/L^2=0$.  The inner photon sphere and ISCO also
retain their Schwarzschild radii, $3M$ and $6M$.  The layer can nevertheless
create additional orbit structure, and finite-$\ell$ QNMs remain a global
two-horizon problem; only the leading photon-sphere expectation is local
\cite{CardosoEtAl:2009geodesic}.

\subsection{Flat-limit-adapted bridge and compactification}

For the $M>0$ black-hole case, the production bridge adapted to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_f} is
\begin{equation}
 B_\chi(r)=-1+\frac{8M^2}{r^2}
 -\frac{8M^2}{r_c^2}\chi_L(r).
 \label{eq:exterior_bridge}
\end{equation}
It has $B_\chi(2M)=1$, $B_\chi(r_c)=-1$, and $-1<B_\chi<1$ between the
horizons; beyond $r_c$, $B_\chi<-1$.  The height satisfies
$h_\chi'=B_\chi/f_\chi$, with regular crossings verified in
Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}.  Above $L_\star/M=160$ the production
switch has fixed compact endpoints, but its contribution to the boost is
$\mathcal O(M^2/L^2)$ at fixed $r$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 B_\chi(r)=-1+\frac{8M^2}{r^2}
 \qquad(L\to\infty),
 \label{eq:exterior_bridge_flat_limit}
\end{equation}
which is the minimal-gauge Schwarzschild boost.  Likewise
$r^2\chi_L/L^2\to0$, so the
metric and bridge recover Schwarzschild at fixed radius even if $\chi_L$ has
a nonzero limiting profile.

Define
\begin{equation}
 D_L=1-\frac{2M}{r_c},
 \qquad
 \rho_\chi(r)=\frac{1-2M/r}{D_L}.
 \label{eq:exterior_compactification}
\end{equation}
This places $2M$ and $r_c$ at $\rho_\chi=0$ and $1$ and tends to
$1-2M/r$.  Choose
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_{\chi,L}=\frac{2M}{r},
 \qquad \Omega_{\chi,L}(1)=\frac{2M}{r_c}>0,
 \label{eq:exterior_conformal_factor}
\end{equation}
whose positive endpoint value vanishes in the Schwarzschild limit.

The principal endpoint coefficients recover Schwarzschild
(Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}).  Conformal coupling also recovers
$P_{\rm Schw}(1)$, whereas minimal coupling retains the lower-order offset of
uniform SdS because every member has an exact-SdS cap.  This nonuniformity
anticipates the tail sensitivity below.

With the same normalization $r_0=4M$, define
\begin{equation}
 q_\chi=\int_{r_0}^{r_c}\frac{1+B_\chi}{f_\chi}\,dr,
 \qquad U=\tau-q_\chi.
 \label{eq:exterior_clock}
\end{equation}
The endpoint is regular.  Comparison with the integrable Schwarzschild tail
gives
\begin{equation}
 q_\chi=q_0-\frac{8M^2}{r_c}
 +o\!\left(\frac{M^2}{r_c}\right)
 \longrightarrow q_0=4M\ln\frac{r_0}{r_0-2M}.
 \label{eq:exterior_clock_flat_limit}
\end{equation}
Thus the fixed-angle-width exterior-supported family shares the common
Schwarzschild clock used above.

\end{equation}
A nonlinear test must instead evolve a covariant conserved regulator.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Artificial cosmology is a controlled limit, not merely the addition of a
positive cosmological constant.  The metric, foliation, compactification,
data map, and waveform clock must approach the same asymptotically flat
problem.  Horizon regularity alone does not select it.  The minimal gauge
satisfies the adopted global and principal-coefficient criteria for the
matched Schwarzschild comparison.

With these structures matched, the uniform fixed-mode and localized multimode
sequences support extrapolated agreement with the Schwarzschild waveform at
the conservative $1\%$ level on their prompt-dominated intervals.  A separate
large-$L$ calculation recovers the Schwarzschild dipole Price-law rate over a
refinement-supported intermediate interval, without establishing the full
tail waveform or a later cosmological crossover.  The caustic scalings remain
consistency evidence rather than precision measurements.

The exterior-supported family leaves the strong-field metric exactly
Schwarzschild, and its conserved anisotropic layer reduces the quadrupole
ringdown-window error at every tested length.  The same profile generates
additional long-lived tail structure, showing that near-zone shielding does
not remove sensitivity to the outer scattering problem.  This proof of
principle is neither an optimized regulator nor a universal tail or
quasinormal-spectrum result.  The decisive next test is finite-$L$ nonlinear
Einstein evolution with a covariant conserved regulator, especially because
generic three-dimensional scri-fixed metric evolution remains open.

\subsection{Production ladders and residual refinement}

The uniform fixed-mode ladder is
$(N,\Delta\tau/M)=(384,0.005)$, $(512,0.00375)$, and $(768,0.0025)$,
evolved to $\tau=200M$ with boundary output every $0.03M$.  The localized
source ladder is
$(N_r,\Delta\tau/M,\ell_{\max})=(1024,0.001,42)$,
$(1536,1/1500,46)$, and $(2048,0.0005,50)$, evolved to $\tau=60M$ with output
at the stepping cadence and observers at $r=8M$, $r=12M$, and the outer
boundary.  Each finite-$L$ run has a separately evolved Schwarzschild control
at the same level.  Radial resolution, timestep, and, for the localized
source, angular truncation are refined together.

Let $R_q=X_q-Y_q$ be a residual waveform at refinement level $q$.  The direct
medium-to-fine change is
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{mf}=
 \frac{\|R_{\rm medium}-R_{\rm fine}\|}
 {\|W_{{\rm Schw},{\rm fine}}\|},
 \label{eq:paired_refinement_change}
\end{equation}
using the fixed-mode or Parseval norm as appropriate.  It is not
$|E_{2,\rm medium}-E_{2,\rm fine}|$.  Coarse-to-medium and medium-to-fine
changes determine the observed coupled order and Richardson estimate.  We use
the effective ladder parameter $h_q^{\rm eff}=N_q^{-1}$, which indexes the
joint refinement of radial resolution and timestep---and of $\ell_{\max}$ for
the sourced runs---rather than a pure spatial mesh width.  For normalized
successive changes $d_{cm}$ and $d_{mf}$, the coupled order $p$ solves
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d_{cm}}{d_{mf}}
 =\frac{(h_c^{\rm eff})^p-(h_m^{\rm eff})^p}
 {(h_m^{\rm eff})^p-(h_f^{\rm eff})^p},
 \label{eq:coupled_refinement_order}
\end{equation}
using the actual, generally unequal resolution ratios.  The corresponding
fine-grid scale is $d_{mf}/[(N_f/N_m)^p-1]$.  Direct thresholds retain the
larger of $d_{mf}$ and this scale.  If an exterior ladder is nonmonotone, no
Richardson order is assigned and the larger successive change is used
instead.

All numerical values in this appendix come from $\xi=0$ evolutions, and every
waveform comparison uses all three levels of its stated ladder.

The exterior-supported comparison applies the same paired-residual principle
separately to the uniform and exterior families.  Its conservative scale is
defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:exterior-tests}.  The ringdown-window exterior ladders are
monotone for $L/M=80$, $160$, and $320$; the $L/M=640$ ladder is not, so no
Richardson order is assigned there.  For these runs, the reduction constraint
and its stored maximum norm are
\begin{align*}
 C_j(\tau)&=\psi_j(\tau)-[\partial_\rho u(\tau)]_j,\\
 \|C\|_{\infty,{\rm stored}}
 &:=\max_{\tau\in\mathcal T_{\rm stored}}\max_j|C_j(\tau)|.
\end{align*}
Here $j$ runs over the base-grid Chebyshev collocation points.  For these
$100M$ runs, $\mathcal T_{\rm stored}$ contains the initial and terminal
records; some archives contain a numerically duplicated terminal sample.
This is an absolute, unnormalized diagnostic evaluated only at those
snapshots, not over the continuous time history.  In coarse, medium, and fine
order it is $(7.54\times10^{-4},2.71\times10^{-4},2.14\times10^{-3})$ for
$L/M=320$ and
$(1.18\times10^{-3},2.87\times10^{-3},6.10\times10^{-4})$ for $L/M=640$.
The overall maximum occurs in the medium $L/M=640$ run.  Because neither
sequence decreases monotonically, this quantity is a stability guard rather
than a convergence measure.  The archives do not retain a corresponding
pointwise derivative-field scale, so we assign no relative normalization.
All background, representation, and stability guards pass.  The
family-specific scales used in the comparison are given explicitly in
Table~\ref{tab:exterior-qnm-improvement}.

For the fixed-mode family the largest cumulative refinement change is
$0.1478\%$ at $L/M=320$ and $0.0465\%$ at $L/M=640$, with Richardson
estimates $0.0138\%$ and $0.0035\%$.  Derived extrapolants inherit the
refinement of all three members, which is why their observed changes reach
$0.409\%$ while their fine-grid residuals stay near $0.03\%$.
On the disjoint intervals refinement dominates: fine-grid residuals of $0.096$
to $0.211\%$ on $40\leq U/M\leq80$ carry refinement changes of $1.02$ to
$3.14\%$, and on $80\leq U/M\leq160$ refinement exceeds the residual
outright; we therefore treat those intervals only as diagnostics, not as
late-time accuracy statements.  For the localized source family the
observed refinement changes stay at the $10^{-4}\%$ level on the archived
interval.

The standalone Schwarzschild check compares complete archived fields with a
sphere and time integrated Parseval norm.  Relative to the fine
$(N_r,\Delta\tau/M,\ell_{\max})=(2048,0.0005,50)$ archive, the coarse and
medium ladder differences are $3.44\times10^{-7}$ and
$7.66\times10^{-8}$.  Truncating the fine response itself isolates angular
omission from radial and temporal error; the relative omitted-field norms are
$2.29\times10^{-9}$ at $\ell_{\max}=42$ and $1.26\times10^{-10}$ at
$\ell_{\max}=46$.  The largest stored reduction constraint is
$1.80\times10^{-11}$.

A separate minimally coupled sourced calculation compares the two
implementations at $L/M=80$ on Schwarzschild and SdS.  It is a validation data
set, not part of either production sequence.  Across these archives, the
largest sphere-integrated relative difference is $5.58\times10^{-4}$ and the

\subsection{Caustic timing audit}

We include a timing measurement in a scaling fit only when all four
matched-template lag optimizations avoid their $\pm4M$ bounds and $|D_1|$ is
at least three times its combined fixed-source deterministic sensitivity.
This rule excludes both local $L/M=640$ values, which remain as open markers
in Fig.~\ref{fig:d1}.  The separate $1M$ null-geodesic residual threshold
applies only to the $L/M=12$ phase pairs.

The expected local and outer powers are tested with
\begin{align}
 \frac{D_{1,\mathrm{local}}}{M}
 &=a_2\left(\frac{M}{L}\right)^2
 +a_4\left(\frac{M}{L}\right)^4,\notag\\
 \frac{D_{1,\mathrm{outer}}}{M}

 \label{eq:d1_scaling_models}

\subsection{Caustic timing definition}

At a fixed extraction radius, let $U_{\rm dir}$ be the direct-pulse arrival
reconstructed at $\gamma=0$ and $U_{\rm echo}$ the first caustic-echo arrival
at $\gamma=\pi$.  The primary estimator is the matched-template lag against
the corresponding Schwarzschild pulse.  We define
\begin{equation}
 D_1(r;L)=
 \left(U_{\rm echo}-U_{\rm dir}\right)_{\mathrm{SdS},L}
 -\left(U_{\rm echo}-U_{\rm dir}\right)_{\mathrm{Schw}},
 \label{eq:d1_definition}
\end{equation}
so a background-independent clock offset cancels.  Appendix
\ref{app:convergence} gives the selection rule and scaling models.

\subsection{Finite-radius crossover protocol}

The main text quotes the $\xi=0$, $r=8M$ transition because it is the cleanest
supporting case.  This separate crossover study treats the $\ell=1$ response
for $L/M=20$, $40$, $80$, and $160$ at $r=4M$, $8M$, $16M$, and $\hc$.
Finite-radius observations are compared with a separate Schwarzschild
run at the same areal radius, while $\hc$ is compared with Schwarzschild
$\scri^+$.  An additional $\ell=2$, $L/M=80$ calculation checks the
mode dependence.

Departure is the end of the last persistent interval, before cosmological
entry, on which the local decay rate agrees with the Schwarzschild reference.
Entry is the first persistent interval on which the rate agrees with the
expected minimally coupled SdS value.  The rate is constructed from a
centered root-mean-square
envelope and a cubic Savitzky--Golay filter.  We repeat the extraction for
$54$ combinations of smoothing width, root-mean-square fraction, persistence
interval, and tolerance.  A setting resolves a transition only when it finds
both endpoints.  At the aggregate level, a case is called resolved when at
least $27$ settings do so and marginal when between $1$ and $26$ do so.  If
none resolves, the case is reported by the missing endpoint, such as ``no
cosmological entry.''  Quoted times are medians over the resolving settings,
with the full sweep range as a systematic interval.

At $r=8M$, the entry medians are $\kappa_cU=2.83$ for $L/M=80$ and $2.94$
for $L/M=160$, with sweep ranges $[2.56,3.07]$ and $[2.68,3.19]$.
They agree to $4\%$ in cosmological units while the corresponding geometric
times differ by a factor of $2.05$.  The departure medians, $115.9M$ and
$133.5M$, are instead approximately fixed in black-hole units; their
dimensionless ranges are $[1.15,1.76]$ and $[0.71,0.96]$ in $\kappa_cU$.
The transition interval consequently widens from approximately $117M$ to
$343M$.  With only two clean lengths, this contrast is consistency evidence
for the two physical scales and not a fitted scaling law.

The shorter-length cases show why the aggregate classification matters.  At
$L/M=20$ no observer enters a resolved cosmological regime.  At $L/M=40$ the
$r=4M$ case has no cosmological entry, the $r=8M$ and $r=16M$ cases are
marginal with respectively $1/54$ and $18/54$ resolving settings, while the
cosmological-horizon case is resolved at the threshold $27/54$.

The Schwarzschild reference does not settle within $5\%$ of its own
$\ell=1$ Price-law value until $U=220.3M$ at $r=8M$ and $167.3M$ at $\scri^+$,
both later than the corresponding finite-$L$ departures.  For $\ell=2$ the
Price-law value is not attained at $r=4M$ within the evolved interval.  Thus the
tested $L/M\leq160$ solutions leave the Schwarzschild waveform before a clean
Schwarzschild power-law regime is established.  This supplies physical
context for the short-length comparison interval; the production disjoint
windows in Sec.~\ref{sec:direct_recovery} are labeled diagnostic for the
separate numerical reason that their refinement change is not subdominant.

\subsection{Shared-flux exterior reduction}

The long exterior-tail runs use an algebraically equivalent conservative
characteristic reduction.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf h:=\pi+\psi,\qquad \mathsf j:=\pi-\psi,
 \label{eq:conservative_characteristics}
\end{equation}
and the shared fluxes
\begin{equation}
 F_+:=A(1+B)\mathsf h,\qquad
 F_-:=A(1-B)\mathsf j.
 \label{eq:conservative_fluxes}
\end{equation}
The evolution system becomes
\begin{align}
 \partial_\tau u&=\tfrac12(F_++F_-),\notag\\
 \partial_\tau\mathsf h&=\partial_\rho F_+-P_{\ell,\xi}u,\notag\\
 \partial_\tau\mathsf j&=-\partial_\rho F_--P_{\ell,\xi}u.
 \label{eq:conservative_characteristic_system}
\end{align}
Near the endpoints we retain the null factors as
$A(1+B)=(1-\rho)\alpha_+$ and $A(1-B)=\rho\alpha_-$, with finite
$\alpha_\pm$.  Reusing the same discrete fluxes in all three equations
preserves
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm ch}:=\tfrac12(\mathsf h-\mathsf j)-\partial_\rho u
 \label{eq:conservative_constraint}
\end{equation}
at the semidiscrete level.  No damping is added, and $C_{\rm ch}=C$ in the
continuum.  Schwarzschild and uniform SdS tail controls use
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:evolve_u}--\eqref{eq:evolve_pi}; exterior-tail production uses
Eq.~\eqref{eq:conservative_characteristic_system}.

\subsection{Exterior-tail formulation check}
\label{app:tail-formulation-check}

The standard and shared-flux reductions are algebraically equivalent at the
continuum level but differ after semidiscretization.  We therefore compare
their waveforms on the same exterior background rather than relying on the
continuum transformation alone.  A direct short regression at $L/M=80$
agrees in absolute waveform amplitude to better than $10^{-7}$.  The
independent long calculation uses
endpoint-factored fields
\begin{equation}
 H=\alpha_+(\pi+\psi),\qquad
 J=\alpha_-(\pi-\psi),
 \label{eq:factored_characteristics}
\end{equation}
so that the physical fluxes are $(1-\rho)H$ and $\rho J$.  It damps only the
continuum reduction constraint, for which
$\partial_\tau C=-M^{-1}C$, and evolves the same scalar equation, geometry,
initial data, clock, and output times as the shared-flux production system.

For $L/M=640$, $\ell=1$, and $\xi=0$, both formulations were evolved through
$U/M=1000$ at $N=1536$ and $2048$ with
$\Delta\tau/M=0.0025$.  At $N=2048$, their outer-boundary relative
$L^2$ differences range from $0.62\%$ to $2.65\%$ over six late-signal audit
windows between $U/M=100$ and $950$; the small late amplitude makes these
windowed comparisons establish only percent-level waveform agreement.
Nevertheless,
the endpoint-factored and shared-flux systems give longest
$p_{\rm eff}\simeq3$ intervals of $12.15M$ and $11.90M$, respectively, so
both fail the required $40M$ persistence test and identify the same
qualitative slow component.  The endpoint-factored value
$p_{\rm eff}=1.176$ is a median local index, whereas $p=1.086$ is the slope of
a fixed $120$--$250M$ log--log fit to the shared-flux waveform
($R^2=0.9926$); they are distinct estimators.  This independent formulation
supports the tail classification, not a more accurate late-time waveform.
The three-grid and halved-timestep ladders set the production accuracy.

\subsection{Matched coupling and exterior protocol}

The regulator question concerns radiation at the asymptotic end.  The
matched $L/M=640$ comparison therefore uses an $\ell=1$ waveform at $\hc$ for
uniform and exterior-supported SdS and compares it with the same Schwarzschild
waveform at $\scri^+$.  The matched calculation uses $L/M=640$ and extends
through $U/M=1000$ for both $\xi=0$ and $1/6$.  This test complements the
finite-radius crossover above: it asks whether a finite cosmological boundary
can reproduce even a short interval of the Schwarzschild null-infinity Price
law before its own asymptotics take over.

From a centered root-mean-square envelope $A(U)$ we form the local power index
$p_{\rm eff}=-d\ln A/d\ln U$.  A candidate Schwarzschild-like interval must
remain within $10\%$ of the null-infinity dipole value $p=3$ for at least
$40M$.  A candidate subsequent exponential regime is measured with
$\gamma_{\rm eff}=-d\ln A/dU$.  For uniform SdS, the large-$L$ reference rates are
$\gamma_{\rm eff}/\kappa_c\simeq1$ for $\xi=0$ and $2$ for $\xi=1/6$.
The uniform rate must remain within $10\%$ of its target for at least the
semilog fit width $\Delta(\kappa_cU)=0.25$.  No target is imposed on the
exterior-supported geometry; its late signal is analyzed without assigning
an asymptotic decay law.
Each claimed interval lies above the time-dependent numerical floor and
persists under an envelope- and fit-window sweep.  Plotted indices require an
envelope above ten times the measured spatial or temporal refinement floor,
agreement within $0.1$ between the $N=2048$ and $3072$ indices, and agreement
within $0.1$ between full- and half-step $N=2048$ indices; neighborhoods of
signed zero crossings are omitted.  Three spatial resolutions
and a fixed-resolution halved-timestep run test the ordering and duration of
the intervals.  The primary estimator uses a $10M$ envelope and $40M$ local
fit.  We also combine envelope widths $5M$, $10M$, and $20M$ with fit widths
$30M$, $40M$, and $60M$.  Across these nine choices, the Schwarzschild
candidate lasts $815.4$--$841.9M$ and the uniform conformal candidate lasts
$131.1$--$148.1M$; both pass.  The longest candidates in uniform minimal,
exterior minimal, and exterior conformal SdS last at most $10.2M$, $12.0M$,
and $11.3M$, respectively, so all three fail.  The classification is therefore
insensitive to the estimator widths.

On the resolved $N=3072$ exterior envelopes, unweighted least-squares fits of
$\ln A$ against $\ln U$ give $A\sim U^{-1.086}$ ($R^2=0.9926$) for $\xi=0$
on $120\leq U/M\leq250$ and $A\sim U^{-0.920}$ ($R^2=0.9911$) for $\xi=1/6$
on $160\leq U/M\leq400$.  These finite-interval exponents describe the chosen
transition profile and are not universal tail indices.

For uniform conformal SdS, the relative waveform changes on the accepted
$153.3$--$294.8M$ interval are $7.90\times10^{-5}$ from $N=1536$ to $2048$
and $7.97\times10^{-6}$ from $N=2048$ to $3072$; halving the timestep at

family, the three spatial grids use $N=1536$, $2048$, and $3072$ with
$\Delta\tau/M=0.0025$, supplemented by an $N=2048$ run at half that timestep.
On the successive $300$--$500M$, $500$--$750M$, and $750$--$950M$ windows,
the $N=2048$--$3072$ relative changes are $2.41\times10^{-6}$,
$3.80\times10^{-6}$, and $2.97\times10^{-5}$ for $\xi=0$, and
$4.28\times10^{-5}$, $3.15\times10^{-5}$, and $1.34\times10^{-4}$ for
$\xi=1/6$.  The preceding spatial differences are larger by factors of about
$10$--$11$ and $7.4$--$7.6$, respectively.  Halving the timestep changes
these signals by only $3$--$4\times10^{-8}$, and the largest stored exterior
reduction-constraint norm is $1.09\times10^{-8}$.

\section{Exterior profile, implementation details, and reproducibility}

\subsection{Production switch and profile diagnostics}
The numerical family follows the horizon-scaled prescription through
$L_\star/M=160$ and then imposes a common minimum width in Chebyshev angle on
the transition and exact-SdS cap.  This leaves the $L/M=80$ and $160$ members
unchanged and prevents either outer region from collapsing onto the endpoint.
With the compactification~\eqref{eq:exterior_compactification}, define
$\vartheta(r)=\arccos[2\rho_\chi(r)-1]$ on $2M\leq r\leq r_c$; Chebyshev
collocation points are uniformly spaced in $\vartheta$.

With $\beta=9/10$, first define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat R_1(L)&=\beta r_c,\\
 \widehat\rho_{\chi,1}(L)&=
 \frac{1-2M/\widehat R_1}{D_L},\\
 \widehat\vartheta_1(L)&=
 \arccos\!\left(2\widehat\rho_{\chi,1}-1\right).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:horizon_scaled_transition}
\end{equation}
The final endpoints are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{L_\star}{M}&=160,\\
 \vartheta_\star&=\widehat\vartheta_1(L_\star)
 =0.07526440137447271,\\
 \vartheta_1(L)&=
 \max\{\widehat\vartheta_1(L),\vartheta_\star\},\\
 \vartheta_0(L)&=2\vartheta_1(L),\\
 \rho_{\chi,i}(L)&=\frac{1+\cos\vartheta_i(L)}{2},\\
 R_i(L)&=\frac{2M}{1-D_L\rho_{\chi,i}(L)}
 \quad(i=0,1).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:transition_radii}
\end{equation}
Thus $2M<R_0<R_1<r_c$.  For $L\leq L_\star$, the floor is inactive,
$R_1=\beta r_c$, and the transition and cap have equal angle widths,
$\vartheta_0-\vartheta_1=\vartheta_1$.  For $L>L_\star$, both widths equal
$\vartheta_\star$ and both compact endpoints are fixed:
\begin{align}
 \rho_{\chi,0}^\star&=0.9943459581991452,\notag\\
 \rho_{\chi,1}^\star&=0.9985844858695327,\notag\\
 \Delta\rho_{\rm tr}
 &=\rho_{\chi,1}^\star-\rho_{\chi,0}^\star
 =0.004238527670387571,\notag\\
 \Delta\rho_{\rm cap}
 &=1-\rho_{\chi,1}^\star
 =0.001415514130467255.
 \label{eq:transition_compact_widths}
\end{align}

The production switch is Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_switch} with
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_L(r)=\frac{\vartheta_0-\vartheta(r)}
 {\vartheta_0-\vartheta_1}.
 \label{eq:production_exterior_switch}
\end{equation}
It gives equal nonvanishing widths to the transition and exact-SdS cap in
$\vartheta$.  Their compact endpoints approach the fixed values above, with
the areal limits
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{R_{0,\infty}}{M}
 &=\frac{2}{1-\rho_{\chi,0}^\star}
 =353.729256069105,\\
 \frac{R_{1,\infty}}{M}
 &=\frac{2}{1-\rho_{\chi,1}^\star}
 =1412.914189235122,\\
 \frac{R_0}{r_c}&\longrightarrow0,
 &\frac{R_1}{r_c}&\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:transition_radii_limit}
\end{equation}
The exact-SdS cap still ends at the cosmological horizon, but its inner edge
does not remain at a fixed fraction of that horizon.  The transition begins
and ends at finite, distant radii in the flat limit, while the metric
correction there vanishes as $L^{-2}$.  The entire strong-field region is
exactly Schwarzschild throughout the production sequence, for which
$R_0>6M$.

A useful unexpanded diagnostic is
\begin{equation}
 R_{ab}R^{ab}
 =2\left(\Lambda_{\parallel}^2+\Lambda_{\perp}^2\right),
 \label{eq:exterior_ricci_squared}
\end{equation}
and the Kretschmann scalar is
\begin{equation}
 R_{abcd}R^{abcd}=f_\chi''{}^2
 +4\left(\frac{f_\chi'}{r}\right)^2
 +4\left(\frac{1-f_\chi}{r^2}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:exterior_kretschmann}
\end{equation}

Because $R_1$ tends to a finite areal radius in the final family,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Delta m_{\rm tr}}{M}
 \sim\frac12\left(\frac{R_{1,\infty}}{M}\right)^3
 \left(\frac{M}{L}\right)^2
 \longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:transition_mass_fixed_angle_family}
\end{equation}

For a generic layer of areal width $\Delta=R_1-R_0$,
\begin{equation}
 \lambda'=\mathcal O\!\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\Delta}\right),
 \qquad
 \lambda''=\mathcal O\!\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\Delta^2}\right).
 \label{eq:transition_derivative_scales}
\end{equation}
A narrow areal layer can generate large curvature and a sharp scattering
feature.  Here both endpoints have finite limits and
$\Delta\to1059.184933166017M$.  The limiting profile therefore has
$\chi_L'=\mathcal O(M^{-1})$ and
$\chi_L''=\mathcal O(M^{-2})$.  At fixed $M$, the strict large-$L$ scalings
of $\lambda=3\chi_L/L^2$ are
\begin{equation}
 \lambda'=\mathcal O(L^{-2}M^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \lambda''=\mathcal O(L^{-2}M^{-2}).
 \label{eq:transition_width_floored_derivative_scales}
\end{equation}
The combinations entering the source eigenvalues and Ricci curvature are
$\mathcal O(L^{-2})$.  The construction therefore replaces
distributed strong-field bias by a smooth, low-curvature modification in a
distant region.  The lapse change near the cosmological horizon remains order
unity, while the transition retains a fixed spectral width.




\subsection{Endpoint coefficients and clock asymptotics}

The coefficients used in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:evolve_u}--\eqref{eq:evolve_pi} are
obtained without numerical differentiation of the compactification:
\begin{align}
 G_\chi&=\frac{2M}{D_Lr^2},&
 A_\chi&=\frac{f_\chi G_\chi}{1-B_\chi^2},\notag\\
 P^\chi_{\ell,\xi}&=\frac{D_L}{2M}
 \left[\ell(\ell+1)+rf_\chi'+\xi r^2R_\chi\right].
 \label{eq:exterior_scalar_coefficients}
\end{align}
Their removable horizon limits are assigned analytically, as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:A_endpoint}.
In the exact-SdS cap, the horizon equation gives
$D_L=r_c^2/L^2=1-2M/r_c$.  The outer endpoint values are therefore
\begin{align}
 A_\chi(1)&=\frac{r_c-3M}{8M(r_c-2M)},&
 c_{+,\chi}(1)&=2A_\chi(1),\notag\\
 P^\chi_{\ell,\xi}(1)&=\frac{D_L}{2M}
 \left[\ell(\ell+1)-2+\frac{6M}{r_c}\right.\notag\\
 &\left.\hspace{7em}+12\xi D_L\right].
 \label{eq:exterior_outer_endpoint_coefficients}
\end{align}
Hence
\begin{align}
 A_\chi(1)&\longrightarrow\frac{1}{8M},&
 c_{+,\chi}(1)&\longrightarrow\frac{1}{4M},\notag\\
 P^\chi_{\ell,0}(1)&\longrightarrow
 \frac{\ell(\ell+1)-2}{2M},&
 P^\chi_{\ell,1/6}(1)&\longrightarrow
 \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2M}.
 \label{eq:exterior_outer_endpoint_flat_limit}
\end{align}

The exterior bridge's horizon crossings are regular; for example,
\begin{align}
 \lim_{r\to2M^+}\frac{1-B_\chi^2}{f_\chi}&=8,\notag\\
 \lim_{r\to r_c^-}\frac{1-B_\chi^2}{f_\chi}
 &=\frac{16M^2}{r_c(r_c-3M)}>0.
 \label{eq:exterior_bridge_regular_limits}
\end{align}

The clock endpoint is also regular:
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{r\to r_c}\frac{1+B_\chi}{f_\chi}
 =\frac{B_\chi'(r_c)}{f_\chi'(r_c)}
 =\frac{8M^2}{r_c(r_c-3M)}.
 \label{eq:exterior_clock_endpoint}
\end{equation}
To obtain its flat limit, choose fixed $R>R_{1,\infty}$.  For sufficiently
large $L$, the transition lies below $R$ and $[R,r_c]$ is exactly SdS.  On
$[r_0,R]$, the integrand in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_clock} approaches its
Schwarzschild value with $\mathcal O(L^{-2})$ corrections.  In the exact cap,
the cancellation-free expression is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1+B_\chi}{f_\chi}
 =\frac{8M^2(r_c+r)}{r^2r_c^2
 \left[(r_c+r)/L^2-2M/(rr_c)\right]}.
 \label{eq:exterior_clock_cap}
\end{equation}
Comparison with the integrable Schwarzschild tail gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_clock_flat_limit}.

\subsection{Exterior ringdown implementation and audit}

The minimally coupled comparison uses RK222 with $3/2$ dealiasing and stores
the waveform every $0.03M$.  The coarse, medium, and fine spatial resolutions
are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 L/M=80:&\quad(384,512,768),\\
 L/M=160:&\quad(768,1024,1536),\\
 L/M=320:&\quad(1024,1536,2048),\\
 L/M=640:&\quad(768,1024,1536).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exterior_resolution_ladders}
\end{equation}
At every $L$, the corresponding timesteps are $0.005M$, $0.00375M$, and
$0.0025M$, matching the control ladder.  The bounded zeroth-order potential
is explicit in the IMEX split, while transport is implicit.  Since each
ladder refines space and time together, its numerical scale includes both
effects; no separate fixed-resolution timestep check is used to reduce it.

The angle floor is active at $L/M=320$ and $640$, where the audited transition
radii are
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 L/M & R_0/M & R_1/M\\ \hline
 320 &168.2329417&260.5413505\\
 640 &228.1477989&440.4330107
\end{array}
 \label{eq:fixed_angle_transition_radii}
\end{equation}
At $L/M=320$, the fine grid places $49$ Chebyshev points in each of the
transition and cap; at $L/M=640$, it places $37$ in each.  The minimally
coupled $L/M=640$ potential-representation error is
$3.47\times10^{-6}$ relative to the analytic transition minimum.

For either coupling, the scalar potential is
\begin{equation}
 V_{\ell,\xi}^\chi=f_\chi\left[
 \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}+\frac{f_\chi'}{r}+\xi R_\chi\right].
 \label{eq:exterior_scalar_potential}
\end{equation}
Using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exterior_metric} and
\eqref{eq:exterior_ricci_scalar}, the conformally coupled specialization is
\begin{equation}
 V_{\ell,1/6}^\chi=f_\chi\left[
 \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}+\frac{2M}{r^3}
 +\frac{r\lambda'}{9}+\frac{r^2\lambda''}{18}\right].
 \label{eq:exterior_conformal_scalar_potential}
\end{equation}
It equals the Schwarzschild potential inside $R_0$ and the corresponding SdS
potential outside $R_1$.  Its derivative terms require an independent
resolution audit for $\xi=1/6$.

The metric bounds in Sec.~\ref{sec:exterior-supported} exclude unintended
roots.  The numerical preflight checks $f_\chi>0$, $|B_\chi|<1$, finite
coefficients, transition and cap point counts, potential resolution, and
nonfinite or order-of-magnitude growth.  The $\xi=0$ sequence additionally
requires a nonnegative represented potential.  These guards detect failure;
the three-level waveform comparisons determine accuracy.  Stored constraint
maxima sample available snapshots rather than a continuous history.

The prompt-inclusive $0$--$80M$ audit gives reductions of $18.0\%$, $14.1\%$,
$10.5\%$, and $7.35\%$ as $L/M$ increases.  In the disjoint $40$--$80M$
window, the improvement is unresolved at $L/M=320$ and $640$; the calculation
therefore supports no late-time claim.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

The reproducibility package keeps raw simulations separate from derived
tables and figures.  Machine-readable manifests record each physical
configuration and resolution, link every reported artifact to its inputs,
verify checksums, and confirm that postprocessing leaves raw data unchanged.
The records fix the mass normalization, foliation, compactification, data or
source prescription, waveform clock, and analysis interval.  They cover the
uniform finite-time and large-$L$ Price-law ladders, localized-source,
exterior-ringdown, matched $L/M=640$ tail, cross-implementation, and
independent-formulation checks.

The public release includes the code, raw and derived data, analysis scripts,
and foliation and late-time diagnostics.  Compact encoding of the localized
responses introduces at most a $10^{-7}$ relative peak-reconstruction error,
which is negligible for the percent-level comparisons.
