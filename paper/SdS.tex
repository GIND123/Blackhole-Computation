Schwarzschild--de Sitter scalar waves}

\author{Govind Arun Kumar}
\email{govind02@umd.edu}
\affiliation{Institute for Physical Science and Technology, University of

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Misner proposed using a small positive cosmological constant to regulate the
formally singular terms introduced by conformal compactification at future
null infinity.  We test this idea for scalar waves on black-hole backgrounds.
Our Schwarzschild--de Sitter families share a controlled Schwarzschild limit,
which reveals that regularity at the cosmological horizon is not enough: the
foliation must also have suitable asymptotic behavior.  In fixed-background
scalar evolutions, both single-mode and localized-source waveforms approach a
separately evolved Schwarzschild reference as the cosmological length grows;
on the tested finite-time intervals, extrapolated differences support a
conservative $1\%$ bound.  An exterior-supported family leaves the
strong-field region unchanged and confines the cosmological modification to
the outer region.  At fixed
cosmological length, it reduces ringdown-window waveform errors by roughly
$8$--$30\%$ relative to uniform Schwarzschild--de Sitter.  Late-time tails
expose its limitation: an intermediate Schwarzschild decay
appears in the uniform conformally coupled case, whereas the exterior
transition produces additional scattering and does not recover the same tail.
Artificial cosmology is therefore a promising finite-time waveform regulator,
especially for ringdown, but it cannot reproduce every observable that probes
the asymptotic structure.  Its application to the nonlinear Einstein equations
is the next test.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Radiation from isolated systems has a clean definition at future null
infinity, $\scri^+$.  Hyperboloidal evolution brings this boundary onto the
computational domain through spacelike slices that approach outgoing null
hypersurfaces.  Compactification places infinity at a finite characteristic
outflow boundary.  The harder task is to evolve the nonlinear Einstein
equations there with regular, well-conditioned variables.  Misner proposed
regulating their formally singular conformal terms with a small positive
cosmological constant
\cite{misner_hyperboloidal_2006,misner_over_2006,misner_excising_2006}.
At finite cosmological length $L$, a cosmological horizon replaces the
$\Omega=0$ endpoint by one with $\Omega_L>0$, but also introduces a physical
bias.  A finite-$L$ family is useful only if its metric, foliation,
compactification, data map, and waveform clock approach the same
asymptotically flat problem.  Regular horizon crossing alone does not select
the asymptotic Schwarzschild end.

Characteristic evolution and Cauchy--characteristic matching provide one
route to the asymptotic boundary \cite{Winicour:2012characteristic}; recent
matching calculations cover fully nonlinear binary-black-hole mergers and
their late-time tails \cite{MaEtAl:2025CCM}.  Friedrich's conformal field
equations instead enlarge the regular variable set
\cite{friedrich1983cauchy}.  Early three-dimensional and axisymmetric
calculations achieved global evolutions but exposed costly constraint
systems, gauge sensitivity, and loss of resolution as a moving $\scri^+$
contracted the physical grid
\cite{Frauendiener:1998II,Hubner:1999scheme,Hubner:2001timelike,
FrauendienerHein:2002,Husa:2002problems}.  The generalized conformal field
equations use a Weyl connection and conformal geodesics
\cite{Friedrich:1995general,Friedrich:2003geodesics} and have enabled
nonlinear black-hole scattering
\cite{BeyerEtAl:2017GCFE,FrauendienerStevens:2021massloss,
FrauendienerStevensThwala:2025}.  Their current initial-boundary framework
does not fix $\scri^+$ spatially and still faces late-time compactification
losses \cite{CamdenEtAl:2025,FrauendienerStevens:2023compactification}.

Scri fixing prevents this moving-boundary loss of resolution by anchoring
$\scri^+$ at a fixed spatial boundary.  On fixed black-hole backgrounds, it
also removes artificial outer data and finite-radius waveform extrapolation
\cite{zenginoglu_hyperboloidal_2008,Zenginoglu:2008pw}.  Time-domain
Schwarzschild and Kerr calculations established its accuracy and efficiency,
including long evolutions that resolve power-law tails
\cite{zengin2008hyperboloidal,ZenginogluNunezHusa:2009,
ZenginogluTiglio:2009,RaczToth:2011,Bernuzzi:2011aj,
ZenginogluKhanna:2011,Harms:2014dqa}.  Frequency-domain formulations later
used the same horizon-to-$\scri^+$ regularity for spectral decompositions and
self-force calculations
\cite{AnsorgMacedo:2016,Macedo:2020,MacedoEtAl:2022}.  This record establishes
scri fixing as an effective numerical gauge, especially for long evolutions.
We therefore require the finite-$L$ family to approach a scri-fixed
Schwarzschild gauge.  Geroch limits of SdS provide related geometric context
\cite{ZhouPanossoMacedo:2025}.

Model fields test asymptotic infrastructure before it is coupled to gravity.
Nonsymmetric scalar fields have been evolved with fixed $\scri^+$ on
Minkowski and Schwarzschild backgrounds \cite{ZenginogluKidder:2010}, and
recent three-dimensional calculations treat nonlinear wave models on
compactified hyperboloids
\cite{Peterson:2023bha,Rinne:2025nonlinear,
ZenginogluBernuzziNutzi:2026}.

Prescribed-conformal-factor Einstein formulations retain familiar metric
variables and regularize formally singular terms through constraints and gauge
\cite{zenginoglu_hyperboloidal_2008,Zenginoglu:2008pw}.  Nonlinear conformal
BSSN/Z4 implementations include spherical self-gravitating scalar fields,
vacuum tests, and charged collapse
\cite{Vano-Vinuales:2014koa,Vano-Vinuales:2017qij,
VanoVinuales:2024reference,Alvares:2025emkg}.  Constrained CMC formulations
derive regular boundary values from constraints and have reached twist-free
axisymmetry and spherical Einstein--matter systems
\cite{Moncrief:2008ie,Rinne:2009qx,RinneMoncrief:2013}, supported by
three-dimensional black-hole initial data and a general constrained tetrad
system \cite{BuchmanPfeifferBardeen:2009,BardeenSarbachBuchman:2011}.

Dual-foliation generalized harmonic evolution separates strong-field
coordinates from a compactified target system
\cite{Hilditch:2015aba,HilditchEtAl:2018,DuarteEtAl:2023} and has advanced
from three-dimensional model problems to full Einstein evolution in spherical
symmetry \cite{Peterson:2024spherical,Peterson:2025strong}.  N\"utzi's
symmetric-hyperbolic vacuum system fixes null infinity for nonlinear small
data, with compatible constraint solutions
\cite{Nutzi:2023,Nutzi:2025,Nutzi:2026}.  These advances leave open a robust,
generic three-dimensional metric free-evolution code that fixes $\scri^+$ at
the outer boundary and supports binary black holes.  Misner's conformally
invariant Maxwell calculation on de Sitter established an early
fixed-background test of artificial cosmology; the black-hole case adds
strong-field scattering and a second horizon.

Scalar waves on Schwarzschild--de Sitter (SdS) provide an analogous
black-hole scattering problem between the future black-hole horizon $\hb$ and
the future cosmological horizon $\hc$.  We compare minimal and conformal
curvature coupling, $\xi=0$ and $1/6$, within the family analyzed by Brady
et al.~\cite{brady_radiative_1999}.  The comparison separates regulator
behavior shared by both equations from effects that depend on the asymptotic
field equation.

For $\Lambda>0$, the future conformal boundary is spacelike.  Its asymptotic
symmetries and radiative degrees of freedom differ from those at
$\scri^+$, as do the associated fluxes
\cite{Ashtekar:2014zfa,Ashtekar:2015lla,Ashtekar:2015lxa}.  We restrict the
fixed-background question: how closely a finite cosmological calculation
approximates a specified asymptotically flat solution.  The bridge
continuation beyond $\hc$ determines the asymptotic end, so we track the
limiting coordinates, clock, and compactified evolution coefficients together.

Writing $\Lambda=3/L^2$, the SdS metric approaches Schwarzschild on every
fixed near-zone interval as $L/M\to\infty$.  Each finite value of $L$ keeps
the SdS global causal structure.  Schwarzschild fields develop inverse-power
tails
\cite{price1972nonspherical,zengin2008hyperboloidal,
Angelopoulos:2016wcv}.  SdS fields eventually display cosmological decay
whose rate depends on $\xi$
\cite{Brady:1996za,brady_radiative_1999,Molina:2003dc}.  In the large-horizon
limit, Brady et al. find the decay rate
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\gamma_{\ell,\xi}}{\kappa_c}
 =\ell+\frac32-\frac12\sqrt{9-48\xi}
 +\mathcal O(M/L),
 \label{eq:intro_brady_exponent}
\end{equation}
so the leading $\ell=1$ values are
$\gamma_{1,0}/\kappa_c=1$ and
$\gamma_{1,1/6}/\kappa_c=2$.
A finite-$L$
calculation approximates the asymptotically flat waveform over a finite
interval.  Cosmological decay sets its later behavior.

Using the bridge geometry of Ref.~\cite{zenginoglu_bridge_2025}, we first test
whether a one-parameter background family has a controlled global
Schwarzschild limit.  We then compare fixed-mode and localized-source scalar
waveforms with a separately evolved Schwarzschild reference.  We evolve
angle-dependent scalar fields in a spherical-harmonic representation;
spherical symmetry diagonalizes the angular operator, so the production
calculation reduces exactly to independent radial modal equations.  The
minimal-coupling sequence supports a conservative $1\%$ extrapolated recovery
for the tested prompt-dominated observables; the matched tail calculation also
tests conformal coupling.

Uniform SdS modifies the strong-field region at every finite $L$.  We therefore
construct a static, spherically symmetric conserved cosmological tensor that
vanishes there and reaches the same positive-$\Lambda$ form in a smooth outer
layer.  This exterior-supported family improves the tested ringing waveform
at fixed $L$ but produces additional long-time scattering.  The objective of
the exterior-supported calculation is to demonstrate the existence and
potential advantage of a consistent localized regulator, not to optimize its
profile or determine the smallest usable cosmological length.  Profile and
cost optimization and the complete gravitational spectrum belong to later
work.

The paper addresses the controlled-background limit, scalar-wave recovery,
and the exterior-supported proof of principle at fixed-background level.  It
does not test the regularity or conditioning of the conformal Einstein
equations.  A nonlinear Einstein evolution with a covariant conserved
regulator is the decisive next test.

Section~\ref{sec:bridges} establishes the controlled flat limit and selects
the benchmark foliation.  Section~\ref{sec:scalar} defines the scalar model
and comparison protocol.  Section~\ref{sec:results} reports Schwarzschild
recovery with uniform artificial cosmology, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:exterior-supported} gives the localized construction, ringdown
comparison, and long-time limitation.  The appendices collect numerical
audits and implementation details.

\subsection{Schwarzschild--de Sitter static patch}

The SdS metric in static coordinates is
\begin{align}
 ds^2&=-f(r)dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2d\omega^2,
 \label{eq:sds_metric}\\
 f(r)&=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{r^2}{L^2},
 \qquad d\omega^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2.
 \label{eq:f}
\end{align}
We use geometrized units $G_{\rm N}=c=1$.  The simulations set $M=1$;
coordinates are quoted in units of $M$, and comparison measures are
dimensionless.
For $0<M<L/(3\sqrt{3})$, $f$ has a black-hole root $r_b$, a
cosmological root $r_c$, and a negative root $r_u=-(r_b+r_c)$.  They obey
\begin{align}
 L^2&=r_b^2+r_br_c+r_c^2,
 &2ML^2&=r_br_c(r_b+r_c),
 \label{eq:root_relations}\\
 f(r)&=\frac{(r-r_b)(r_c-r)(r-r_u)}{L^2r}.
 \label{eq:f_factorized}
\end{align}
For $i=b,c$, the positive surface gravities follow from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:kappa}.  The partial-fraction formulas use the positive
algebraic coefficient $\kappa_u:=|f'(r_u)|/2$ at the negative root:
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_i=\frac12\left|f'(r_i)\right|
 =\frac12\left|\frac{1}{r_i}-\frac{3r_i}{L^2}\right|,
 \qquad i\in\{b,c,u\}.
 \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
With an arbitrary reference radius $r_0\in(r_b,r_c)$, the normalized
tortoise coordinate is
\begin{align}
r_{*,L}(r)={}&\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}
 \ln\frac{r-r_b}{r_0-r_b}
-\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}
 \ln\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_0}
\notag\\
&+\frac{1}{2\kappa_u}
 \ln\frac{r-r_u}{r_0-r_u}.
\label{eq:tortoise}
\end{align}
The tortoise coordinate tends to $-\infty$ at $r_b$ and to $+\infty$ at
$r_c$.  In the large-$L$ limit,
\begin{equation}
 r_b\to2M,\qquad r_c=L-M+\mathcal{O}(M^2/L),
 \label{eq:horizon_flat_limit}
\end{equation}
so the static patch expands to the Schwarzschild exterior.  A precise
geometric identification of this limit is given in
Ref.~\cite{ZhouPanossoMacedo:2025}.

\subsection{Global bridge criterion}

To determine whether a horizon-regular foliation also has a controlled
Schwarzschild limit, introduce a stationary height function,
\begin{equation}
 \tau=t+h(r),\qquad B(r):=f(r)h'(r),
 \label{eq:height}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the boost relative to the tortoise coordinate $r_*$.  For a radial coordinate
$\rho=g(r)$ and $G=d\rho/dr$, the metric becomes
\begin{equation}
 ds^2=-f\,d\tau^2+\frac{2B}{G}\,d\tau d\rho
 +\frac{1-B^2}{fG^2}\,d\rho^2+r^2d\omega^2.
 \label{eq:transformed_metric}
\end{equation}
A future-directed spacelike bridge within the static patch satisfies
\begin{equation}
 |B|<1,\qquad B(r_b)=+1,\qquad B(r_c)=-1,
\label{eq:boost_conditions}
\end{equation}
where $(1-B^2)/f$ must tend to a finite positive nonzero value at each
horizon for a radial map with finite nonzero endpoint values of $G$.
Equivalently, $h\sim+r_*$ at $\hb$ and $h\sim-r_*$ at $\hc$.  These conditions
make the leaves null-transverse and give the transformed coefficients regular
horizon limits \cite{zenginoglu_bridge_2025}.  They are local conditions,
however, and do not determine the foliation beyond $\hc$.  On the future
branch of the expanding region, where $f<0$, spacelike continuation requires
$B<-1$.  How $B$ continues from its horizon value $B(r_c)=-1$ determines how
the leaves approach the spacelike future boundary.  This region also supports
forward and scattering problems \cite{Schlue:2012pq,Bernhardt:2024hfs}.

Two height functions demonstrate this remaining freedom.  Both are normalized
by $h(r_0)=0$ and have the same horizon data:
\begin{align}
h_{\rm min}(r)={}&
\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}\ln\left|\frac{r-r_b}{r_0-r_b}\right|
+\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}\ln\left|\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_0}\right|,
\label{eq:minimum_height}\\
h_{\rm mg}(r)={}&
\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}
\ln\left|\frac{(r-r_b)r_0}{(r_0-r_b)r}\right|
+\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}
\ln\left|\frac{(r_c-r)r_0}{(r_c-r_0)r}\right|
\notag\\
&-\frac{1}{2\kappa_u}
\ln\left|\frac{(r-r_u)r_0}{(r_0-r_u)r}\right|.
\label{eq:minimal_height}
\end{align}
Local horizon regularity supplies only the two logarithms in $h_{\rm min}$.
The minimal-gauge construction arose in hyperboloidal QNM expansions
\cite{AnsorgMacedo:2016,MacedoJaramilloAnsorg:2018} and named in
Ref.~\cite{Macedo:2020}.  Its derivation for static spherical metrics uses the
in--out and out--in strategies of Ref.~\cite{PanossoMacedo:2023qzp}.  For SdS,
the out--in construction adds the regular third-root contribution and yields a
controlled Schwarzschild limit \cite{ZhouPanossoMacedo:2025}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:minimal_height} gives that construction in our sign and
normalization conventions.

Both families satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:boost_conditions}, but their global limits
differ.  Figure~\ref{fig:bridge_foliations} follows the leaves beyond $\hc$.
As $L\to\infty$, the minimum-height leaves close toward a Cauchy-like
asymptotic end, whereas the minimal-gauge leaves open toward Schwarzschild
$\scri^+$.  The conformal diagrams thus show why regular horizon crossing
alone does not determine a useful flat limit.

Horizon regularity fixes the local crossing behavior but does not select the
asymptotic Schwarzschild end.

Matched waveforms also require a common retarded-time origin.  For a bridge
normalized by $h(r_0)=0$ and $r_{*,L}(r_0)=0$, the offset between $\tau$ and
the outgoing null coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 q_B(L;r_0):=\lim_{r\to r_c}(h+r_{*,L})
 =\int_{r_0}^{r_c}\frac{1+B}{f}\,dr.
 \label{eq:general_q}
\end{equation}
The integrand is regular at $r_c$.  If $q_B$ diverges, the finite-$L$ waveform
clock has no finite Schwarzschild limit.  A finite $q_B$ supplies the required
common time origin.

For the minimal-gauge Schwarzschild limit and the normalization used below,
\begin{equation}
 q_0=4M\ln\frac{r_0}{r_0-2M},
 \label{eq:q_zero}
\end{equation}
which gives $q_0=4M\ln2$ at $r_0=4M$.

\subsection{Compactification, clock, and coefficient behavior}

In addition to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:minimum_height} and
\eqref{eq:minimal_height}, we consider four boosts specified directly:
\begin{align}
B_{\rm lin}&=-1+2\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_b},
\label{eq:linear_boost}\\
B_{\rm fl}&=-1+2\frac{r_c-r}{r_c-r_b}\frac{r_b^2}{r^2},
\label{eq:flat_linear_boost}\\
B_{\rm Mav}&=-\frac{1-3M/r}{\sqrt{1-9M^2\Lambda}}
 \sqrt{1+\frac{6M}{r}},
\label{eq:mav_boost}\\
B_{\rm sr}&=-\gamma r+\frac{\beta}{r^2},
\label{eq:slow_roll_boost}\\
\gamma&=\frac{r_c^2+r_b^2}{r_c^3-r_b^3},\qquad
\beta=\frac{r_c^2r_b^2(r_c+r_b)}{r_c^3-r_b^3}.
\label{eq:slow_roll_parameters}
\end{align}
The Mavrogiannis boost is motivated by wave estimates on SdS and turns at the
photon sphere $r=3M$
\cite{mavrogiannis2024quasilinear,mavrogiannis2023morawetz}.

At fixed areal radius, with $x=M/r$, the six boosts have the limits
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{lcc}
\text{bridge}&B_0(r)&B_0(\infty)\\
\hline
\text{minimum height}&3x-\tfrac12&-\tfrac12\\
\text{minimal gauge}&-1+8x^2&-1\\
\text{linear}&+1&+1\\
\text{modified linear}&-1+8x^2&-1\\
\text{Mavrogiannis}&-(1-3x)\sqrt{1+6x}&-1\\
\text{slow roll}&4x^2&0
\end{array}
\label{eq:boost_limits}
\end{equation}
An outgoing Schwarzschild hyperboloidal slice must satisfy $B_0\to-1$ as
$r\to\infty$.  With compactification~\eqref{eq:compactification}, regularity
at null infinity further requires $1+B_0=\mathcal{O}(M^2/r^2)$ with a finite
positive leading coefficient.  These conditions exclude the minimum-height,
linear, and slow-roll families.  The minimal, modified-linear, and
Mavrogiannis families satisfy the pointwise criterion.  Pointwise convergence
is still insufficient, however: the scalar equations require compactified
coefficients that converge uniformly through the expanding region.  An outer
layer in the modified-linear family violates this stronger condition near
$\rho=1$.

We place both horizons at fixed coordinate locations using
\begin{align}
 \rho&=\frac{1-r_b/r}{1-r_b/r_c},
 &r(\rho)&=\frac{r_b}{1-(1-r_b/r_c)\rho},
 \label{eq:compactification}\\
 G(r)&=\frac{d\rho}{dr}
 =\frac{r_b}{(1-r_b/r_c)r^2}.
 \label{eq:G}
\end{align}
The black-hole and cosmological horizons are at $\rho=0$ and $1$,
respectively.  At fixed $r$,
\begin{equation}
 \rho\to\rho_0:=1-\frac{2M}{r},
 \label{eq:rho_limit}
\end{equation}
which is also the compactification used by the separate Schwarzschild
implementation.

The corresponding dimensionless spacetime conformal factor may be chosen as
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_L:=\frac{r_b}{r}
 =1-\left(1-\frac{r_b}{r_c}\right)\rho .
 \label{eq:conformal_factor}
\end{equation}
At every finite $L$ the outer endpoint is the cosmological horizon and
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_L(1)=\frac{r_b}{r_c}>0.
 \label{eq:finite_L_conformal_factor}
\end{equation}
In the Schwarzschild limit,
$\Omega_L\to\Omega_0=1-\rho$ and $\Omega_0(1)=0$, so the same endpoint becomes
$\scri^+$.  Misner's regulator relies on the contrast between this vanishing
limit and the finite-$L$ value in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite_L_conformal_factor}.  To expose the relevant scale,
suppose that $g$ satisfies $G_{ab}[g]+\Lambda g_{ab}=0$ and define
$\bar g=\Omega_L^2g$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 G_{ab}[\bar g]=\mathcal T_{ab}[\Omega_L]
 -\frac{\Lambda}{\Omega_L^2}\bar g_{ab},
 \label{eq:conformal_einstein_scaling}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T_{ab}$ collects derivatives of $\Omega_L$
\cite{Zenginoglu:2008pw}.  At the cosmological horizon,
$\Lambda/\Omega_L^2\to3/(4M^2)$ as $L/M\to\infty$.  A nonzero $\Omega_L$,
however, neither proves regularity nor guarantees good conditioning.  The
scalar equations contain no tensorial conformal Einstein source terms, gauge
couplings, or gravitational constraints and therefore cannot test those
properties.  Minimal scalar coupling nevertheless retains a lower-order
endpoint term with the same $\Lambda/\Omega_L^2$ scaling, whereas conformal
coupling cancels it.  The two cases provide, respectively, an algebraic stress
test and a conformally regular radiative analogue.  They test whether matched
scalar observables recover their Schwarzschild counterparts in a controlled
large-$L$ limit, not whether the Einstein system does so.

For the coefficient diagnostics, define
\begin{align}
 p&:=\frac{d\rho}{dr_*}=fG,
 &A&:=\frac{p}{1-B^2},
 \label{eq:coefficients}\\
 c_-&=-A(1+B)=-\frac{p}{1-B},
 &c_+&=A(1-B)=\frac{p}{1+B}.
 \label{eq:characteristic_speeds}
\end{align}
Figure~\ref{fig:foliation_limit} tests the six bridge families under the fixed
compactification~\eqref{eq:compactification}.  The minimum-height, linear,
and slow-roll clock offsets diverge as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 q_{\rm min}&=\tfrac12L\ln2+\mathcal O\!\left[M\ln(L/M)\right],\\
 q_{\rm lin}&=2L\ln2+\mathcal O\!\left[M\ln(L/M)\right],\\
 q_{\rm sr}&=L\ln2+\mathcal O\!\left[M\ln(L/M)\right].
\end{split}
\label{eq:offset_asymptotics}
\end{equation}
Across $20\leq L/M\leq160$, the minimal-gauge offset moves from
$2.4619M$ to $2.7243M$ toward $q_0=2.7726M$.  The modified-linear family
approaches the same value, whereas the finite Mavrogiannis offset approaches a
different Schwarzschild hyperboloidal clock.

 \caption{Large-$L$ bridge diagnostics.  (a) Fixed-$r$ boost limits;
 (b) outer-boundary clock offsets; (c) maximum characteristic speeds; and
 (d) minimum $A$ on the compact interval.  The minimal gauge has the required

The coefficient panels rule out the remaining locally regular alternatives.
For the linear boost at $L/M=160$,
$M\max|c_\pm|=19.86$ and $M\min A=3.88\times10^{-5}$, with the maximum speed
growing linearly in $L/M$.  The minimum of $MA$ falls as $(M/L)^2$ for the
minimum-height and slow-roll families, and linear slices share this decay.
The minimal and Mavrogiannis gauges instead keep coefficients of order
$M^{-1}$; at $L/M=160$ their
$(M\max|c_\pm|,M\min A)$ pairs are $(0.2498,0.06324)$ and
$(0.1499,0.07495)$.  The modified-linear family has the right pointwise boost
but a nonuniform outer layer,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{L\to\infty}M A_{\rm fl}(1)=\frac14
 \ne
 \lim_{\rho\to1^-}\lim_{L\to\infty}M A_{\rm fl}(\rho)=\frac18,
\label{eq:modified_linear_layer}
\end{equation}
with $Mc_{+,\rm fl}(1)\to1/2$ rather than the interior Schwarzschild value
$1/4$.  Thus some horizon-regular families have the wrong asymptotic end,
some have divergent clocks, and others develop degenerate or nonuniform
compactified coefficients.  We use the minimal gauge because it has the
required end, finite clock, and bounded coefficients
\cite{PanossoMacedo:2023qzp,ZhouPanossoMacedo:2025}.

The nonzero value $\Omega_L(1)>0$ supplies the geometric premise of Misner's
regulator.  It does not prove regularity or conditioning of the conformal
Einstein equations.

\section{Scalar-wave benchmark and comparison protocol}
\label{sec:scalar}

\subsection{Curvature coupling and regular first-order system}

We study the massless curvature-coupled equation
\begin{equation}
 \left(\Box_g-\xi R[g]\right)\Phi=0,
 \qquad \xi\in\left\{0,\frac16\right\},
 \label{eq:curvature_coupled_wave}
\end{equation}
where $g$ denotes the physical SdS or Schwarzschild metric.  Minimal coupling
has $\xi=0$; conformal coupling in four spacetime dimensions has $\xi=1/6$.
Write
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(t,r,\theta,\varphi)=\frac{1}{r}
 \sum_{\ell m}u_{\ell m}(t,r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi).
 \label{eq:scalar_decomposition}
\end{equation}
Each mode satisfies
\begin{align}
 \left(-\partial_t^2+\partial_{r_*}^2-V_{\ell,\xi}\right)u&=0,
 \label{eq:wave_equation}\\
 V_{\ell,\xi}&=f\left[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}
 +\frac{f'(r)}{r}+\xi R[g]\right].
 \label{eq:scalar_potential}
\end{align}
For uniform SdS, $R[g]=12/L^2$ and therefore
\begin{equation}
 V_{\ell,\xi}=f\left[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}
 +\frac{2M}{r^3}+\frac{2(6\xi-1)}{L^2}\right].
 \label{eq:uniform_coupled_potential}
\end{equation}
The explicit $-2/L^2$ term occurs at $\xi=0$ and cancels at $\xi=1/6$.
In Schwarzschild, $R[g]=0$, so both couplings give the same equation and the
same reference waveform.

The conformal transformation isolates the role of curvature coupling.  Let
$\bar g=\Omega_L^2 g$ and $\phi=\Omega_L^{-1}\Phi$.  For the sourced equation,
conformal covariance gives \cite{ZenginogluKidder:2010}
\begin{equation}
 \left(\Box_{\bar g}-\frac{R[\bar g]}6\right)\phi
 +\left(\frac16-\xi\right)\frac{R[g]}{\Omega_L^2}\phi
 =\Omega_L^{-3}S.
 \label{eq:conformal_source_transform}
\end{equation}
The second term survives at $\xi=0$ but vanishes at $\xi=1/6$.  Because
$\Omega_L=r_b/r$, the evolved modal field is proportional to its unphysical
counterpart, $\phi_{\ell m}=(u_{\ell m}/r_b)Y_{\ell m}$.  The distinction
therefore lies not in whether the field is rescaled, but in the physical
curvature coupling represented by that field.  The same identity marks the
limits of the analogy with the motivating Einstein system: neither coupling
captures all of its tensorial, gauge, and constraint structure.  The uniform,
localized-source, cross-code, caustic, and ringdown-window results below use
$\xi=0$.  The matched outer-boundary tail study compares $\xi=0$ with
$\xi=1/6$ and leaves the earlier data unchanged.  Finally, ``minimal'' in
``minimal gauge'' describes the foliation and is unrelated to minimal scalar
coupling.

We suppress the mode labels below.  With $p$ and $A$ defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:coefficients}, set
\begin{equation}
 P_{\ell,\xi}:=\frac{V_{\ell,\xi}}{p}.
 \label{eq:potential_coefficient}
\end{equation}
The first-order fields are
\begin{equation}
 \psi:=\partial_\rho u,\qquad
 \pi:=\frac{1-B^2}{p}\partial_\tau u-B\psi.
 \label{eq:first_order_variables}
\end{equation}
The evolution system is
\begin{align}
 \partial_\tau u&=A(B\psi+\pi),
 \label{eq:evolve_u}\\
 \partial_\tau\psi&=\partial_\rho\!\left[A(B\psi+\pi)\right],
 \label{eq:evolve_psi}\\
 \partial_\tau\pi&=\partial_\rho\!\left[A(\psi+B\pi)\right]-P_{\ell,\xi}u.
 \label{eq:evolve_pi}
\end{align}
We monitor the reduction constraint
\begin{equation}
 C:=\psi-\partial_\rho u.
 \label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}

The long exterior-tail calculation uses a conservative characteristic
reduction and is checked independently against an algebraically equivalent
endpoint-factored formulation.  Appendix~\ref{app:latetime} gives the
reduction and comparison.

The radial characteristic speeds are given in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:characteristic_speeds}.
The endpoint speeds are $(c_+,c_-)=(0,c_-<0)$ at $\rho=0$ and
$(c_+,c_-)=(c_+>0,0)$ at $\rho=1$.  The characteristic flow determines all
endpoint values.  For a
simple crossing with $B'(r_h)\ne0$, analytic cancellation gives
\begin{equation}
 A_h=\frac{f'(r_h)G(r_h)}{-2s_hB'(r_h)},\qquad
 s_b=+1,\quad s_c=-1.
 \label{eq:A_endpoint}
\end{equation}
We cancel every height-function pole algebraically and assign
Eq.~\eqref{eq:A_endpoint} directly at each endpoint.

The evolution domain ends on $\hc$.  The analytic continuation of the height
function beyond that horizon is nevertheless essential: it selects the
asymptotic end approached by the family as $L/M\to\infty$.  The finite-$L$
evolutions test the regular crossing coefficients and extract the outer
waveform on the cosmological horizon.

For the minimal gauge, the limiting Schwarzschild coefficients are
\begin{align}
 B_0&=-1+2(1-\rho_0)^2,
 &A_0&=\frac{1}{8M(2-\rho_0)},
 \label{eq:schwarz_coefficients}\\
 P_{\rm Schw}&=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)+(1-\rho_0)}{2M}.
 \label{eq:schwarz_potential}
\end{align}
The Schwarzschild code implements these closed-form expressions separately
from the finite-$L$ geometry.
For uniform SdS at fixed interior $\rho$, both finite-$L$ potentials approach
this result.  At the moving outer endpoint, however, the limits are
\begin{align}
 \lim_{L\to\infty}P_{\ell,0}(1)
 &=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)-2}{2M},\notag\\
 \lim_{L\to\infty}P_{\ell,1/6}(1)
 &=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2M}=P_{\rm Schw}(1).
 \label{eq:coupling_endpoint_limits}
\end{align}
For $\ell=1$, the minimal-coupling endpoint limit vanishes while the
conformal-coupling limit equals the Schwarzschild value $1/M$.  This
nonuniformity is a lower-order effect and does not alter the characteristics,
but it can change the waveform tail at the outer boundary.

\subsection{Matched data, sources, clocks, and observables}
\label{sec:protocol}

The physical data and source are matched geometrically, not by assigning the
same compact-coordinate profile.

\paragraph{Fixed-window data.}
For the finite-time flat limit, the reduced field is the same smooth compact
profile in areal radius on every background:
\begin{equation}
x=\frac{r-4M}{1.5M},
\label{eq:displacement_x}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
u(0,r)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\exp\left(1-\frac{1}{1-x^2}\right),& |x|<1,\\
0,&|x|\geq1.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:displacement_data}
\end{equation}
The support is $2.5M<r<5.5M$.  We initialize
$\psi=D_\rho u$, where $D_\rho$ is the Chebyshev differentiation operator in
the finite spectral representation, and
\begin{equation}
 \pi=-B\psi,
\label{eq:killing_stationary_data}
\end{equation}
so that $\partial_\tau u=\partial_tu=0$ initially.  The data are momentarily
stationary with respect to the static Killing field and retain a nonzero
normal derivative on the bridge.  Their support in areal radius stays fixed
across $L$.

\paragraph{Tail data.}
For the late-time study,
\begin{equation}
 u=\psi=0,\qquad \partial_\tau u=G_{\rm v}(r),\qquad
 \pi=\frac{G_{\rm v}(r)}{A},
\label{eq:velocity_data}
\end{equation}
where $x_{\rm v}=(r-6M)/(3M)$ and the peak-normalized $C^\infty$ profile is
\begin{equation}
 G_{\rm v}(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\exp\left(1-\frac{1}{1-x_{\rm v}^2}\right),
   & |x_{\rm v}|<1,\\
 0,& |x_{\rm v}|\geq1.
 \end{cases}
\label{eq:velocity_profile}
\end{equation}
Its support is $3M<r<9M$.  Dividing by the background-dependent $A$ fixes
the prescribed Killing-time derivative profile across backgrounds.  The
initial momentum field changes with $A$.  Since the $\tau=0$ bridge hypersurfaces
vary with $L$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:displacement_data}--\eqref{eq:velocity_profile}
define a geometric map among their data.

\paragraph{Retarded time.}
We set $r_0=4M$ and normalize
\begin{equation}
 h_L(r_0)=0,\qquad r_{*,L}(r_0)=0.
\end{equation}
For the minimal gauge, the cosmological-horizon logarithms in
$h_L+r_{*,L}$ cancel exactly, giving
\begin{align}
q_L={}&\frac{1}{2\kappa_b}
\ln\frac{(r_c-r_b)^2r_0}{r_c(r_0-r_b)^2}
\notag\\
&+\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa_u}-\frac{1}{2\kappa_c}\right)
\ln\frac{r_c}{r_0}.
\label{eq:q_L}
\end{align}
Waveforms are compared at the geometric retarded time
\begin{equation}
 U=\tau-q_L.
\label{eq:retarded_time}
\end{equation}
No relative time translation, amplitude rescaling, time dilation, or transfer
correction is fitted.
At $\hc$, $U$ is the outgoing retarded-time coordinate.  The same
normalization reaches Schwarzschild $\scri^+$ in the limit.  At finite
radius, $U$ is a stationary clock normalized at the outer boundary.  Its
relation to the local outgoing coordinate is
\begin{equation}
 U-(t-r_{*,L})=h_L(r)+r_{*,L}(r)-q_L.
\label{eq:finite_radius_clock}
\end{equation}
Because an additive clock offset does not affect a decay rate, we quote
transition times using the geometric origin in Eq.~\eqref{eq:retarded_time}.

\paragraph{Localized source.}
\label{sec:localized_protocol}

The fixed-mode calculation isolates the radial and temporal scalar-wave
limit.  We next test the regulator on a normalized source localized in
radius, time, and angle.  Its spherical-harmonic decomposition excites a
multimode response, including propagation through caustics
\cite{DolanOttewill:2011,ZenginogluGalley:2012,CasalsNolan:2023}.  We hold the
physical source prescription fixed across SdS and Schwarzschild, so source
width is part of the physical response rather than a numerical error bar.

The angle-dependent field is represented in spherical harmonics.  Spherical
symmetry and linearity diagonalize the angular operator, so the localized
source excites many modes that propagate independently and form the caustic
response through coherent reconstruction.  For the separable source below,
the $m$ dependence factors out and one radial response is evolved for each
retained $\ell$.  The method retains the angular transforms, modal
reconstruction, time integration, and characteristic outer boundary, but no
nonspherical or nonlinear evolution is performed here.
For either coupling, the sourced family solves
\begin{equation}
 \left(\Box_g-\xi R[g]\right)\Phi=S,
 \qquad \Phi|_{\tau=0}=\partial_\tau\Phi|_{\tau=0}=0.
 \label{eq:sourced_wave_equation}
\end{equation}
We hold the physical source $S$ fixed.  Under the conformal transformation,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:conformal_source_transform} represents it as
$\Omega_L^{-3}S$.  The physical radial implementation leaves the source term
unchanged and modifies only $V_{\ell,\xi}$.  The completed localized
production sequence uses $\xi=0$.

Define $b(x)=\exp\left[1-1/(1-x^2)\right]$ for $|x|<1$ and $b=0$ otherwise.
This is the profile used in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:displacement_data} and
\eqref{eq:velocity_profile}.  Let $\gamma$ denote the angle from the emitter
direction $(\theta_s,\varphi_s)=(\pi/2,0)$.  The source factorizes as
\begin{equation}
 S=\mathcal A\,T(t)\,R(r)\,\mathcal P_\kappa(\gamma),
 \label{eq:source_product}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
 T(t)&=\frac{b\left[(t-t_s)/\sigma_t\right]}{\sigma_t\beta_0},
 &\beta_0&=\int_{-1}^{1}b(x)\,dx,
 \label{eq:source_time}\\
 R(r)&=\frac{b\left[(r-r_s)/\sigma_r\right]}{\beta_2},
 &\beta_2&=\int b\!\left[\frac{r-r_s}{\sigma_r}\right]r^2dr,
 \label{eq:source_radial}\\
 \mathcal P_\kappa(\gamma)&=\frac{\kappa\,e^{\kappa(\cos\gamma-1)}}
 {2\pi\left(1-e^{-2\kappa}\right)}.
 \label{eq:source_angular}
\end{align}
Each factor has unit integral in its own variable.  Hence
$\int\sqrt{-g}\,S\,d^4x=\mathcal A$ on every background, and changing a width
does not change the integrated emitter strength.  We set $r_s=6M$,
$\sigma_r=0.75M$, $t_s=30M$, $\sigma_t=2M$, and $\kappa=64$.  These choices
give the angular scale $\kappa^{-1/2}=0.125$ radians, radial support
$5.25M<r<6.75M$, and temporal support $28M<t<32M$.  The same parameters
define the SdS and Schwarzschild sources.

The real-spherical-harmonic expansion of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:source_angular} is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_\kappa(\gamma)
 =\sum_{\ell m}a_{\ell m}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi),
 \label{eq:source_angular_expansion}
\end{equation}
with constant angular coefficients
\begin{equation}
 a_{\ell m}=\frac{i_\ell(\kappa)}{i_0(\kappa)}
 Y_{\ell m}(\theta_s,\varphi_s),
 \label{eq:source_modal_amplitudes}
\end{equation}
with $i_\ell$ the modified spherical Bessel function.  Modes below
$10^{-13}$ of the largest amplitude are dropped, which for an equatorial
emitter removes exactly the orders forbidden by the reflection symmetries.
Because the radial and temporal factors are independent of $m$, the code
evolves one response $v_\ell$ for each distinct retained $\ell$:
\begin{equation}
 \left(-\partial_t^2+\partial_{r_*}^2-V_{\ell,\xi}\right)v_\ell
 =f r\,\mathcal A T(t)R(r).
 \label{eq:sourced_radial_equation}
\end{equation}
For each response, define $\psi_\ell=\partial_\rho v_\ell$ and the
corresponding $\pi_\ell$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:first_order_variables}.  The first
two equations of the first-order system are unchanged, while
Eq.~\eqref{eq:evolve_pi} becomes
\begin{align}
 \partial_\tau\pi_\ell
 ={}&\partial_\rho\!\left[A(\psi_\ell+B\pi_\ell)\right]-P_{\ell,\xi}v_\ell\notag\\
 &-\frac{r}{G}\,\mathcal A
 T\!\left(\tau-h_L(r)\right)R(r).
 \label{eq:sourced_pi_equation}
\end{align}
The explicit evaluation at the Killing time $t=\tau-h_L(r)$ makes the same
physical emitter act on every background.  All responses start from
$v_\ell=\psi_\ell=\pi_\ell=0$, so the source generates the entire field.
The truncation $\ell_{\max}$ is set by the refinement ladder and enters only
through the omitted angular power.

The modal coefficients are $u_{\ell m}=a_{\ell m}v_\ell$.  Because $v_\ell$
is identical for every $m$ at fixed $\ell$, we store it once per $\ell$
alongside the coefficients $a_{\ell m}$.  A directional waveform, including
a caustic direction, is therefore
\begin{equation}
 u(U,\theta,\varphi)
 =\sum_{\ell m}a_{\ell m}v_\ell(U)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi),
 \label{eq:angular_reconstruction}
\end{equation}
at a given observer.  Extraction uses $r=8M$, $r=12M$,
and the outer boundary, with the retarded time of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:retarded_time} in every case.

At the outer endpoint the completed $\xi=0$ fixed-mode observable is the
reduced field $W_L(U)\equiv W_{L,0}(U):=u_2(U,\rho=1)$ on $\hc$; its
Schwarzschild counterpart $W_{\rm Schw}(U):=u_2(U,\rho_0=1)$ is evaluated at
$\scri^+$.  Thus $W$ is the
relevant spherical-harmonic coefficient of the radiation-scaled field
$r\Phi$, not the full field $\Phi$.  For a common time interval
$\mathcal J$, the direct fixed-mode waveform error is
\begin{equation}
 E_2(L;\mathcal J)=
 \frac{\|W_L-W_{\rm Schw}\|_{L^2(\mathcal J)}}
      {\|W_{\rm Schw}\|_{L^2(\mathcal J)}},
 \label{eq:regulator_E2}
\end{equation}
where $W_{\rm Schw}$ is computed with the separate closed-form Schwarzschild
coefficient implementation.  No relative time translation is fitted.  For
the localized source, $W$ denotes the outer-boundary modal
collection $\{u_{\ell m}\}$ and the primary comparison uses the
sphere-integrated Parseval norm
\begin{equation}
 \|W\|_{\mathcal J,S^2}^2=
 \int_{\mathcal J}dU\sum_{\ell m}|u_{\ell m}(U)|^2.
 \label{eq:parseval_norm}
\end{equation}
The corresponding relative error is
\begin{equation}
 E_{2,S^2}(L;\mathcal J)=
 \frac{\|W_L-W_{\rm Schw}\|_{\mathcal J,S^2}}
      {\|W_{\rm Schw}\|_{\mathcal J,S^2}}.
 \label{eq:localized_regulator_E2}
\end{equation}
For the completed minimally coupled sequence, we model the finite-$L$
waveform as
\begin{equation}
 W_{L,0}=W_{\rm Schw}+c_{1,0}\frac{M}{L}
 +c_{2,0}\left(\frac{M}{L}\right)^2
 +\mathcal O\!\left((M/L)^3\right).
 \label{eq:regulator_expansion}
\end{equation}
The three-level combination
\begin{equation}
 W_\infty^{(L)}=
 \frac{W_L-6W_{2L}+8W_{4L}}{3},
 \label{eq:nested_extrapolant}
\end{equation}
cancels the two displayed finite-$L$ corrections.  We test this extrapolation,
rather than assume it, by comparing the nested triples based at $L/M=80$ and
$160$ with one another and with $W_{\rm Schw}$.  A conformally coupled
sequence must establish its own finite-$L$ expansion rather than inherit
these coefficients.

The fixed-mode comparison uses cumulative intervals that begin at $U=0$ and
end at $U/M=40$, $80$, and $160$, together with the disjoint intervals
$40\leq U/M\leq80$ and $80\leq U/M\leq160$.  The localized-source comparison
uses the common simulated interval $0\lesssim U/M\leq57.2$, from the first
nonnegative output time through the last time reached by every evolution.
Before evaluating a norm, we linearly interpolate all samples onto a common
$U$ grid.  The fixed-mode tables also report $E_\infty$, normalized by the
largest Schwarzschild amplitude in the same interval, together with an
amplitude ratio and a phase offset from the zero-lag analytic-signal overlap.

\subsection{Numerical implementations and error control}

Two double-precision implementations use independent spatial discretizations
and time integrators.  The fixed-mode family uses Dedalus~3
\cite{burns_dedalus_2020}, a Chebyshev-$T$ basis, and RK222.  The localized
source uses a uniform radial grid, eighth-order finite differences, and
classical RK4 with staged source evaluation.  They share the analytic
geometry, physical comparison protocol, and modal reconstruction, but not the
spatial or temporal discretization.  Appendix~\ref{app:convergence} gives a
direct sourced-waveform comparison.

Every production family has a three-level refinement ladder, with a matched
Schwarzschild reference at each level.  We distinguish discretization error
from estimator, window, and cadence sensitivities and from physical
source-width dependence.  Appendix~\ref{app:convergence} gives the complete
ladders and numerical audit; Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} records the
exterior implementation and provenance.

\section{Schwarzschild recovery with uniform artificial cosmology}

With the minimal-gauge family, the finite-$L$ waveforms converge directly
toward the separately evolved Schwarzschild reference, and the two nested
extrapolants support a conservative $1\%$ recovery for the tested
prompt-dominated observables.  Unless stated otherwise, the results in this
section use minimal coupling, $\xi=0$.

large $L/M$ must be for a finite cosmological calculation to approximate the

localized-source response.

\subsection{Supporting Schwarzschild--de Sitter physics}
\label{sec:sds_physics}

Caustic timing supplies an independent probe of the global SdS geometry.  In
the minimally coupled localized response, we measure the shift $D_1$ in the
spacing between the direct pulse and the first echo.  Its scaling is
compatible with a local correction of order $(M/L)^2$ at fixed areal radius
and a larger outer response approaching order $M/L$.  The combined
deterministic timing sensitivities exceed the specified tolerance in all four
target-bearing cases.  The fits therefore test these powers but do not
determine precise coefficients.

Appendices~\ref{app:convergence} and~\ref{app:latetime} give the complete
timing estimator, selection, fit, and sensitivity audit.

The cosmological scale also limits how long a finite-$L$, minimally coupled
waveform can track Schwarzschild.  At $r=8M$, the $\ell=1$ waveform departs
from Schwarzschild decay at $U=115.9M$ for $L/M=80$ and at $133.5M$ for
$L/M=160$.  The near constancy of these times in units of $M$ identifies a
black-hole scale.  Cosmological decay begins at $\kappa_cU=2.83$ and $2.94$,
whose near constancy instead identifies the scale $\kappa_c^{-1}$.  These
scales separate as $L$ increases.

The finite-$L$ waveforms depart before the Schwarzschild Price-law regime is
established: at the same radius, the independent reference does not settle
within $5\%$ of its $\ell=1$ power law until $U=220.3M$.  The sequence
therefore shows a Schwarzschild-like transient, departure, and cosmological
decay, rather than a power-law plateau followed by a cosmological tail.
Appendix~\ref{app:latetime} gives the rate-estimator sweep and radius
dependence.  Section~\ref{sec:matched-tail-result} reports the separate
matched large-$L$ outer-boundary test, which uses both curvature couplings
because their late SdS targets differ.

\section{Exterior-supported artificial cosmology}
\label{sec:exterior-supported}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The uniform construction alters the metric throughout the static patch,
including the strong-field region.  Even an observable dominated by that
region therefore approaches the specified Schwarzschild problem only as
$L/M$ grows.  To reduce this finite-$L$ bias while retaining Misner's
cosmological horizon, we choose the same exterior value
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda=\frac{3}{L^2},
 \label{eq:exterior_lambda}
\end{equation}
but set the associated cosmological tensor to zero throughout a domain
containing the event horizon, initial data, source, photon sphere, and other
local structures.  At fixed exterior $\Lambda$, we ask whether this
localization brings the scalar waveform closer to the Schwarzschild waveform
than uniform SdS does.  A positive result would provide a more accurate
finite-$L$ approximation, or reach a prescribed waveform tolerance at smaller
$L/M$.  Section~\ref{sec:exterior-tests} tests this question numerically.

The construction must also satisfy the global foliation criterion established
in Sec.~\ref{sec:bridges}.  Regularity at the cosmological horizon is
insufficient: the bridge, compactification, and waveform clock must approach
the same Schwarzschild problem.

The three backgrounds separate strong-field bias from outer-propagation bias.
In uniform SdS, the term $-r^2/L^2$ alters the metric even at the photon
sphere, and the scalar potential depends on $\xi$ through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform_coupled_potential}.  In the exterior-supported family,
the metric, bridge boost, Ricci scalar, and areal-radius potential are exactly
Schwarzschild throughout the initial-data and strong-field region for both
couplings.  Waveform differences can then arise only from propagation through
the outer layer, the cosmological boundary, and the associated clock.
Conformal coupling also samples the layer curvature through $R/6$.  The
matched tail calculation tests how this outer propagation depends on $\xi$.
Exact interior shielding follows from the static spherical ansatz; in a
generic nonlinear $3+1$ problem, an exterior regulator enters the constraint
equations and need not leave the near zone unchanged.

\subsection{Conserved cosmological tensor and exact metric}

A coordinate-dependent substitution $\Lambda\mapsto\lambda(r)$ in the
vacuum equations is inconsistent:
\begin{equation}
 G_{ab}+\lambda(r)g_{ab}=0,
 \label{eq:variable_lambda_inconsistent}
\end{equation}
and the contracted Bianchi identity would give
\begin{equation}
 0=\nabla^aG_{ab}=-\nabla_b\lambda,
 \label{eq:variable_lambda_bianchi}
\end{equation}
forcing $\lambda$ to be constant.  We instead couple the metric to a
conserved second-rank tensor,
\begin{equation}
 G^a{}_b+\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b=0,
 \qquad \nabla_a\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b=0,
 \label{eq:cosmological_tensor_equations}
\end{equation}
which reduces to $\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b=\Lambda\delta^a{}_b$ wherever the
cosmological term is constant.  Dymnikova introduced this radially
boost-invariant anisotropic-vacuum structure and its mass-function
interpretation \cite{Dymnikova:2000algebraic,Dymnikova:2002mass}.  Those
works provide the algebraic framework, not the exterior-supported regulator
developed here.  We reverse the usual regular-center profile: the tensor
vanishes for $r\leq R_0$ and reaches the positive constant-$\Lambda$ form for
$r\geq R_1$.

Choose $2M<R_0<R_1<r_c$ and a smooth switch $\chi_L$ that vanishes for
$r\leq R_0$, equals one for $r\geq R_1$, and has all derivatives vanish at
the transition endpoints.  The following compact step gives an explicit
construction.

The standard compact $C^\infty$ step is
\begin{align}
 \eta(x)&=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&x\leq0,\\
 e^{-1/x},&x>0,
 \end{cases},
 &
 S(x)&=\frac{\eta(x)}{\eta(x)+\eta(1-x)}.
 \label{eq:smooth_step}
\end{align}
The switch is
\begin{equation}
 \chi_L(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&r\leq R_0,\\[1mm]
 S\!\left(\zeta_L(r)\right),&R_0<r<R_1,\\[1mm]
 1,&r\geq R_1,
 \end{cases},
 \label{eq:exterior_switch}
\end{equation}
where $\zeta_L$ is a smooth monotone map with
$\zeta_L(R_0)=0$ and $\zeta_L(R_1)=1$.
For the numerical sequence, the transition and exact-SdS cap retain a fixed
minimum width in Chebyshev angle, preventing either region from collapsing
onto the endpoint as $L$ grows.  Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} gives the
complete production prescription.  The radial cosmological profile is
$\lambda(r)=\Lambda\chi_L(r)$.

The metric that preserves the exterior Kottler mass parameter $M$ is
\begin{equation}
 ds^2=-f_\chi(r)dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f_\chi(r)}+r^2d\omega^2,
 \label{eq:exterior_metric}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 f_\chi(r)&=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{r^2}{3}\lambda(r)\\
 &=1-\frac{2M}{r}-\frac{r^2}{L^2}\chi_L(r).
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:exterior_f}
\end{equation}
It has the exact regional form
\begin{equation}
 f_\chi(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 1-\dfrac{2M}{r},&r\leq R_0,\\[2mm]
 1-\dfrac{2M}{r}-\dfrac{r^2}{L^2}\chi_L(r),
    &R_0<r<R_1,\\[2mm]
 1-\dfrac{2M}{r}-\dfrac{r^2}{L^2},&r\geq R_1.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:exterior_f_regions}
\end{equation}
The construction is defined for $M>0$.  Its strong-field region is exactly
Schwarzschild and its exterior is exactly SdS.  The transition is
not vacuum in the ordinary Einstein equations.  It is an exact solution with
a conserved anisotropic effective source, or equivalently a solution of the
modified system \eqref{eq:cosmological_tensor_equations}.

Write
\begin{equation}
 f_\chi=1-\frac{2m_\chi}{r},
 \qquad
 m_\chi(r)=M+\frac{r^3\lambda(r)}{6}.
 \label{eq:exterior_mass_function}
\end{equation}
For a metric in this form,
\begin{equation}
 G^t{}_t=G^r{}_r=-\frac{2m_\chi'}{r^2},
 \qquad
 G^\theta{}_\theta=G^\varphi{}_\varphi=-\frac{m_\chi''}{r}.
 \label{eq:mass_function_einstein}
\end{equation}
Define source eigenvalues that do not conflict with the evolution coefficient
$A$ or bridge boost $B$:
\begin{align}
 \Lambda_{\parallel}(r)&=\lambda+\frac{r}{3}\lambda',
 \label{eq:lambda_parallel}\\
 \Lambda_{\perp}(r)&=\lambda+r\lambda'
 +\frac{r^2}{6}\lambda''.
 \label{eq:lambda_perp}
\end{align}
The exact cosmological tensor is then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b
 =\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
 \Lambda_{\parallel},\Lambda_{\parallel},
 \Lambda_{\perp},\Lambda_{\perp}\right)}.
 \label{eq:exterior_cosmological_tensor}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, their nonzero components read
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 G^t{}_t=G^r{}_r
 &=-\left(\lambda+\frac{r}{3}\lambda'\right),\\
 G^\theta{}_\theta=G^\varphi{}_\varphi
 &=-\left(\lambda+r\lambda'+\frac{r^2}{6}\lambda''\right).
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:exterior_modified_equations}
\end{equation}
The only nontrivial component of the conservation law reduces to
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_a\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_r
 =\Lambda_{\parallel}'
 +\frac{2}{r}\left(\Lambda_{\parallel}-\Lambda_{\perp}\right)=0,
 \label{eq:exterior_conservation_check}
\end{equation}
which follows directly from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:lambda_parallel} and
\eqref{eq:lambda_perp}; the other components vanish by stationarity and
spherical symmetry.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b=0\quad(r\leq R_0),
 \qquad
 \mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b=\Lambda\delta^a{}_b\quad(r\geq R_1).
 \label{eq:exterior_tensor_regions}
\end{equation}
The derivative terms in the layer are required by conservation and cannot be
discarded.

Placed on the matter side, this tensor gives
\begin{equation}
 T^a{}_b=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b
 =\operatorname{diag}(-\varepsilon,p_r,p_\perp,p_\perp),
 \label{eq:exterior_effective_stress}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon$ denotes the effective density, avoiding confusion with
the compact coordinate $\rho$,
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon=\frac{\Lambda_{\parallel}}{8\pi},
 \qquad p_r=-\varepsilon,
 \qquad p_\perp=-\frac{\Lambda_{\perp}}{8\pi}.
 \label{eq:exterior_pressures}
\end{equation}
The radial null energy condition is saturated, whereas the tangential one is
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon+p_r=0,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon+p_\perp=-\frac{r}{16\pi}\Lambda_{\parallel}'.
 \label{eq:exterior_nec}
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
 \int_{R_0}^{R_1}\Lambda_{\parallel}'\,dr
 =\Lambda_{\parallel}(R_1)-\Lambda_{\parallel}(R_0)=\Lambda>0,
 \label{eq:exterior_nec_integral}
\end{equation}
$\Lambda_{\parallel}'>0$ somewhere, and the tangential null energy condition
is violated there.  The layer is an artificial regulator medium, not ordinary
matter.  This fact does not by itself preclude numerical use, but it is
central to stability and to any covariant nonlinear completion.

The curvature scalar is
\begin{equation}
 R_\chi=4\lambda+\frac{8}{3}r\lambda'
 +\frac{1}{3}r^2\lambda'',
 \label{eq:exterior_ricci_scalar}
\end{equation}
and equals zero in the interior and $4\Lambda$ in the exterior.  All
curvature quantities are smooth across the layer and throughout the
black-hole exterior; the usual Schwarzschild singularity at $r=0$ remains
when $M>0$.  Further curvature diagnostics are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}.
The Misner--Sharp mass accumulated across the transition is
\begin{align}
 \Delta m_{\rm tr}=m_\chi(R_1)-m_\chi(R_0)
 &=\frac{\Lambda R_1^3}{6}=\frac{R_1^3}{2L^2},
 \label{eq:transition_mass_dimensional}\\
 \boxed{\frac{\Delta m_{\rm tr}}{M}}
 &=\frac12\frac{(R_1/M)^3}{(L/M)^2}.
 \label{eq:transition_mass}
\end{align}
Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} gives its large-$L$ scaling.
The tested sequence is still preasymptotic: at $L/M=640$ this quantity is
$104.2917$, which is not perturbatively small.  Its large value is compatible
with small local curvature because the source is diffuse and distant, but the
fixed-background scalar experiment does not show that such a layer can act as
a weak source in nonlinear Einstein evolution.  We record it as a global
diagnostic.  Numerical suitability for the scalar benchmark is tested through
the curvature, scalar potential, compactified coefficients, returned
radiation, and cost.

A narrow transition can produce large derivatives and scattering even when
$\Lambda$ is small; the production profile therefore uses a broad,
spectrally resolved layer.

The bounds $0\leq\chi_L\leq1$ imply
\begin{equation}
 f_{\rm SdS}(r)\leq f_\chi(r)\leq f_{\rm Schw}(r).
 \label{eq:exterior_metric_bounds}
\end{equation}
For subextremal SdS, $f_{\rm SdS}>0$ between its black-hole and
cosmological roots.  On $(2M,R_0]$, the new metric is exactly Schwarzschild
and $f_\chi>0$.  For $r\geq R_0>6M$, one is outside the SdS black-hole root,
so $f_\chi\geq f_{\rm SdS}>0$ up to $r_c$.  Hence the intended choice
$R_0>6M$ and $R_1<r_c$ guarantees
\begin{equation}
 f_\chi(r)>0\qquad(2M<r<r_c),
 \label{eq:exterior_no_extra_horizon}
\end{equation}
and excludes a transition-region horizon.  Root finding remains a useful
implementation check for other profiles.  The exact near-horizon geometry gives
\begin{equation}
 r_{b,\chi}=2M,
 \qquad \kappa_{b,\chi}=\frac{1}{4M},
 \label{eq:exterior_black_hole_horizon}
\end{equation}
while $r_c$ is the usual exterior root
\begin{equation}
 1-\frac{2M}{r_c}-\frac{r_c^2}{L^2}=0.
 \label{eq:exterior_cosmological_horizon}
\end{equation}
If $R_0>6M$, the inner photon sphere, innermost stable circular orbit, and
black-hole surface gravity take their exact Schwarzschild values,
\begin{equation}
 r_{\rm ph}=3M,
 \qquad r_{\rm ISCO}=6M,
 \qquad \kappa_{b,\chi}=\frac{1}{4M}.
 \label{eq:exterior_local_structures}
\end{equation}
The transition can nevertheless create additional light rings or circular
orbit structure.  This local agreement is necessary but not sufficient for
global QNM agreement.  It suggests that the photon-sphere branch and leading eikonal behavior may be
close \cite{CardosoEtAl:2009geodesic}, but finite-$\ell$ QNMs solve a global
boundary-value problem involving the full exterior potential and both
horizons.

\subsection{Flat-limit-adapted bridge and compactification}

For the $M>0$ black-hole case, the production bridge adapted to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_f} is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{B_\chi(r)=-1+\frac{8M^2}{r^2}
 -\frac{8M^2}{r_c^2}\chi_L(r)}.
 \label{eq:exterior_bridge}
\end{equation}
It obeys
\begin{equation}
 B_\chi(2M)=+1,
 \qquad B_\chi(r_c)=-1.
 \label{eq:exterior_bridge_endpoints}
\end{equation}
Because $0\leq\chi_L\leq1$, one has $-1<B_\chi<1$ for
$2M<r<r_c$; since $\chi_L=1$ outside the layer, $B_\chi<-1$ for
$r>r_c$.  The height follows from
\begin{equation}
 h_\chi'(r)=\frac{B_\chi(r)}{f_\chi(r)}.
 \label{eq:exterior_height}
\end{equation}
This choice is convenient, but neither unique nor numerically optimized.
The crossings are regular; Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} gives their
analytic limits.
For the production family, Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} shows that both
compact endpoints become fixed above $L_\star/M=160$.  The limiting switch
may then remain nonzero at fixed radius, but its bridge contribution vanishes:
because $r_c=\mathcal O(L)$, the last term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_bridge} is $\mathcal O(M^2/L^2)$ at fixed $r$.
Therefore
\begin{equation}
 B_\chi(r)=-1+\frac{8M^2}{r^2}
 \qquad(L\to\infty),
 \label{eq:exterior_bridge_flat_limit}
\end{equation}
which is the minimal-gauge Schwarzschild boost in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:schwarz_coefficients}.  Likewise,
$r^2\chi_L/L^2\to0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_f}.  Thus the metric and bridge
recover Schwarzschild on every fixed physical-radius interval even though
$\chi_L$ itself may have a nonzero limiting profile.

Define
\begin{equation}
 D_L=1-\frac{2M}{r_c},
 \qquad
 \rho_\chi(r)=\frac{1-2M/r}{D_L}.
 \label{eq:exterior_compactification}
\end{equation}
The compactification in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_compactification} places $2M$
at $\rho_\chi=0$ and $r_c$ at $\rho_\chi=1$ and tends to
$1-2M/r$ as $L\to\infty$.  Its conformal factor may be chosen as
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_{\chi,L}=\frac{2M}{r},
 \qquad \Omega_{\chi,L}(1)=\frac{2M}{r_c}>0,
 \label{eq:exterior_conformal_factor}
\end{equation}
with $\Omega_{\chi,L}(1)\to0$ in the Schwarzschild limit.  Smaller $L$ increases
this nonzero endpoint value and may strengthen the regulator effect.  Whether
it improves the regularity or conditioning of conformal Einstein evolution
remains an open numerical question.

Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} gives the coefficient and endpoint
expansions.
The principal endpoint coefficients therefore recover their Schwarzschild
values.  Conformal coupling also recovers $P_{\rm Schw}(1)$, whereas minimal
coupling retains the lower-order endpoint offset already found for uniform
SdS.  Moving the transition outward cannot remove this nonuniformity because
every member retains an exact-SdS cap.  This endpoint structure provides an
analytic explanation for the tail sensitivity tested below.  In summary, the
background geometry, principal endpoint coefficients, and waveform clock
approach their Schwarzschild counterparts, but not every lower-order
compactified coefficient converges uniformly.

With the same normalization $r_0=4M$, define
\begin{equation}
 q_\chi=\int_{r_0}^{r_c}\frac{1+B_\chi}{f_\chi}\,dr,
 \qquad U=\tau-q_\chi.
 \label{eq:exterior_clock}
\end{equation}
The endpoint is regular, and Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} gives the
clock expansion.
Comparison with the integrable Schwarzschild tail then gives
\begin{equation}
 q_\chi=q_0-\frac{8M^2}{r_c}
 +o\!\left(\frac{M^2}{r_c}\right)
 \longrightarrow q_0=4M\ln\frac{r_0}{r_0-2M}.
 \label{eq:exterior_clock_flat_limit}
\end{equation}
Thus the fixed-angle-width exterior-supported family shares the common
Schwarzschild clock used above.

\subsection{Ringdown-window comparison}
\label{sec:exterior-tests}

We test whether moving the cosmological modification out of the strong-field
region reduces the minimally coupled scalar-wave error at fixed exterior
$\Lambda$.  Exact Schwarzschild geometry inside $R_0$ does not guarantee this
outcome: waves still cross the transition and satisfy an outgoing condition at
$r_c$.  We therefore keep the physical datum, waveform clock, Schwarzschild
reference, and uniform SdS controls fixed.

The calculation uses $\xi=0$, the compact pure-$\ell=2$ datum, and
$L/M=80,160,320,640$.  We compare all members over a common interval and
evolve the two largest lengths to $\tau=100M$.  The potential is exactly
Schwarzschild inside $R_0$ and exactly SdS outside $R_1$.  Appendix
\ref{app:reproducibility} records the resolution ladders, timesteps,
transition diagnostics, and numerical preflight.
All transition-resolution and stability guards pass.

Against the same separately evolved Schwarzschild waveform $W_{\rm Schw}$, define
\begin{equation}
 E_X(L;\mathcal J)
 =\frac{\|W_X-W_{\rm Schw}\|_{\mathcal J}}{\|W_{\rm Schw}\|_{\mathcal J}},
 \qquad X\in\{\mathrm{SdS},\chi\}.
 \label{eq:exterior_waveform_error}
\end{equation}
We again compare raw outer-boundary waveforms without fitted corrections.  The
focused post-production comparison defines the ringdown window as
$15\leq U/M\leq45$.  Equal-duration windows
$10$--$40M$ and $20$--$50M$ test sensitivity to this choice; the overlapping
windows are correlated diagnostics, not independent measurements.  The
prompt-inclusive $0$--$80M$ norm remains a secondary audit.  These $100M$
evolutions do not address the long-time tail or a possible late return from
the layer.

For each family, we estimate discretization sensitivity from grid-to-grid
changes in the residual $W_X-W_{\rm Schw}$, normalized by
$\|W_{\rm Schw}\|_{\mathcal J}$.  When these changes decrease, $\eta_X$ is the
larger of the medium-to-fine change and the estimated remaining fine-grid
scale.  For a nonmonotone ladder, $\eta_X$ is the larger successive change and
has no Richardson interpretation.  We call an observed-grid ordering
margin-separated when
$E_\chi+\eta_\chi<E_{\rm SdS}-\eta_{\rm SdS}$.  Because both families use the
same frozen Schwarzschild reference, its numerical floor enters absolute
accuracy tests but is not counted twice in this ordering test.  Even the more
pessimistic test---adding that floor to the exterior upper bound and
subtracting it from the uniform lower bound---preserves all four nominal-window
orderings.  Neither criterion establishes continuum convergence.

Figure~\ref{fig:exterior-qnm-residuals} compares the signed residuals.  Moving
the cosmological term outward reduces the residual norm across each ringdown
window, without a fitted time shift or amplitude rescaling.

improves the prompt ringing waveform, but the tested tail remains sensitive to
the transition, exact-SdS cap, and cosmological boundary.  These data do not
separate the three contributions.

The positive claim is deliberately limited to fixed-background scalar waves.
At fixed exterior $\Lambda$, exterior support reduces the ringdown-window
error by more than the conservative observed-grid margins.  The transition
mass is not perturbatively small, however, and exact near-zone shielding is a
property of this static spherical background, not of nonlinear constraint
data.

The motivating Einstein problem lies outside this scope.  A three-dimensional
extension must specify both the support and the conservation and dynamics of
the added regulator.  Gravitational perturbations satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \delta\!\left(\nabla_a\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b\right)=0.
 \label{eq:perturbed_conservation}
\end{equation}
so the metric cannot generally be perturbed while
$\mathcal{\Lambda}^a{}_b$ is held fixed.  Background, scalar-test-field, and
gravitational feasibility are distinct claims.  A nonlinear test must evolve
a covariant conserved regulator and assess constraints, hyperbolicity,
regularity, stability, accuracy, and cost.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Artificial cosmology is a controlled limit, not merely the addition of a
positive cosmological constant.  The metric, foliation, compactification,
data map, and waveform clock must approach the same asymptotically flat
problem.  Horizon regularity alone does not select that limit.  The minimal
gauge does: it has a finite Schwarzschild clock and bounded, nondegenerate
compactified wave coefficients.

With these structures matched, uniform SdS waveforms approach a separately
evolved Schwarzschild reference as $L/M$ grows.  The prompt-dominated
fixed-mode comparison and the localized multimode response support a
conservative $1\%$ extrapolated agreement on their stated intervals.  This is
not a uniform-in-time bound, and the caustic timing shifts remain consistency
evidence rather than precision measurements.

The exterior-supported family leaves the strong-field metric exactly
Schwarzschild and replaces its distributed finite-$L$ bias with a distant,
conserved anisotropic layer.  For the minimally coupled ringdown window, it
reduces the raw waveform error at every tested length, from a $25.7\%$
relative reduction at $L/M=80$ to $8.6\%$ at $L/M=640$.  The improvement does
not extend to the tested tail: uniform conformal SdS supports a
refinement-resolved $141.5M$ interval consistent with the Schwarzschild
$U^{-3}$ law, whereas neither exterior coupling passes the declared $40M$
persistence test.  These results provide a fixed-background proof of
principle for the tested finite-time scalar waveform, not a global
quasinormal-spectrum result, a universal tail law, or an optimized regulator
profile.

The calculations are fixed-background scalar tests.  A nonlinear extension
must supply a covariant conserved regulator, evolve its perturbations, and
test constraints, regularity, stability, accuracy, and cost.  This remains
especially relevant because generic three-dimensional metric evolution with
$\scri^+$ fixed at the outer boundary is still open.  The next step is
therefore a finite-$L$ nonlinear Einstein evolution equipped with a covariant
conserved regulator and subjected to the same controlled large-$L$
comparison.

 \frac{D_{1,\mathrm{local}}}{M}
 &=a_2\left(\frac{M}{L}\right)^2

\section{Auxiliary caustic and late-time protocols}
\label{app:latetime}

\subsection{Caustic timing definition}

At a fixed extraction radius, let $U_{\rm dir}$ be the direct-pulse arrival
reconstructed at $\gamma=0$ and $U_{\rm echo}$ the first caustic-echo arrival
at $\gamma=\pi$.  The primary estimator is the matched-template lag against
the corresponding Schwarzschild pulse.  We define
\begin{equation}
 D_1(r;L)=
 \left(U_{\rm echo}-U_{\rm dir}\right)_{\mathrm{SdS},L}
 -\left(U_{\rm echo}-U_{\rm dir}\right)_{\mathrm{Schw}},
 \label{eq:d1_definition}
\end{equation}
so a background-independent clock offset cancels.  Appendix
\ref{app:convergence} gives the selection rule and scaling models.

\subsection{Finite-radius crossover protocol}

The main text quotes the $\xi=0$, $r=8M$ transition because it is the cleanest
supporting case.  This separate crossover study treats the $\ell=1$ response
for $L/M=20$, $40$, $80$, and $160$ at $r=4M$, $8M$, $16M$, and $\hc$.
Finite-radius observations are compared with a separate Schwarzschild
run at the same areal radius, while $\hc$ is compared with Schwarzschild
$\scri^+$.  An additional $\ell=2$, $L/M=80$ calculation checks the
mode dependence.

Departure is the end of the last persistent interval, before cosmological
entry, on which the local decay rate agrees with the Schwarzschild reference.
Entry is the first persistent interval on which the rate agrees with the
expected minimally coupled SdS value.  The rate is constructed from a
centered root mean square
envelope and a cubic Savitzky--Golay filter.  We repeat the extraction for
$54$ combinations of smoothing width, root-mean-square fraction, persistence
interval, and tolerance.  A setting resolves a transition only when it finds
both endpoints.  At the aggregate level, a case is called resolved when at
least $27$ settings do so and marginal when between $1$ and $26$ do so.  If
none resolves, the case is reported by the missing endpoint, such as ``no
cosmological entry.''  Quoted times are medians over the resolving settings,
with the full sweep range as a systematic interval.

At $r=8M$, the entry medians are $\kappa_cU=2.83$ for $L/M=80$ and $2.94$
for $L/M=160$, with sweep ranges $[2.56,3.07]$ and $[2.68,3.19]$.
They agree to $4\%$ in cosmological units while the corresponding geometric
times differ by a factor of $2.05$.  The departure medians, $115.9M$ and
$133.5M$, are instead approximately fixed in black-hole units; their
dimensionless ranges are $[1.15,1.76]$ and $[0.71,0.96]$ in $\kappa_cU$.
The transition interval consequently widens from approximately $117M$ to
$343M$.  With only two clean lengths, this contrast is consistency evidence
for the two physical scales and not a fitted scaling law.

The shorter-length cases show why the aggregate classification matters.  At
$L/M=20$ no observer enters a resolved cosmological regime.  At $L/M=40$ the
$r=4M$ case has no cosmological entry, the $r=8M$ and $r=16M$ cases are
marginal with respectively $1/54$ and $18/54$ resolving settings, while the
cosmological-horizon case is resolved at the threshold $27/54$.

The Schwarzschild reference does not settle within $5\%$ of its own
$\ell=1$ Price value until $U=220.3M$ at $r=8M$ and $167.3M$ at $\scri^+$,
both later than the corresponding finite-$L$ departures.  For $\ell=2$ the
Price value is not attained at $r=4M$ within the evolved interval.  Thus the
tested finite-$L$ solutions leave the Schwarzschild waveform before a clean
Schwarzschild power-law regime is established.  This supplies physical
context for the finite-$L$ comparison interval; the production disjoint
windows in Sec.~\ref{sec:direct_recovery} are labeled diagnostic for the
separate numerical reason that their refinement change is not subdominant.

\subsection{Shared-flux exterior reduction}

The long exterior-tail runs use an algebraically equivalent conservative
characteristic reduction.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf h:=\pi+\psi,\qquad \mathsf j:=\pi-\psi,
 \label{eq:conservative_characteristics}
\end{equation}
and the shared fluxes
\begin{equation}
 F_+:=A(1+B)\mathsf h,\qquad
 F_-:=A(1-B)\mathsf j.
 \label{eq:conservative_fluxes}
\end{equation}
The evolution system becomes
\begin{align}
 \partial_\tau u&=\tfrac12(F_++F_-),\notag\\
 \partial_\tau\mathsf h&=\partial_\rho F_+-P_{\ell,\xi}u,\notag\\
 \partial_\tau\mathsf j&=-\partial_\rho F_--P_{\ell,\xi}u.
 \label{eq:conservative_characteristic_system}
\end{align}
Near the endpoints we retain the null factors as
$A(1+B)=(1-\rho)\alpha_+$ and $A(1-B)=\rho\alpha_-$, with finite
$\alpha_\pm$.  Reusing the same discrete fluxes in all three equations
preserves
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm ch}:=\tfrac12(\mathsf h-\mathsf j)-\partial_\rho u
 \label{eq:conservative_constraint}
\end{equation}
at the semidiscrete level.  No damping is added, and $C_{\rm ch}=C$ in the
continuum.  Schwarzschild and uniform SdS tail controls use
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:evolve_u}--\eqref{eq:evolve_pi}; exterior-tail production uses
Eq.~\eqref{eq:conservative_characteristic_system}.

\subsection{Exterior-tail formulation check}
\label{app:tail-formulation-check}

The standard and shared-flux reductions are algebraically equivalent at the
continuum level but differ after semidiscretization.  We therefore compare
their waveforms on the same exterior background rather than relying on the
continuum transformation alone.  A direct short regression at $L/M=80$
agrees in absolute waveform amplitude to better than $10^{-7}$.  The
independent long calculation uses
endpoint-factored fields
\begin{equation}
 H=\alpha_+(\pi+\psi),\qquad
 J=\alpha_-(\pi-\psi),
 \label{eq:factored_characteristics}
\end{equation}
so that the physical fluxes are $(1-\rho)H$ and $\rho J$.  It damps only the
continuum reduction constraint, for which
$\partial_\tau C=-M^{-1}C$, and evolves the same scalar equation, geometry,
initial data, clock, and output times as the shared-flux production system.

For $L/M=640$, $\ell=1$, and $\xi=0$, both formulations were evolved through
$U/M=1000$ at $N=1536$ and $2048$ with
$\Delta\tau/M=0.0025$.  At $N=2048$, their outer-boundary relative
$L^2$ differences range from $0.62\%$ to $2.65\%$ over six late-signal audit
windows between $U/M=100$ and $950$; the small late amplitude makes these
windowed relative differences more stringent than the prompt-dominated
full-record norm.  More importantly, the endpoint-factored and shared-flux
systems give longest $p_{\rm eff}\simeq3$ intervals of $12.15M$ and $11.90M$,
respectively.  Both independently fail the required $40M$ persistence test.
The former gives a median slow-component index $p_{\rm eff}=1.176$, while the
latter gives $p=1.086$ with $R^2=0.9926$ on its fixed $120$--$250M$ fit.
Both formulations therefore reject the Price-law interval and resolve the
same slow exterior component.  This test checks formulation dependence; it
does not supply another accuracy estimate.  The three-grid and
halved-timestep ladders set the production accuracy.

\subsection{Matched outer-boundary tail protocol}

The regulator question concerns radiation at the asymptotic end.  The
large-$L$ tail comparison therefore uses an $\ell=1$ waveform at $\hc$ for
uniform and exterior-supported SdS and compares it with the same Schwarzschild
waveform at $\scri^+$.  The completed calculation uses $L/M=640$ and extends
through $U/M=1000$ for both $\xi=0$ and $1/6$.  This test complements the
finite-radius crossover above: it asks whether a finite cosmological boundary
can reproduce even a short interval of the Schwarzschild null-infinity Price
law before its own asymptotics take over.

From a centered root-mean-square envelope $A(U)$ we form the local power index
$p_{\rm eff}=-d\ln A/d\ln U$.  A candidate Schwarzschild-like interval must
remain within $10\%$ of the null-infinity dipole value $p=3$ for at least
$40M$.  A candidate subsequent exponential regime is measured with
$\gamma_{\rm eff}=-d\ln A/dU$.  For uniform SdS, the large-$L$ reference rates are
$\gamma_{\rm eff}/\kappa_c\simeq1$ for $\xi=0$ and $2$ for $\xi=1/6$.
The uniform rate must remain within $10\%$ of its target for at least the
semilog fit width $\Delta(\kappa_cU)=0.25$.  No target is imposed on the
exterior-supported geometry; its late signal is analyzed without assigning
an asymptotic decay law.
Each claimed interval lies above the time-dependent numerical floor and
persists under an envelope- and fit-window sweep.  Plotted indices require an
envelope above ten times the measured spatial or temporal refinement floor,
agreement within $0.1$ between the $N=2048$ and $3072$ indices, and agreement
within $0.1$ between full- and half-step $N=2048$ indices; neighborhoods of
signed zero crossings are omitted.  Three spatial resolutions
and a fixed-resolution halved-timestep run test the ordering and duration of
the intervals.  The primary estimator uses a $10M$ envelope and $40M$ local
fit.  We also combine envelope widths $5M$, $10M$, and $20M$ with fit widths
$30M$, $40M$, and $60M$.  Across these nine choices, the Schwarzschild
candidate lasts $815.4$--$841.9M$ and the uniform conformal candidate lasts
$131.1$--$148.1M$; both pass.  The longest candidates in uniform minimal,
exterior minimal, and exterior conformal SdS last at most $10.2M$, $12.0M$,
and $11.3M$, respectively, so all three fail.  The classification is therefore
insensitive to the estimator widths.

On the resolved $N=3072$ exterior envelopes, unweighted least-squares fits of
$\ln A$ against $\ln U$ give $A\sim U^{-1.086}$ ($R^2=0.9926$) for $\xi=0$
on $120\leq U/M\leq250$ and $A\sim U^{-0.920}$ ($R^2=0.9911$) for $\xi=1/6$
on $160\leq U/M\leq400$.  These finite-interval exponents describe the chosen
transition profile and are not universal tail indices.

For uniform conformal SdS, the relative waveform changes on the accepted
$153.3$--$294.8M$ interval are $7.90\times10^{-5}$ from $N=1536$ to $2048$
and $7.97\times10^{-6}$ from $N=2048$ to $3072$; halving the timestep at
$N=2048$ changes the waveform by $3.23\times10^{-8}$.  For the exterior
family, the three spatial grids use $N=1536$, $2048$, and $3072$ with
$\Delta\tau/M=0.0025$, supplemented by an $N=2048$ run at half that timestep.
On the successive $300$--$500M$, $500$--$750M$, and $750$--$950M$ windows,
the $N=2048$--$3072$ relative changes are $2.41\times10^{-6}$,
$3.80\times10^{-6}$, and $2.97\times10^{-5}$ for $\xi=0$, and
$4.28\times10^{-5}$, $3.15\times10^{-5}$, and $1.34\times10^{-4}$ for
$\xi=1/6$.  The preceding spatial differences are larger by factors of about
$10$--$11$ and $7.4$--$7.6$, respectively.  Halving the timestep changes
these signals by only $3$--$4\times10^{-8}$, and the largest stored exterior
reduction-constraint norm is $1.09\times10^{-8}$.

Figure~\ref{fig:matched-tail} and Table~\ref{tab:matched-tail} give the full
comparison.  The exterior features converge under refinement, but neither
coupling resolves a Schwarzschild Price interval or a cosmological entry.
These null results are retained rather than converted into crossover times.

\section{Exterior profile, implementation details, and reproducibility}
\label{app:reproducibility}

\subsection{Production switch and profile diagnostics}
The numerical family follows the horizon-scaled prescription through
$L_\star/M=160$ and then imposes a common minimum width in Chebyshev angle on
the transition and exact-SdS cap.  This leaves the $L/M=80$ and $160$ members
unchanged and prevents either outer region from collapsing onto the endpoint.
With the compactification~\eqref{eq:exterior_compactification}, define
$\vartheta(r)=\arccos[2\rho_\chi(r)-1]$ on $2M\leq r\leq r_c$; Chebyshev
collocation points are uniformly spaced in $\vartheta$.

With $\beta=9/10$, first define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat R_1(L)&=\beta r_c,\\
 \widehat\rho_{\chi,1}(L)&=
 \frac{1-2M/\widehat R_1}{D_L},\\
 \widehat\vartheta_1(L)&=
 \arccos\!\left(2\widehat\rho_{\chi,1}-1\right).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:horizon_scaled_transition}
\end{equation}
The final endpoints are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{L_\star}{M}&=160,\\
 \vartheta_\star&=\widehat\vartheta_1(L_\star)
 =0.07526440137447271,\\
 \vartheta_1(L)&=
 \max\{\widehat\vartheta_1(L),\vartheta_\star\},\\
 \vartheta_0(L)&=2\vartheta_1(L),\\
 \rho_{\chi,i}(L)&=\frac{1+\cos\vartheta_i(L)}{2},\\
 R_i(L)&=\frac{2M}{1-D_L\rho_{\chi,i}(L)}
 \quad(i=0,1).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:transition_radii}
\end{equation}
Thus $2M<R_0<R_1<r_c$.  For $L\leq L_\star$, the floor is inactive,
$R_1=\beta r_c$, and the transition and cap have equal angle widths,
$\vartheta_0-\vartheta_1=\vartheta_1$.  For $L>L_\star$, both widths equal
$\vartheta_\star$ and both compact endpoints are fixed:
\begin{align}
 \rho_{\chi,0}^\star&=0.9943459581991452,\notag\\
 \rho_{\chi,1}^\star&=0.9985844858695327,\notag\\
 \Delta\rho_{\rm tr}
 &=\rho_{\chi,1}^\star-\rho_{\chi,0}^\star
 =0.004238527670387571,\notag\\
 \Delta\rho_{\rm cap}
 &=1-\rho_{\chi,1}^\star
 =0.001415514130467255.
 \label{eq:transition_compact_widths}
\end{align}

The production switch is Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_switch} with
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_L(r)=\frac{\vartheta_0-\vartheta(r)}
 {\vartheta_0-\vartheta_1}.
 \label{eq:production_exterior_switch}
\end{equation}
It gives equal nonvanishing widths to the transition and exact-SdS cap in
$\vartheta$.  Their compact endpoints approach the fixed values above, with
the areal limits
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \frac{R_{0,\infty}}{M}
 &=\frac{2}{1-\rho_{\chi,0}^\star}
 =353.729256069105,\\
 \frac{R_{1,\infty}}{M}
 &=\frac{2}{1-\rho_{\chi,1}^\star}
 =1412.914189235122,\\
 \frac{R_0}{r_c}&\longrightarrow0,
 &\frac{R_1}{r_c}&\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:transition_radii_limit}
\end{equation}
The exact-SdS cap still ends at the cosmological horizon, but its inner edge
does not remain at a fixed fraction of that horizon.  The transition begins
and ends at finite, distant radii in the flat limit, while the metric
correction there vanishes as $L^{-2}$.  The entire strong-field region is
exactly Schwarzschild throughout the production sequence, for which
$R_0>6M$.

A useful unexpanded diagnostic is
\begin{equation}
 R_{ab}R^{ab}
 =2\left(\Lambda_{\parallel}^2+\Lambda_{\perp}^2\right),
 \label{eq:exterior_ricci_squared}
\end{equation}
and the Kretschmann scalar is
\begin{equation}
 R_{abcd}R^{abcd}=f_\chi''{}^2
 +4\left(\frac{f_\chi'}{r}\right)^2
 +4\left(\frac{1-f_\chi}{r^2}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:exterior_kretschmann}
\end{equation}

Because $R_1$ tends to a finite areal radius in the final family,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Delta m_{\rm tr}}{M}
 \sim\frac12\left(\frac{R_{1,\infty}}{M}\right)^3
 \left(\frac{M}{L}\right)^2
 \longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:transition_mass_fixed_angle_family}
\end{equation}

For a generic layer of areal width $\Delta=R_1-R_0$,
\begin{equation}
 \lambda'=\mathcal O\!\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\Delta}\right),
 \qquad
 \lambda''=\mathcal O\!\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\Delta^2}\right).
 \label{eq:transition_derivative_scales}
\end{equation}
A narrow areal layer can generate large curvature and a sharp scattering
feature.  Here both endpoints have finite limits and
$\Delta\to1059.184933166017M$.  The limiting profile therefore has
$\chi_L'=\mathcal O(M^{-1})$ and
$\chi_L''=\mathcal O(M^{-2})$.  At fixed $M$, the strict large-$L$ scalings
of $\lambda=3\chi_L/L^2$ are
\begin{equation}
 \lambda'=\mathcal O(L^{-2}M^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \lambda''=\mathcal O(L^{-2}M^{-2}).
 \label{eq:transition_width_floored_derivative_scales}
\end{equation}
The combinations entering the source eigenvalues and Ricci curvature are
$\mathcal O(L^{-2})$.  The construction therefore replaces
distributed strong-field bias by a smooth, low-curvature modification in a
distant region.  The lapse change near the cosmological horizon remains order
unity, while the transition retains a fixed spectral width.




\subsection{Endpoint coefficients and clock asymptotics}

The coefficients used in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:evolve_u}--\eqref{eq:evolve_pi} are
obtained without numerical differentiation of the compactification:
\begin{align}
 G_\chi&=\frac{2M}{D_Lr^2},&
 A_\chi&=\frac{f_\chi G_\chi}{1-B_\chi^2},\notag\\
 P^\chi_{\ell,\xi}&=\frac{D_L}{2M}
 \left[\ell(\ell+1)+rf_\chi'+\xi r^2R_\chi\right].
 \label{eq:exterior_scalar_coefficients}
\end{align}
Their removable horizon limits are assigned analytically, as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:A_endpoint}.
In the exact-SdS cap, the horizon equation gives
$D_L=r_c^2/L^2=1-2M/r_c$.  The outer endpoint values are therefore
\begin{align}
 A_\chi(1)&=\frac{r_c-3M}{8M(r_c-2M)},&
 c_{+,\chi}(1)&=2A_\chi(1),\notag\\
 P^\chi_{\ell,\xi}(1)&=\frac{D_L}{2M}
 \left[\ell(\ell+1)-2+\frac{6M}{r_c}\right.\notag\\
 &\left.\hspace{7em}+12\xi D_L\right].
 \label{eq:exterior_outer_endpoint_coefficients}
\end{align}
Hence
\begin{align}
 A_\chi(1)&\longrightarrow\frac{1}{8M},&
 c_{+,\chi}(1)&\longrightarrow\frac{1}{4M},\notag\\
 P^\chi_{\ell,0}(1)&\longrightarrow
 \frac{\ell(\ell+1)-2}{2M},&
 P^\chi_{\ell,1/6}(1)&\longrightarrow
 \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2M}.
 \label{eq:exterior_outer_endpoint_flat_limit}
\end{align}

The exterior bridge's horizon crossings are regular; for example,
\begin{align}
 \lim_{r\to2M^+}\frac{1-B_\chi^2}{f_\chi}&=8,\notag\\
 \lim_{r\to r_c^-}\frac{1-B_\chi^2}{f_\chi}
 &=\frac{16M^2}{r_c(r_c-3M)}>0.
 \label{eq:exterior_bridge_regular_limits}
\end{align}

The clock endpoint is also regular:
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{r\to r_c}\frac{1+B_\chi}{f_\chi}
 =\frac{B_\chi'(r_c)}{f_\chi'(r_c)}
 =\frac{8M^2}{r_c(r_c-3M)}.
 \label{eq:exterior_clock_endpoint}
\end{equation}
To obtain its flat limit, choose fixed $R>R_{1,\infty}$.  For sufficiently
large $L$, the transition lies below $R$ and $[R,r_c]$ is exactly SdS.  On
$[r_0,R]$, the integrand in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_clock} approaches its
Schwarzschild value with $\mathcal O(L^{-2})$ corrections.  In the exact cap,
the cancellation-free expression is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1+B_\chi}{f_\chi}
 =\frac{8M^2(r_c+r)}{r^2r_c^2
 \left[(r_c+r)/L^2-2M/(rr_c)\right]}.
 \label{eq:exterior_clock_cap}
\end{equation}
Comparison with the integrable Schwarzschild tail gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exterior_clock_flat_limit}.

\subsection{Exterior ringdown implementation and audit}

The minimally coupled comparison uses RK222 with $3/2$ dealiasing and stores
the waveform every $0.03M$.  The coarse, medium, and fine spatial resolutions
are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 L/M=80:&\quad(384,512,768),\\
 L/M=160:&\quad(768,1024,1536),\\
 L/M=320:&\quad(1024,1536,2048),\\
 L/M=640:&\quad(768,1024,1536).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:exterior_resolution_ladders}
\end{equation}
At every $L$, the corresponding timesteps are $0.005M$, $0.00375M$, and
$0.0025M$, matching the control ladder.  The bounded zeroth-order potential
is explicit in the IMEX split, while transport is implicit.  Since each
ladder refines space and time together, its numerical scale includes both
effects; no separate fixed-resolution timestep check is used to reduce it.

The angle floor is active at $L/M=320$ and $640$, where the audited transition
radii are
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 L/M & R_0/M & R_1/M\\ \hline
 320 &168.2329417&260.5413505\\
 640 &228.1477989&440.4330107
\end{array}
 \label{eq:fixed_angle_transition_radii}
\end{equation}
At $L/M=320$, the fine grid places $49$ Chebyshev points in each of the
transition and cap; at $L/M=640$, it places $37$ in each.  The minimally
coupled $L/M=640$ potential-representation error is
$3.47\times10^{-6}$ relative to the analytic transition minimum.

For either coupling, the scalar potential is
\begin{equation}
 V_{\ell,\xi}^\chi=f_\chi\left[
 \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}+\frac{f_\chi'}{r}+\xi R_\chi\right].
 \label{eq:exterior_scalar_potential}
\end{equation}
Using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:exterior_metric} and
\eqref{eq:exterior_ricci_scalar}, the conformally coupled specialization is
\begin{equation}
 V_{\ell,1/6}^\chi=f_\chi\left[
 \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}+\frac{2M}{r^3}
 +\frac{r\lambda'}{9}+\frac{r^2\lambda''}{18}\right].
 \label{eq:exterior_conformal_scalar_potential}
\end{equation}
It equals the Schwarzschild potential inside $R_0$ and the corresponding SdS
potential outside $R_1$.  Its derivative terms require an independent
resolution audit for $\xi=1/6$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:exterior_metric_bounds} excludes unintended roots.  The
numerical preflight also checks $f_\chi>0$, $|B_\chi|<1$, finite compactified
coefficients, and adequate spectral resolution of the transition potential.
The $\xi=0$ sequence requires a nonnegative represented potential; the
conformal sequence checks finiteness and resolution without imposing a sign.
The audit verifies the active endpoint branch, separate minimum point counts
in the transition and cap, coefficient extrema, $q_\chi$, potential
resolution, $\Delta m_{\rm tr}/M$, runtime, and step count.  It rejects
nonfinite evolution and order-of-magnitude growth in stored waveforms,
snapshots, or reduction constraints.  These guards detect failure; the
three-level waveform comparisons determine accuracy.
Stored reduction-constraint maxima compare the available snapshots across a
ladder; they are not an interval-resolved constraint history.

The prompt-inclusive $0$--$80M$ audit gives reductions of $18.0\%$, $14.1\%$,
$10.5\%$, and $7.35\%$ as $L/M$ increases.  In the disjoint $40$--$80M$
window, the improvement is unresolved at $L/M=320$ and $640$; the calculation
therefore supports no late-time claim.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

The reproducibility package keeps raw simulations separate from derived
tables and figures.  Machine-readable manifests record each physical
configuration and resolution, link every reported artifact to its inputs,
verify checksums, and confirm that postprocessing leaves raw data unchanged.
The records fix the mass normalization, foliation, compactification, data or
source prescription, waveform clock, and analysis interval.  They cover the
uniform, localized-source, exterior-ringdown, matched-tail,
cross-implementation, and independent-formulation checks.

The public release includes the code, raw and derived data, analysis scripts,
and foliation and late-time diagnostics.  Compact encoding of the localized
responses introduces at most a $10^{-7}$ relative peak-reconstruction error,
which is negligible for the percent-level comparisons.  As throughout the
paper, small refinement changes establish that discretization is subdominant;
they do not turn all displayed digits into physical accuracy.
